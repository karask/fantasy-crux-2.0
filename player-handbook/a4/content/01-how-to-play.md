## Playing the Game

You play one adventurer. The Gamemaster (GM) describes the world and plays everyone else. Say what your character tries to do and how; the GM tells you what happens. When the outcome is uncertain and both success and failure would matter, the GM asks for a roll.

Do not roll for routine work, impossible feats, or actions whose failure changes nothing. A locksmith with time and tools simply opens a cheap lock; nobody climbs to the moon.

### What you need

A character sheet, this handbook, two ten-sided dice (one marked for tens), and a few other dice: D4, D6, D8 and D12.

### Dice notation

A number after **D** gives the die's sides; a number before it says how many to roll and add. **2D6+2** means roll two six-sided dice, add them, then add 2.

Distances are in metres; on a grid, one square is 2 m. Round to the nearest whole number, **halves up**, unless a rule says otherwise. Damage never goes below 0.

## Making a Test

Almost every uncertain action is a **test**: roll D100 against a skill, such as Athletics 48%.

1. **Say what you want and how you try.** The GM says what success gains and what failure risks.
2. **Set the modifiers.** Circumstances add Bonus or Penalty dice (below).
3. **Roll D100.** Roll the tens die and the units die together and read them as a number: tens 5 and units 9 read **59**. A tens 0 gives a single digit, such as 07; **00 reads as 100**.
4. **Grade the result** and apply what you agreed in step 1.

### Grade your result

Check for a Fumble first: it overrides everything else.

- **Fumble:** 99 or 100 when your skill is below 100%; only 100 at 100%. You fail with a serious consequence that fits the risk: you drop your weapon, the rope frays, the guard turns.
- **Critical:** from 01 up to your skill's **tens digit**. At 60% that is 01–06; at 59%, 01–05. Any skill above 0% criticals on at least 01. You succeed especially well: faster, more thoroughly, or with an extra benefit.
- **Success:** any other roll at or below your skill.
- **Failure:** any other roll above your skill.

Skills never exceed 100%.

> **Example.** Mara climbs a wet wall with Athletics 48%. She rolls 31: a Success. At 04 it would be a Critical (her tens digit is 4), so she reaches the top in half the time. At 99 she Fumbles and slips.

## Bonus and Penalty Dice

Circumstances that clearly help or hinder you add **Bonus dice** (+1B, +2B…) or **Penalty dice** (−1P, −2P…). They never change your skill or your critical range; they give you more results to choose from.

1. **Total and cancel.** Each +1B cancels one −1P. Keep at most **+3B or −3P**.
2. **Roll extra tens dice.** Roll one units die, one tens die, and one more tens die for each remaining Bonus or Penalty die.
3. **Pair them.** Every tens die pairs with the **same units die**, giving several candidate results.
4. **Keep one.** With Bonus dice keep the **best** result; with Penalty dice, the **worst**.

**Which result is best?** Grade every candidate first: Critical beats Success, Success beats Failure, Failure beats Fumble. Between two results of the same grade, the **higher** Critical or Success is better (it wins contests), and the **lower** Failure or Fumble is better.

{{fig:bonus}}

> **Example.** You roll units 5 and tens 2 and 6: the candidates are **25** and **65**. With a 70% skill both succeed, so a Bonus die keeps the higher 65 and a Penalty die keeps 25. With a 60% skill, 65 fails, so a Bonus die keeps 25 and a Penalty die keeps 65.

A modest advantage or obstacle is usually +1B or −1P. Most of this handbook's modifiers are listed with the rule they belong to.

## Opposed Tests

When two characters compete, or one resists another, **both roll** their own skill with their own dice. Then compare:

1. **Better grade wins:** Critical > Success > Failure > Fumble.
2. **Both succeed at the same grade:** the higher roll wins.
3. **Both fail at the same grade:** the lower roll wins.
4. **Equal rolls:** the higher unmodified skill wins; if still tied, the defender (or the way things already are) wins.

> **Example.** A thief's Deception 42 (Success) beats the guard's Perception 31 (Success): same grade, higher roll. Had the thief rolled 04, a Critical, she would win whatever the guard rolled short of a better Critical.

### Helping

Allies with a practical way to help, and normally **26% or more** in the skill, give the acting character **+1B in total**, however many help. Exceptionally effective help can give +2B.

## Your Character Sheet

These numbers appear on every sheet. Chapter Two shows how to work them out.

- **Characteristics:** seven scores, usually 3–18, describing body and mind.
- **Skills:** percentages you roll against.
- **Hit Points (HP):** written as current/maximum, such as 8/12. Injury lowers current HP.
- **Major Wound Level (MWL):** a single hit at least this large wounds you seriously. It never drops as you lose HP.
- **Power Points (PP):** current/maximum; they fuel magic and some items.
- **Movement:** metres you can move with one Movement Action; 15 m for a human.
- **Combat Order:** added to 1D10 to decide who acts first in a fight.
- **Damage Modifier (DM):** extra damage for strong, big characters (or less for small, weak ones).
- **Armour Points (AP):** damage your armour stops from each hit.
- **ENC:** how heavy and bulky your gear is.
- **Improvement Points (IP):** spent to raise skills and buy Talents.
- **Hero Points:** a few chances to change your fate.

### Hero Points

Every character starts with **2 Hero Points**; the GM awards more for heroic play and major achievements. Spend one when you declare its effect:

- **Reroll:** reroll a failed test (a Fumble counts) after seeing it but before its consequences. The new result stands. Any test can be rerolled only once, whatever the source.
- **Downgrade a Major Wound** or **Avoid Death:** see Chapter Four.
- **Story benefit:** with the GM's approval, add a plausible lucky detail or coincidence. Bigger changes can cost several points, and friends can chip in.
