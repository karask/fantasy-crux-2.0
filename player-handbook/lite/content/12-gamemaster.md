# Gamemaster

## When to roll

Roll when the outcome is uncertain and failure has a real consequence. Warn the
players before a risky roll. If success would give only something minor, give
it without a roll. Ask for one test for a long task, not one per step.

## Setting difficulty

There are no difficulty numbers. Most tests use the plain skill; let the
situation add dice. A clear advantage is +1B, a hard task -1P, and a very hard one
-2P. If it cannot fail, it succeeds; if it cannot succeed, do not roll.

## Morale

Foes want to live. When a fight turns against them, the wounded might flee or
surrender, while healthy foes rarely just run. Use Intimidate when the heroes
demand it.

## Rewards

Award **3 IP** for modest progress, **4** for a completed adventure and **5** for
a major achievement, plus a Hero Point for heroic play.

## Quick NPCs

Write a description, a level and gear: **Guard 50 (Shortsword 1D6+1, Leather
2).** The guard has 50% in what guards do best, Close Combat and Perception;
40% in related skills such as Dodge, Resilience and Ranged Combat; 30% or less
in the rest. A minor NPC has **10 HP and Combat Order 10** unless the role calls
for more.

| Level | Typical NPC                         |
| ----: | ----------------------------------- |
|    30 | Frightened villager, untrained thug |
|    40 | Bandit, militia                     |
|    50 | Guard, hunter, sellsword            |
|    60 | Sergeant, assassin, cult leader     |
|    70 | Champion, master of a craft         |

## Encounters

Roughly as many foes as player characters makes a fair fight. Raise one foe's
skill or gear rather than adding more bodies; crowds of weak foes mostly cost
time.

## Reading a creature

Any full-rules creature works as written; Creatures lists common ones. Each is
an average member of its kind. Listed damage already includes its Damage
Modifier, and unlisted skills use their normal bases. Natural weapons are Light
up to SIZ 5, Medium to 20, Heavy to 40, and Huge above; fists and kicks stay
Light. **Paralysed:** conscious, but no Actions, Reactions or voluntary
movement; it can still resist.

## Creature types

- **Living:** wounds, Bleeding, Dying and 0 PP work as for characters.
- **Incorporeal:** no body, HP, MWL, AP or DM. Mundane attacks cannot harm it.
- **Spirit:** a soul. Spirit magic and Spirit Combat reach it; at 0 PP it is
  banished from the scene.
- **Construct, Elemental, Undead:** nonliving and Soulless. No breathing,
  eating, sleep, fatigue, poison or disease; Wounded but never Bleeding or
  Dying; destroyed at 0 HP; 0 PP does not stop them.
- **Soulless:** immune to Spirit magic, Spirit Combat and possession.
- **Mindless:** immune to Mind magic. Skeletons, zombies, elementals and
  programmed constructs are Mindless; animals are not.
- **Anchored:** bound to a place, object or body, as its profile says.

## Multiattack

A listed Multiattack spends one Combat Action: the first attack normally, each
later one at -1P (not cumulative). Each attack can pick its own target and
allows its own Reaction. The creature keeps its base Reaction and still Dodges
once a round. No Charge, off-hand or other extra attacks with it.

## Spirit Combat and possession

Only a creature with Spirit Combat can start it: a Combat Action at Close
range, with no Reaction spent. The target opposes with Spirit Combat or
Persistence. The winner deals its listed PP damage, ignoring armour; a defender
using Persistence deals 1D4 PP. Soulless creatures cannot be targeted.

A spirit that brings a living target to 0 PP can possess it with a later Combat
Action while both are Close, with no roll. They share one set of actions a
round, at the host's Combat Order. Spirit Combat and Spirit magic can still
target the possessor; when it leaves, the host is unconscious until it regains
PP.
