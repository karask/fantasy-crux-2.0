# Play

## Make a test

1. Say what your character wants and how they try.
2. The GM names the **skill** and any **Bonus or Penalty dice**. Routine and
   impossible tasks need no roll.
3. Roll **D100**: a tens die and a units die read 01 to 100; `00` is 100. A
   result at or under the skill succeeds.
4. Apply the result and play on. Do not repeat the test until something changes.

## Result grades

| Roll                         | Grade                                                    |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01 to the skill's tens digit | **Critical:** success with an extra benefit.             |
| At or under the skill        | **Success.**                                             |
| Over the skill               | **Failure.**                                             |
| 99–00; only 00 at 100%       | **Fumble:** failure with a consequence fitting the risk. |

A 59% skill is Critical on 01–05; any skill above 0% is Critical on 01. A
Fumble overrides success. Skills never exceed 100%.

A **Critical** succeeds especially quickly, thoroughly or well, and can reveal
something extra. A **Fumble** adds a serious consequence that follows from the
risk: lost time, broken gear, danger or a worse position.

## Bonus and Penalty dice

Each **+1B** adds one tens die and you keep the best result; each **-1P** adds
one and you keep the worst. Pair every tens die with the same units die.
Cancel them one for one and keep at most **+3B or -3P**.

**Best result:** the higher grade first (Critical, Success, Failure, Fumble).
Among successes the higher roll is better; among failures, the lower.

> **Example.** Skill 70%, +1B: units 5, tens 2 and 6 give 25 or 65. Both
> succeed, so keep 65.

## Opposed tests

When someone resists, both roll. The higher grade wins. With the same grade,
the higher success wins, or the lower failure. Still tied: the higher skill
wins, then the defender; with no defender, nothing changes. Two successes of 42
and 31: 42 wins. Two failures of 72 and 85: 72 wins.

## Let the fiction lead

Rules settle uncertain moments; they do not replace common sense. The GM can
grant dice for a strong advantage, rule that size or anatomy makes an action
impossible, or let an obvious approach succeed without a roll. Say so before
the roll.

## Help

Helpers who know the job, with 26% or more in the skill, give **+1B in
total**, not per helper. Exceptional teamwork can give +2B.

## Group tests

If one failure sinks everyone, such as sneaking past a guard, roll once for the
weakest member. If one success is enough, such as spotting an ambush, roll once
for the best, with +1B for the others' help.

## Hero Points

Each player character starts with **2**; the GM awards more for heroic play.
Spend 1 to:

- **Reroll** a failed test, before its consequences. One reroll per test.
- **Downgrade a Major Wound:** keep the HP loss, but not Wounded or Bleeding.
- **Avoid Death:** at 0 HP, become stable and unconscious. Wake after the scene
  with 1 HP.
- **Story benefit:** with GM approval, add a plausible favourable detail.

## Example of play

Corin sneaks past a sentry at night: Deception 55% against the sentry's
Perception 45%. Moonlight is partial darkness, so the sentry rolls at -1P, and
Corin's dark cloak gives him +1B. Corin's dice read 25 or 65; he keeps 25, a
Success. The sentry's read 38 or 78; he must keep 78, a Failure. Corin slips
past.

## Dice and time

`2D6+1` means roll two six-sided dice and add 1. Round to the nearest whole
number; halves round up. A **round** is five seconds. A **scene** is one
continuous situation, such as a fight, a chase or a negotiation. Distances are
in metres; on a grid, a square is 2 m.
