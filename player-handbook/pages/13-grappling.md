# Grappling

**One controller holds one target. Defending against the seizure costs a Reaction.**

## Seize · Combat Action

Be adjacent and commit one free, usable grasping limb; you may commit a second.
An aware target may spend **one eligible Reaction** before the opposed roll:
your **Unarmed Combat** against their **Dodge or Parry**. Dodge uses the base
Reaction and counts toward its once-per-round limit. A weapon or shield Parry
uses Close Combat; an empty-handed Parry uses Unarmed Combat. An extra off-hand
Parry keeps its normal item restrictions and -1P.

If the target cannot or chooses not to defend, make an **unopposed Unarmed
Combat** test: success holds them; failure or Fumble does not. Otherwise, win
the opposed test to control the hold. If the attempt fails, your limbs remain free.

If the target wins with a **successful weapon Parry**, you also lose **1 HP
ignoring AP**. A shield/unarmed Parry, or a failed weapon Parry that wins the
contest, causes no such damage.

## While held together

While the grapple lasts, neither participant can take a **Movement Action**.
Both suffer **-1P on every test** and can attack or Parry only with Unarmed Combat
or a ready Light one-handed weapon. A committed limb cannot attack, Parry, or
qualify for an off-hand option.

Committing a **second limb gives +1B to your grapple contests**. During a hold,
either participant may add or release that second limb freely at the start of
their own turn. The held participant commits one free usable limb when first
making an escape or opposing grapple test; the first limb grants no bonus.

## Escape · Combat Action

The held participant must win **Unarmed Combat versus Unarmed Combat** to escape.
The controller's opposing roll does not spend a Reaction.
Only the controller may release freely, on their own turn. Separation, the
controller's incapacitation, or loss of their last committed limb also ends the hold.
An ordinary release or escape leaves you adjacent and frees committed limbs.

No second controller can seize the same target. Adjacent helpers can instead
provide combined Assistance, committing one limb each for that test; see page 04.
Size, anatomy, and footing may add modifiers or make a hold impossible.
