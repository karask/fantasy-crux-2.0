# Competing & Helping

When someone directly resists you, roll against each other. When they help,
their contribution can improve your roll.

## Competing

Both participants roll the relevant skill and choose their own final result
after Bonus or Penalty dice. Compare in this order:

1. **Grade:** Critical beats Success, which beats Failure, which beats Fumble.
2. **Same successful grade:** higher roll wins.
3. **Both Fail or both Fumble:** lower roll wins.
4. **Equal rolls:** higher unmodified base skill wins.
5. **Still tied:** the defender wins; without a defender, things stay as they were.

**Example:** A scout's Deception **42 Success** beats a sentry's Perception
**31 Success**. A **Critical** Perception would beat either ordinary Success.

Ordinary attacks and their Reactions use the combat procedure on pages 07–08,
not this comparison. Grappling explicitly uses an opposed test.

## Helping

Explain a practical way to help. An assistant normally needs **26% or more** in
the relevant skill and enough time to contribute.

Meaningful help grants **+1B in total**, regardless of how many people assist.
Exceptionally effective combined help may grant **+2B**. Combine this with other
modifiers, then cancel and cap normally.

**Example:** Two capable companions steady the ladder. Their combined help gives
the climber **+1B**, not one die for each helper.

## Editorial notes

**Sources:** Opposed tests → Compare results; Assistance → Helping a test.

**Visual:** Two linked situations: a scout passing a sentry and companions
steadying a ladder. Put `DECEPTION 42 / SUCCESS` and `PERCEPTION 31 / SUCCESS`
beside the relevant characters. One assistance badge connects both helpers.

**Exact labels:** `HIGHER SUCCESS WINS`, `COMBINED HELP +1B`.

**Must retain:** Matching failed grades use the lower number. Assistance requires
a plausible method; it is neither automatic nor a bonus per helper.
