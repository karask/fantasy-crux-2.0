# Handbook visual direction

## Purpose and page grammar

Each page answers one practical question through a memorable scene, a small
sequence, or a comparison. The page must teach at actual A5 reading size.
Artwork carries the action; exact text identifies what changes in the rules.

Use A5-format portrait pages on landscape A4 sheets cut in half and spiral bound.
The exact half-sheet trim is 148.5 × 210 mm (nominal A5). Allow 10 mm outer and
15 mm binding-edge text margins, with 9 mm at the top. Mirror the binding margin:
left on odd/front pages, right on even/back pages. The 15 mm allowance is a
working clearance, not a hole template; confirm it against the intended punch.
The full `a5/complete/` composition extends the v02 spiral prototype. The v01
folding proof and first A4 layout remain preserved for comparison.

One practical subject or a small related group belongs on each finished page.
Two complementary pages can make a spread, but each must also work when the
spiral is folded back to show one page. Keep essential diagrams off the binding
edge; do not carry an illustration or reading sequence across it. Merge sparse topics, but allow equipment
tables or dense procedures extra pages rather than forcing a fixed density.
Draft IDs 01–23 are not final folios. Set final numbering only after reflow, and
count covers and any bound licence pages in the physical total. Spiral binding
does not impose a multiple-of-four count. Unused half-sheet slots can stay blank
and need not become bound pages.

Three layouts share one design system:

- **Scene:** a dominant instructional illustration and small matched examples.
- **Sequence:** connected moments with labelled action costs and outcomes.
- **Reference:** grouped skill descriptions or a legible equipment table, with
  smaller supporting artwork.

Aim for a short takeaway, a clear example, and the necessary exceptions. Avoid
fixed word-count gates: simplify repetition and scenery before reducing type.
Body copy should normally be 11–12 pt at A5; equipment references may use 10 pt.
Never shrink a dense table or essential rule below 10 pt to meet a page count.
If a later rendered page cannot fit legibly, record the specific editorial issue
for review rather than silently omitting a rule or changing pagination.

## Cold Iron typography and colour

Reuse the website fonts from the installed local font packages:

- **Barlow Condensed 700:** large uppercase titles and strong section headings.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible 400 / 700:** rules copy, captions, and emphasis.
- **IBM Plex Mono 500:** modifiers, page numbers, and compact metadata.

Use warm bone `#e7e0d2` and light bone `#f4efe5` for page surfaces, ink `#171c1d`
for body text, iron `#1c2325` for dark bands, oxide `#a73e29` for penalties,
brass `#cbb06c` for explanatory leaders, and muted paper lines `#cbc2b2`.
Dark teal may distinguish defence or a positive modifier from the oxide attack
annotation. Always name the affected roll and print its sign explicitly; colour
alone never identifies a bonus, a penalty, or the person making the roll.

Use square corners, decisive rules, generous separation, and a narrow page
number rail. The reference tables keep dark headers, bone labels, and fine row
dividers. Avoid decorative scrollwork, faux-burnt parchment, glossy effects,
and game-interface panels unrelated to the website's visual language.

## Artwork

The canonical source is
`art/library/styles/inked-adventure-comic-vivid/STYLE.md`.

Hand-inked Western fantasy adventure comic: variable-weight brush contours,
believable simplified anatomy, bold readable silhouettes, flat opaque local
colour, two or three hard-edged shadow masses, selective material detail, and
sparse dry-brush texture. Mature and handmade. No painterly blending or glossy
3D. Use vivid natural warm/cool colour relationships; no forced sepia palette.

References:

- Style anchor: `art/library/styles/inked-adventure-comic-vivid/anchor.png`.

- Weapon and armour reference art: the `weapons-field-sketch` and
  `armour-field-sketch` subject masters in the same library.

Match cast identity, ink, and rendering. Do not inherit an anchor's scenery,
pose, lighting, or objects unless the scene requires them. New demonstrations
must show the correct actor/target relationship. Opponents may be humans or
orcs without bringing creature statistics or powers into the page.

## Annotations and examples

Generate clean artwork without lettering; typeset rules and labels separately.
The completed artifact is a single integrated page image, with editable text
retained in the composition. Use actual font glyphs for dice faces and numbers.

- Name the roll affected: `ATTACK −1P`, `DODGE −1P`, `EXTRA PARRY −1P`.
- Put modifiers next to the roller; connect environmental labels to their cause.
- Distinguish a sightline from an arrow in flight. A diagram line is not a new rule.
- Keep poses and equipment unambiguous; avoid labels over faces, hands, and contact points.
- Show `OR` where choices are exclusive, and clearly distinguish bonus dice from damage dice.
- Use common action symbols only with words: `ACTION`, `MOVE`, `REACTION`.
- Use ASCII `-` consistently in the new A5 PDF copy and modifier labels.
- Use no tables except the three equipment tables. In particular, outcome
  sequences replace the combat matrix and labelled panels replace cover tables.

## Full-edition review

The reference prototype is Take the Shot / Cover & Defence. Its two scenes show
the archer's shot and the soldier's active response as distinct moments. The
left scene reuses the first cover image; the right scene shows an arrow Parry.
Complete cover is one sentence. Do not spend another panel on the obvious wall.

Review the spread at actual A5 size and as a thumbnail. Check who receives
each modifier, that the defence happens only after a hit, and that the punch
zone does not consume text or labels. Test each page independently as well.
Confirm no cropped faces/contact points,
11-point body text, complete rules, adequate footer clearance, and correct PDF
cut-and-stack page order. Check duplex orientation before cutting and punching.
The user subsequently requested all remaining pages. The complete edition uses
the same typography, mirrored binding margins and website art direction.
Native diagrams teach exact dice, action and damage relationships; two new
illustrations show off-hand follow-up and Set Weapon against Charge. Equipment
references prioritise tables over decorative art. Preserve all earlier proofs.
