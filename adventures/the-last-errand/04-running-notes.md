# Running notes

GM only. A one-page reference for the table, followed by scaling, spotlights, rules notes and setting sources.

## At the table

**Every stop:** find the arch, understand the problem, deliver the letter if you can, get everyone into the circle by the third bell.

| Bell   | Ring it when…                                           | What happens                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| First  | the players understand what this stop demands           | the arch glows                       |
| Second | the players commit to a plan, or after about 15 minutes | the circle lights; a road appears    |
| Third  | the plan resolves, or after about 30 minutes            | last call: the gate takes the circle |

At the Station of Hanging Lamps, no bell rings until the arch is repaired. In the Couriers' Gallery, the same three bells time the doors.

**Seals:** five jumps make the whole route (Hearth, Scale, Rose, Hammer, then Star for home). Storm is the only spare. Each return for someone left behind burns one seal. Aster spends Star only on the homeward jump, unless persuaded at -2P.

**Deliveries:** hand the letter to whoever holds its office, or plausibly speaks for it, and say _"From the Crown, by the Errand."_ Each delivery restores one of Aster's memories. An undelivered letter makes the next arrival **rough**: everyone tests Athletics; a failure means landing prone and taking `1D4` damage, ignoring armour.

**Left behind:** Aster goes back if at least one letter has been delivered. Otherwise the company must win an opposed Influence test against her Persistence 50.

**Finale persuasion:** opposed Influence against Aster's Persistence 50. +1B per delivered letter, -1P per undelivered route letter, +1B for a courier speaking to her, +1B for a promise to deliver Tam's letter, +1B for Mirelle's rite, -1P if anyone threatened her or grabbed the satchel. Cap the net at +3B or -3P.

## Rules notes

**Darkness at the Station of Hanging Lamps.** A lantern lights 3 m. The fungus glow makes the rest of the station **partial darkness**: -1P to sight-based Perception and attacks. The chasm side is **darkness**: -2P and half Movement.

**Heat sight.** Blind Sight (heat) cannot see the ghouls, because ambient-temperature targets defeat it. The rule is exact; make the moment eerie.

**Ghoul venom.** A bite that deals HP damage exposes the target to Potency 22: the victim opposes it with Resilience after a delay of `1D3` rounds, and paralysis lasts `1D10` hours or until healing restores at least 1 HP. First aid restores `1D4` HP, so it frees the victim. A paralysed character is conscious and can still resist, but cannot act.

**The wyvern.** It is dangerous. A bite can deal a Major Wound in one blow. Remind the players about Hero Points before the Top Turn: one can downgrade a Major Wound and another can avoid death. Wyvern venom is Potency 60.

**Grapples.** A wyvern that holds someone cannot take a Movement Action, under the normal grapple rules, so it cannot fly away with them. The danger is the sting and the drop. The victim escapes with a Combat Action and an opposed Unarmed Combat test.

**Persuading a ghost.** Influence against Persistence is an ordinary opposed test. Spirit Combat is the hostile alternative. Both are in the published [Ghost](../../src/content/rules/creatures/ghost.md) and the creature rules.

**Healing between stops.** The whole adventure takes one night. Each patient can receive one first-aid attempt before the next full rest, and only one HP-restoring Shaping, so Tamsin's healing is precious. Spend it where it matters.

## The gate in rules terms

The players never need this; it is here so that the GM can answer questions consistently.

Each station's arch is a **permanent, anchored imperial working**: a ritual `Bend·Paths` effect, Intensity 3, Range 4 (a marked, linked station), Reach covering the station's brass circle, Duration 5. Like every Crownwork, it remains active through a physical anchor: the station's arch and lamp. Most stations failed when their anchors failed.

Each **warrant seal** is a single-use operating permission, much like the Tide Lock's treaty pearl. Presenting a seal satisfies one of the working's conditions, and the seal burns. The warrant records its route order, and the arch reads it.

The gate moves everyone inside the circle when the third bell ends. Under the Bend·Paths rules, an unwilling subject could resist relocation with Resilience; treat the working's stored casting result as 75. Hardly anyone resists being taken out of a sacred grove, a ghoul's station or a wyvern's mesa, but someone might resist being taken _into_ the Hall of Measures.

Aster casts nothing. As the warrant's last bearer and its anchored spirit, she presents its seals. A living courier holding the warrant can do the same, which is why **the Courier's Last Road** works.

After the final jump, the Ninth Milestone's arch crumbles. The network leaves no shortcut behind for later play. Distance in the Crownless Realms stays exactly as hard as it was.

## Setting: what comes from where

**From the published gazetteer:**

- The backward bell, the empty Hall of Measures, the crown of ash, and the mirrors that showed different claimants.
- Crownseat's three governments and their guard posts.
- The Seven Lamps.
- The Verdant Court's three kinds of land, and the Deep-Road Compact and Nine Warrens with their overlapping tunnels.
- The Iron Banner's witnessed oaths and old service contracts, and its wyverns.
- The Saffron Reach's season law, the Wester Capes' harpies, and the Pearlwater's merfolk air-halls.
- The Synod's Rose Lamp hospital, and the Shaping tradition signs used here: Araini leaves turning pale and Banner oathwork's second beat.

**From the [Crownless Realms setting draft](../../potential-worlds/the-crownless-realms/the-crownless-realms.md):** the treaty pearl's model of operating permissions, used here for the warrant seals, and the warning to telegraph monsters before they strike.

**New in this adventure:**

- The Imperial Errand and its waygates, which are lesser works and not Crownworks.
- The Ninth Milestone posting-house, Aster Vael and the Crowning Letters.
- The Consulting Oak and Mother Rhull; the Station of Hanging Lamps and Corporal Hesk; the Red Switchback and Urza Ironvow.
- The Couriers' Gallery inside the Hall of Measures.
- The Gilded Spur Company, Brack, Mirelle, Gannet and Wenna.

None of these changes the published gazetteer.

## Using it with Seven Bells for No King

The Last Errand works on its own. It also fits before [Seven Bells for No King](../seven-bells-for-no-king/README.md) as an alternative opening:

- **Aster's testimony** that "every road refused the Crown in the same breath" is a first, deniable clue to the coronation's failed override. Nobody can prove it from a ghost's memory.
- **The Couriers' Gallery** gives no lasting access to the Hall of Measures: its arch dies with the last jump, and the bronze grille stays locked. The campaign's own route into the Hall is unaffected.
- **The Hall's bells ringing** is public news in Crownseat. Treat it as an early step of the advancing crisis, or as an omen every claimant interprets differently.

If your campaign reserves the Hall of Measures for its finale, move Scene 5 to **the Old Errand House**: the courier depot across the square from the Hall, with a window onto its sealed doors. Everything else in the scene stays the same.

## If you have more time

If the table reaches Scene 4 well ahead of time, expand the **Saffron Reach** flash into a full stop:

- The station is a waystation on a salt causeway at noon, during a seasonal handover.
- The dry magistrate's authority ends at sunset and the flood magistrate's begins. Each claims the company as a prisoner of their season.
- The Saffron Reach letter belongs to whichever magistrate holds the season at the moment of delivery.
- Heat and thirst are the clock.

It burns one seal, so the company loses its spare.
