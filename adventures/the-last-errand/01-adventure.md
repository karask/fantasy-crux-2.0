# The Last Errand

**GM only.** A four-hour introduction to the Crownless Realms for an established company of four pregenerated PCs; three or five also work (see [Scaling](04-running-notes.md#scaling)). Read this file and [the people](02-npcs.md) before play. Give the players only [the brief](03-player-brief-and-handouts.md) and the handouts as they find them.

## The adventure in a minute

A storm drives the company's night coach into a ruined imperial posting-house. A rival company is waiting inside for the satchel the company's client is carrying. In the middle of the fight, the posting-house's great arch wakes, a bell rings backward, and everyone standing in the floor's circle vanishes.

The satchel is the anchor of a ghost. Aster Vael was an imperial courier who left the Hall of Measures on coronation night, ninety-three years ago, carrying the **Crowning Letters**: proclamations of a coronation that never happened. The backward bell threw her home to this posting-house, where she died alone. Tonight she resumes her route, and the old waygate takes the coach, its passengers, the company and its rivals with her.

Each stop shows a different corner of the realms, its people and its law: a Verdant Court assembly in a sacred grove, a deep-road station beneath the Ironspines where cold things hunt in the dark, and a wyvern's mesa on the Emberplain with Banner riders below. At every stop the gate leaves on its **third bell**, and anyone outside the circle stays behind. The satchel's ribbon of **seven seals** burns one seal per jump, with exactly one to spare.

The ghost can no longer hold her letters. Each letter the company delivers to whoever holds its office now gives back a piece of who she was. Her route ends where it began: the Couriers' Gallery of the Hall of Measures, beside the empty throne and the crown of ash. There, while three rival guard posts batter at the doors, she learns what became of her emperor. The company must persuade a grieving ghost to spend her last seal and take everyone home.

## GM truth

### Aster Vael and the Crowning Letters

On coronation night, Courier-Second Aster Vael of the Imperial Errand, aged twenty-four, stepped into the Couriers' Gallery arch at the eighth bell. Her satchel held seven Crowning Letters, to be read aloud at dawn. Three were for the stations on her own route: the Western Wood, the Deep Causeway and the Eastern Banner. The other four were for the southern relay riders she was to meet at the Eastern Banner. Each letter announced that Caldras IV would be crowned at the ninth bell, ordered the provincial beacon lit, and granted a coronation gift. Her warrant ribbon carried seven seals: four for the jumps of her route (out to three stations and back to the Hall), two spares "for the road's mischief", and the Star seal, which always brings a courier home.

As the Gate seal burned for her first jump, the coronation bell rang once, backward. Every waygate refused the Crown's direction at the same moment. Courier law says that a failing road brings its courier home, so the network threw Aster to her home station: the **Ninth Milestone posting-house** on the old Rill Post Road, west of Vellbridge. She landed badly. The station's keepers had gone to Vellbridge for the coronation festival. As couriers were trained to do when a route failed, she locked herself and the letters in the warrant vault beneath the hall. That vault opens from outside only to a courier's warrant, and hers was inside with her. She wrote a letter to her younger brother, Tam, and died before anyone came. Nobody ever did.

Her spirit stayed bound to the satchel. She has slept in the dark for ninety-three years, still on duty.

### What woke her

A month ago, salvagers broke into the warrant vault with picks. They left her bones and sold the satchel twice: first to Captain Sabel Ardent of the Gilded Spur Company, who paid a deposit on behalf of House Castaine's archivist, and then to the antiquarian **Oswin Brack**, who paid more in coin. Brack has hired the Last Wagon Company to escort his coach to Vellbridge overnight, where a League collector will view the satchel at dawn. The Gilded Spur guessed his route and waited out the rain in the one shelter on it: the Ninth Milestone.

When the satchel comes home to the place she died, Aster wakes. The arch knows her warrant. Her route resumes from its first stop, and nothing tonight will make her change its order until she has seen the empty throne.

### The waygates and the seals

The waygates are lesser imperial works, not Crownworks. Each station has a stone arch over a mosaic circle. When a warrant seal is presented and the third bell rings, the station moves everyone and everything inside the circle to the next station on the warrant's route. Almost every station has failed since the empire fell; a few still answer. The ghost does not cast anything: the warrant opens the arch, much as a key opens a lock. (For exact rules, see [the gate's rules note](04-running-notes.md#the-gate-in-rules-terms).)

The satchel's ribbon carries seven wax seals, each stamped with one of the Seven Lamps. Each jump burns the next seal to ash. The Gate seal burned on the failed jump, so tonight's route reaches one seal further down the ribbon and leaves a single spare:

| Seal   | Jump                                                 |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Gate   | Already ash: the Hall of Measures, 93 years ago      |
| Hearth | Ninth Milestone to the Western Wood (the Greenward)  |
| Scale  | Western Wood to the Deep Causeway (the Ironspines)   |
| Rose   | Deep Causeway to the Eastern Banner (the Emberplain) |
| Hammer | Eastern Banner to the Hall of Measures (Crownseat)   |
| Storm  | Spare: "for the road's mischief"                     |
| Star   | Courier law keeps it for the homeward jump           |

Give the players the [seal ribbon](03-player-brief-and-handouts.md#handout-5-the-seal-ribbon) as soon as they notice the seals. They will watch it all night.

### The letters

| Letter                                              | Its coronation gift                                                                                                               | Who could receive it now                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| To the Imperial Steward of the Western Wood         | The timber levy is remitted and the Crown's foresters are recalled                                                                | The office is abolished. The Verdant Court's Speaker, or the dryad as the voice of the land       |
| To the Warden of the Deep Causeway                  | Confirms Warden Brakka Hollowhand, a dwarf, and Gallery-Factor Snick of the Nine Warrens, a goblin, to keep the Causeway together | The Compact and the Warrens each claim the office. Its text supports both                         |
| To the Captain of the Eastern Banner                | The Banner's arrears will be paid in full at the Crowning, with the Crown's thanks                                                | Any sworn Banner captain. To the Banner, this is a sealed imperial admission of a debt still owed |
| Saffron Reach, Wester Capes, Pearlwater, Salt Coast | Various                                                                                                                           | Meant for relay riders who are long gone. They stay in the satchel for the epilogue               |
| Aster to her brother Tam                            | A personal letter on the back of a waybill                                                                                        | Tam is long dead; his grave is in the Synod burial ground at Vellbridge                           |

Aster is Incorporeal and can no longer hold paper, so living hands must make each delivery. A letter counts as **delivered** when someone hands it to a person who holds its office, or plausibly speaks for it, and says the courier's words: _"From the Crown, by the Errand."_ The wax seal on the letter then melts clear. The GM decides what counts as plausible; reward good arguments, ceremony and respect.

**Why deliveries matter:**

- **Aster remembers.** Each delivery restores one memory and makes her stronger. See the ladder in [her profile](02-npcs.md#aster-vael).
- **Smooth roads.** If the last stop's letter was delivered, the next arrival is smooth. If it was not, the arrival is **rough**: everyone tests Athletics; on a failure they land prone and take `1D4` damage, ignoring armour.
- **The finale.** Each delivered letter helps persuade Aster at the end; each undelivered one hinders it.

### The bells and the seals: how the gate works at the table

**Three bells.** At every stop the gate leaves on its third bell. Ring them yourself; a real handbell on the table is ideal.

- **First bell:** the arch glows. Ring it when the players understand what this stop demands.
- **Second bell:** the mosaic circle lights and a road appears inside the arch. Ring it when the players commit to a plan, or after about 15 minutes of table time.
- **Third bell:** last call. Ring it when the plan resolves, or after about 30 minutes of table time. Everyone inside the circle at the end of that round is taken; everyone outside stays behind. For the scramble, turn a one-minute sand timer or count down aloud and let the players act as fast as they can talk.

**Left behind.** If anyone is left behind, the company can tell Aster to go back. Going back burns the next seal and returns everyone, moments later, to the same stop; the bells restart at the second bell. Aster agrees at once if at least one letter has been delivered. Otherwise the company must win an opposed Influence test against her Persistence 50. The company can afford **one** return before it threatens the Star seal. Aster refuses to spend the Star seal on anything but the homeward jump unless the company persuades her at **-2P**.

Anyone left behind for good is not killed by it. They come home weeks later with a story, a debt and a new acquaintance; resolve it in the epilogue.

The whole route uses five seals. Each return uses one more. That arithmetic is the night's real clock; let the players do it.

## Keep it to four hours

Start with the company already on the road; each player needs only a one-sentence introduction. The stops are self-contained. If you run late, the gate can skip one: see the cuts below.

| Table time | Scene                           | Purpose                                                |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 0:00–0:10  | Company and the road            | Introductions, the client, the storm                   |
| 0:10–0:35  | 1. The Ninth Milestone          | Tests, opposed tests and combat basics; the gate wakes |
| 0:35–1:15  | 2. The Consulting Oak           | Influence, a people and its law, the first delivery    |
| 1:15–1:25  | Break                           | Leave the seal ribbon on the table                     |
| 1:25–2:15  | 3. The Station of Hanging Lamps | Darkness, teamwork under attack, a political choice    |
| 2:15–2:55  | 4. The Red Switchback           | A chase, a monster, a Banner oath                      |
| 2:55–3:05  | Flashes                         | The scale of the realms, three heartbeats at a time    |
| 3:05–3:45  | 5. The Couriers' Gallery        | Awe, grief, a siege and the last persuasion            |
| 3:45–4:00  | Epilogue                        | Consequences, burial, pay                              |

These are ceilings, not targets. If a stop goes quickly, bank the time for the finale.

**If running late:** after 15 minutes of arguing at any stop, have an NPC propose a workable plan. At 2:15, if Scene 3 is still running, ring the third bell. If you reach 2:30 before starting Scene 4, skip it: the gate stutters, the Rose seal burns, and the flashes lead straight to the Hall, which leaves the Eastern Banner letter undelivered. Always keep 20 minutes for the finale and 10 for the epilogue.

**Keep them on edge.** End every stop on its third bell, mid-scramble, and open the next stop with the new place already dangerous. Keep the seal ribbon and a bell-track in sight. Telegraph every threat before it strikes. Give every player one moment per stop; the [spotlight table](04-running-notes.md#who-shines-where) helps.

## Before Scene 1: the company and the road

Each player names their character and one thing they carry that matters to them. Then read or paraphrase:

> _Rain hammers the coach roof. Old Gannet has been swearing at the Rill Post Road for an hour. Your client, Master Oswin Brack, has not let go of his iron-bound strongbox since Rillmarket. Opposite him, a young Synod novice prays in a whisper. You are being paid fifty silver each to get them all to Vellbridge by dawn. The Last Wagon Company does not leave anyone behind._

Ask one or two players a question: _What did Brack say when you asked what is in the box? Who on this coach makes you uneasy, and why?_

## Scene 1: The Ninth Milestone

**Purpose:** teach the D100 test, opposed tests and combat; introduce the client, the rivals and the gate.

### Arrival

The coach cracks a wheel in a flooded rut. Gannet steers it, limping, under the broad portico of the old **Ninth Milestone posting-house**: a squat, roofless imperial building of grey stone. Inside, a great arch stands at the centre of a mosaic floor of seven lamps, ringed by a circle of brass inlay ten metres across. Words are cut into the arch; give the players [Handout 2](03-player-brief-and-handouts.md#handout-2-the-arch-inscription) if anyone reads them. Courier stalls line the walls. A stair descends to a vault whose iron door hangs open on fresh pick marks.

Gannet drives the coach and horses into the hall to keep them out of the rain. **This puts the whole coach inside the brass circle.** Point that out only if someone asks about the circle.

**What the company can find before the trouble starts:**

- **The vault** (Perception, or simply going down): a narrow stone room with a bench, an empty iron hook and the bones of a young person in rags of imperial blue. A courier's horn lies by the hand. Pick marks are a month old at most. Healing or Lore: the bones show a broken leg that was splinted and never healed.
- **Brack's strongbox** (Perception against his Deception 60): he keeps glancing between the box and the vault stair.
- **Mirelle** goes cold and still in the hall: _"Someone is standing in the arch."_ Nobody else sees anyone. The wax seals on the satchel inside Brack's box are faintly warm, if anyone checks.

### The ambush

Give them a few minutes, then the **Gilded Spur Company** steps out of the stalls: Captain Sabel Ardent, the orc Tarn Gorvash and the Shaper Lissa Fenn. Sabel has her sword drawn and is polite.

> _"Master Brack. You sold my satchel twice. I've come for the one you owe me. Nobody needs to bleed for a box of old paper."_

Brack hides behind the company and swears he bought it fairly, which is half true. Let the players talk. Sabel's offer: hand over the satchel, keep the client, keep the fee. She will not hurt the passengers. She does not know what the satchel really is.

Social options are real. Pebb's Deception or Influence against her Persistence 45 can buy time or doubt. A good argument that Brack's receipt is legal gives +1B. If the players give up the satchel, the gate still wakes when Sabel carries it across the circle.

If it comes to blows, run **two or three rounds of a short, sharp fight**. Sabel wants the box, not bodies; Tarn grapples whoever holds it; Lissa threatens a firebolt and uses it only if someone draws blood. Teach Combat Order, Dodge and Parry, Parry Size, and armour. It is a scuffle, not a massacre.

### The gate wakes

At the end of the third round, or the moment the satchel crosses the circle (whichever comes first), stop the fight:

> _Every straight line in the hall doubles. You taste copper. The arch fills with cold blue light, and somewhere close, a bell rings once, backward. A young woman in imperial blue stands in the arch with a satchel ribbon in her hand that is not there. "Errand resumed," she says. "Western Wood. Stand within the circle." On the real satchel, the Hearth seal begins to glow._

Ring the bells quickly: the first as she speaks, the second as the circle lights, the third as everyone in the hall stares at one another. Anyone who deliberately runs outside the circle stays in the rain; nobody will, unless they mean to.

The gate takes everyone in the circle: the company, the rivals, the passengers, the coach and the horses. The Hearth seal turns to ash.

### Cliffhanger

End the scene on the light. Before Scene 2, give the players a breath to realise that they are fighting people who are now stranded with them.

## Scene 2: The Consulting Oak

**Region:** the Greenward, home of the Verdant Court. **Purpose:** a people and its law; Influence and opposed tests; the first delivery.

### Arrival

> _Grey light, birdsong, and wet moss under your boots. The coach has come to rest in the middle of a clearing ringed with carved standing stones, and the horses have just trampled a bed of ceremonial seedlings. Around you, thirty people rise from benches in shocked silence: elves in green advocates' sashes, human foresters with charcoal on their hands, wardens with spears. Behind you, an oak older than the empire has grown around a stone arch. Its roots fill the arch like bars. A woman made partly of bark is sitting among them, watching you._

The company has landed in the middle of a **Verdant Court consultation**. The assembly was debating whether to reopen the old Crownroad through the forest. The young elves want it open, the old houses remember the imperial timber levies, and the human charcoal towns fear the Court will erase their charters. Now armed strangers, a coach and an imperial ghost have fallen into the sacred grove. Everyone reads it as an omen for their own side.

**Warden Hob Ashby** and six grove wardens level spears: _"Trespass in a consulted grove."_ **Speaker Aelin Thorne**, who presides, raises one hand, and the spears stop. Explain the law through her: the Court divides land into land that may be owned, land that may only be tended, and land old enough that it must be **consulted**. This grove must be consulted. Its voice is the dryad in the oak, **Mother Rhull**.

### The problem

Aster appears in the arch, faint and confused, and says only her route words: _"Western Wood. The Steward is expected."_ Ring the **first bell** when the players understand two things:

1. The arch is inside Mother Rhull's oak, and its roots fill the circle. Nothing leaves unless she opens them. She sealed this arch herself sixty years ago: _"Nothing of the empire passes through my tree again."_
2. The satchel holds a letter to the "Imperial Steward of the Western Wood". The Court abolished that office ninety years ago.

### Pressures

Use one or two at a time.

- **Lissa panics.** Stranded and terrified, she whispers that she can burn the roots out of the arch. If nobody stops her, she starts her Practice at the second bell. Fire in a consulted grove would turn the assembly into a battle the company cannot win. Her Tell, every flame in the clearing bending towards her, gives the players one round of warning.
- **Brack sells.** He sidles up to the Court's archivist and offers "a genuine imperial proclamation, very rare" for 200 silver.
- **The horses bolt** when the oak's leaves turn their pale undersides out, a sign of Shaping. Gannet runs after them into the trees. If nobody helps, he is not back by the third bell.
- **Sabel proposes a truce:** _"We get home together, then we settle the satchel. Agreed?"_ It is sincere for now.

### Ways through

Reward any sensible plan. These are the likeliest:

- **Read the letter aloud.** Once someone breaks the seal and reads [Handout 3](03-player-brief-and-handouts.md#handout-3-a-crowning-letter), the assembly falls silent. The Crown remitted the timber levy that the old houses still remember. It was granted ninety-three years too late, on the night the empire ended. The old elves are moved; the young ones are stunned. This gives +1B with Mother Rhull and the Speaker.
- **Consult the land.** Under Verdant law anyone may petition the land by sitting with its voice and giving testimony. Run an opposed Influence test against Mother Rhull's Persistence 60, with up to +1B for help. Bonus dice:
  - **+1B** for honest respect, such as an apology for the seedlings.
  - **+1B** for healing the oak root the coach wheel gouged. Tamsin's Alter·Flesh works on plants. Mirelle can treat it with a Healing Kit at -1P.
  - **+1B** for testimony the Court takes seriously. Verse is testimony here. Orren's terrible verse, recited sincerely, earns respect for courage. Seris can tell them honestly that she is an elf raised in Whitewater, not an Araini.
- **Deliver the letter.** Speaker Thorne can accept it "on behalf of the land", but only with Mother Rhull's consent. Mother Rhull can accept it as the land's own voice. Either way, say the courier's words: _"From the Crown, by the Errand."_
- **Mother Rhull's price.** She asks the company to carry one of her acorns home and plant it where the courier died. Nobody needs to know yet what that means. Make sure someone carries the acorn.
- **Force:** cutting or burning the roots. Telegraph clearly: the wardens are ready, and Mother Rhull's Bend·Mind and Alter·Mind are dangerous. If the players insist, the third bell rings early, mid-fight, and the delivery is lost.

**If the persuasion fails,** Speaker Thorne asks the assembly for mercy, and the oak still opens, grudgingly. Mother Rhull keeps the letter unread, so it is not delivered, and the wardens march the company into the circle at spear-point. Nobody is stuck in the grove for failing a roll.

When Mother Rhull consents, the roots open like fingers. Ring the **second bell**, then the **third** once everyone, including Gannet and the horses, is back in the circle.

### The first delivery

If the letter is delivered, Aster manifests fully for the first time, kneels and speaks clearly:

> _"From the Crown, by the Errand. I am Aster Vael, Courier-Second of the Imperial Errand. Forgive me. I am late."_

She remembers her name and her duty, and she can now talk with the company. She still insists on her route.

The Scale seal burns as the gate takes them.

## Scene 3: The Station of Hanging Lamps

**Region:** the Ironspines, beneath the old imperial Deep Causeway. **Purpose:** darkness and light rules, heat sight, teamwork under attack, a political choice.

### Arrival

> _Black. Cold. Water dripping into a depth you can hear but not see. Your lanterns show a stone shelf at the lip of a chasm, and the rear wheels of the coach turning slowly over nothing._

If the Western Wood letter was not delivered, this is a **rough arrival**: everyone tests Athletics, and those who fail land prone and take `1D4` damage, ignoring armour.

**First, the coach.** Its rear wheels hang over the chasm, and Mirelle and Brack are still inside. Hauling it back is brute force: an Athletics test with up to +1B for help, and Orren shines here. Nerin's measured shove can pull one passenger clear. Moving the coach itself needs a wagon-sized target, Reach 1, which makes the working M4: an overreach at -1P. Gannet calms the horses. Failure means the coach slides: the passengers jump clear (Dodge), and the coach is lost into the chasm. That is a real loss but not a disaster.

### The station

The **Station of Hanging Lamps** is an imperial waystation cut into the rock beside the Causeway's side galleries. Dead lamp-cages hang over the chasm on long chains. Faint blue fungus glows on the walls, so everything beyond lantern light is **partial darkness**, and the chasm side is **darkness**. The arch stands against the back wall. It is jammed: its great counterweight has fallen, a cracked gear sits in its housing, and its anchor lamp hangs out over the chasm, unlit.

**No bells ring until the arch is repaired.**

In the old signal room, behind a jammed door, two people have been trapped for a day:

- **Kettle Brassneedle**, a goblin surveyor of the Nine Warrens, bruised and hungry.
- **Haldra Stonemoot**, a dwarf debt-witness of the Deep-Road Compact, Wounded with a splinted leg.

They came on a joint survey to settle which of their peoples holds this station, and they have argued for a whole day. They warn the company in whispers: _"Cold ones. They came out of the dark when our lamp oil ran low."_

### Cold things in the dark

The station's imperial road guard never left. When the gates failed on coronation night, the tunnel behind them collapsed, and Corporal Hesk's crew starved here. They rose as **ghouls**. There are three: Hesk and two others.

**Heat sight fails.** Pebb, Dori and any other goblin or dwarf with Blind Sight (heat) see warm bodies in the dark. The ghouls are as cold as the stone. Tell those players only that _the dark beyond the lanterns is empty of warm bodies_, and let the howling start.

### The repair, under attack

Three tasks, each one a spotlight:

| Task                         | How                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Raise the counterweight**  | Athletics (brute force), with up to +1B for help. A human-sized stone block is exactly what Nerin's measured shove can move.                                        |
| **Replace the cracked gear** | Mechanisms (Dori, Kettle or Nerin), at -1P without a lantern at the work. Kettle knows where the station kept spares. Earth Sense tells Dori the housing is sound.  |
| **Light the anchor lamp**    | Swing the lamp-cage in on its chain: Athletics to reach out over the drop, a thrown line, or Nerin's shove. Then light it from a lantern, or with flint and tinder. |

Run the ghouls in **three waves**, timed to the work:

1. **When work begins:** a howl from the dark. Every living listener tests Persistence against Hesk's Persistence 30. Losers take -1P on attacks and Reactions while any howl is audible; winners are immune for the rest of the fight. A ghoul probes the edge of the light and tries to seize the nearest isolated person, often Mirelle as she tends Haldra, and bite.
2. **When the first task is done:** two ghouls come down the hanging chains. Hesk speaks from the dark in old Crown Speech: _"Courier... have you brought our relief?"_
3. **When the second task is done:** all three attack in earnest, unless a parley is under way.

A damaging ghoul bite carries venom: after `1D3` rounds the victim resists with Resilience against Potency 22, or is **paralysed** for `1D10` hours, or until healing restores at least 1 HP. First aid or Tamsin's healing frees a victim. Make a paralysed friend a rescue problem, not a death sentence.

**If a repair keeps failing,** let Kettle and Haldra take it over together. It costs one more ghoul wave, but the arch gets fixed. The station never traps the company; it only makes them pay.

### The parley

Hesk is intelligent and still a soldier. If **at least one letter has been delivered**, Aster is strong enough to command him:

> _"Corporal Hesk! Stand to!"_

He freezes. Let the players parley. He asks: _"Has the Emperor been crowned?"_ How they answer matters. The truth breaks him, and he weeps without tears. A lie holds him but disgusts Aster. A **new order** works best: _"Guard this station against all comers until you are relieved."_ He salutes, and the ghouls withdraw into the dark for good.

Without Aster's command, the ghouls can still be held off with fire, light and steel, or bargained with using fresh meat. They will take the horses' fodder, or a horse.

### Who is the Warden?

When the arch is repaired, ring the **first bell**. Now the letter: _"To the Warden of the Deep Causeway."_ Kettle and Haldra both reach for it.

Read the letter aloud and its gift settles nothing: it confirms Warden Brakka Hollowhand, a dwarf, and Gallery-Factor Snick of the Nine Warrens, a goblin, _to keep the Causeway together_. Each claims the other's people were only the deputy.

- **Give it to one of them.** That counts as delivered, and the other storms off. Note it for the epilogue.
- **Give it to both,** in both their hands at once, after they agree to keep the station jointly as the letter says. This is the best result, and it takes an opposed Influence test against Haldra's Persistence 60. +1B if Pebb drafts the agreement properly on the spot, and +1B if someone points out that the company just saved them both. That counts as delivered.
- **Give it to nobody.** It stays undelivered, and the next arrival is rough.

Ring the **second bell**, then the **third**. Kettle and Haldra stay; this is their station, and their people will come for them. They may beg to come along, which is a chance for players to make the circle crowded.

If two or more letters are now delivered, Aster remembers coronation night: _"I heard the bell ring backward as I stepped into the arch. Every road screamed at once. They all refused the Crown in the same breath."_

The Rose seal burns.

## Scene 4: The Red Switchback

**Region:** the Emberplain March, land of the Iron Banner. **Purpose:** a chase, a monster, Banner law and a redemption.

### Arrival

> _Heat. Red stone. A sky so wide it hurts. You are standing on a cracked landing-stone at the foot of a mesa, and the arch is not here. It is three hundred metres above you, on the mesa top, at the end of a crumbling switchback road. On the next spire, something with leathery wings stretches in the sun. Below, a column of dust is coming towards you at a gallop._

The jump landed at the station's **lower landing-stone**. Imperial mesa relays had two stones: the waygate dropped arrivals below and sent departures from the arch above. If the Deep Causeway letter was not delivered, the arrival is rough.

Ring the **first bell** at once. The arch is ringing far above. The company has until the third bell to get everyone up the switchback.

### The riders

The dust is **Rider-Captain Urza Ironvow** and five Banner riders, hunting the wyvern that has been taking their herds. They see an imperial coach, imperial blue light and armed strangers on Banner land. Then one rider shouts a name: **Tarn Gorvash**.

Tarn is an oath-breaker. Three years ago he swore to guard a caravan through wyvern country and fled when the wyvern came, and three people died. The Banner's oath-court named him, and every Banner rider may bring him in. Urza demands him: _"Stand aside, company. He is ours under witnessed law."_

Tarn freezes. Sabel looks at the company. Banner law is blunt, literate and fixed on witnessed obligation, and it gives a way through:

- **Hand Tarn over.** It is lawful. Sabel will not forgive it, and Tarn goes quietly.
- **Stand witness.** Banner law lets a new witnessed oath answer an old one. If Tarn swears before witnesses to return and face the oath-court within the season, and someone in the company stands as his guarantor, Urza will accept it. As the oath is sworn, every voice nearby picks up a second beat: Banner oathwork. The guarantor is now bound too. It is a real cost.
- **Fight or flee.** The riders shoot from below while the wyvern dives from above. Telegraph how bad an idea that is.

### The letter

The Eastern Banner letter promises the Banner's arrears _paid in full at the Crowning, with the Crown's thanks_. To the Banner, a sealed imperial admission of a debt still owed is worth more than gold. Urza is a sworn Banner captain, so she can receive it. Brack, of course, tries to sell it to her for 500 silver while the wyvern is circling.

If the company delivers it with the courier's words, Urza reads it twice, closes her eyes and says: _"Ninety-three years. Witnessed."_ Her riders cover the climb with their bows, which gives +1B to every test against the wyvern.

### The climb

The coach must climb three legs of the switchback to reach the arch. On each leg:

1. **The driver tests Driving.** Gannet is Driving 60, or a PC can take the reins. Someone leading the horses gives +1B. The **Narrows**, the middle leg, is one coach wide and crumbling: -1P there. Failure on the Narrows puts a wheel over the edge, and everyone on that side tests Dodge or Athletics to stay aboard. Hauling the wheel back takes brute force; Nerin can shove the coach only at Reach 1, an M4 overreach at -1P. If that fails, the coach is lost and everyone climbs on foot, with the horses led.
2. **The wyvern makes one pass:** a single Bite or Claw, not its Multiattack, at an exposed target. It goes for a horse first, then anyone on the roof or walking alongside. It has flown more than 30 m since the archers' last turn, so they shoot at -2P; Aim helps.
3. **Everyone else acts once:** shoot, shield someone, steady the horses, throw a rope, shout to the riders below.

Ring the **second bell** after the Narrows.

**At the Top Turn**, the wyvern lands between the coach and the arch. It uses its full Multiattack for one round, then seizes someone at the cliff edge in its claws: a grapple with Unarmed Combat, which the target can oppose by spending a Reaction to Dodge or Parry. Mirelle or Lissa makes the most painful choice. While it holds them, its venomous sting hangs over them and a 300-metre drop yawns behind. Breaking the hold is the scene's climax: the victim's escape test, Orren's Wrestler, or enough damage to break the wyvern's nerve.

The wyvern breaks off and flees when it has been **hit three times**, by anyone, even if its scales stop the damage; when it takes **5 or more damage** after armour; or when it suffers any **Critical**. With 5 AP, ordinary arrows rarely hurt it, but they do drive it off. If it is holding someone, it lets go as it flees.

It is deadly. A bite can deal a Major Wound in one blow. Make sure the players see it coming, remind them of Hero Points, and do not use its Multiattack before the Top Turn.

Ring the **third bell** when the company reaches the arch, or when the round after the seizure ends.

If three letters are now delivered, Aster remembers dying: _"The Milestone was empty. Everyone had gone to see the fireworks. I locked the vault, the way they taught us. I wrote to Tam. I promised him the coronation fireworks."_ She looks at the company's clothes: _"How long has it been?"_

The Hammer seal burns.

## Flashes

**Purpose:** the scale of the realms, in three heartbeats at a time.

The gate stutters. It tries three dead stations on the way to Crownseat and fails each time. Describe each flash in two sentences, let one player react, and move on:

1. **The Saffron Reach:** blinding white salt under a blue sky. A lizardfolk judge in a reed palanquin under a parasol stares at you, raises a seal and declares in Tessarak: _"Wrong season!"_ Then the white is gone.
2. **The Wester Capes:** fog, wind and a cliff tower. A harpy shrieks and grabs at the coach roof, and someone tests Dodge or loses one carried item; Brack's strongbox makes a lovely target. Gone.
3. **The Pearlwater:** cold water floods the circle to the waist. Through an air-bell, merfolk faces stare in, and one pushes a half-drowned stranger into the circle: a League sailor named **Wenna Cray**. Gone, and Wenna is now a passenger. Nobody gets left behind.

The flashes burn no extra seal.

## Scene 5: The Couriers' Gallery

**Region:** Crownseat, inside the sealed Hall of Measures. **Purpose:** the setting's central mystery seen up close; grief, persuasion and a siege.

### Arrival

> _Dust. Silence, the deep silence of a place nobody has breathed in for ninety-three years. You are in a long vaulted gallery lined with seven arches; only yours still glows. Pigeonholes full of unsent letters line one wall. Mirrors hang between the arches, every one of them cracked. At the far end, through a tall bronze grille, a vast hall opens: a throne on a dais, empty. On a velvet cushion before it lies a perfect circle of grey ash, in the shape of a crown._

The **mirrors** in the great hall do not agree: each shows a different figure seated on the throne, though the throne itself is empty. Describe one or two; do not explain them.

Then, for the first time in ninety-three years, the Hall's own bells ring, forward, announcing the courier's return. All of Crownseat hears them.

### The siege

Three governments hold Crownseat, and each keeps a guard post at one of the Hall's three bronze doors: **Veyran**, **Ordel** and **Castaine**. Each now believes one of the others has broken into the Hall. Run the doors on the same three bells:

- **First bell** (on arrival): drums and shouting outside. Through the doors, each guard post accuses the others.
- **Second bell:** hammers on bronze. The Castaine captain calls through his door: _"Whoever is inside: hand out the relic and you walk free."_ Sabel hears it. Her patron is Castaine.
- **Third bell:** a door gives. You pick which, or let the players' barricades decide. Claimant guards pour in at the end of that round.

Barricades buy time. Moving the coach against a door, wedging the bronze with a hammer haft, or a convincing shout through the door ("Synod vigil! Seven Lamps' sanctuary!", with Mirelle's help) each delay that door by one bell.

### The errand's end

Aster walks to the grille and kneels:

> _"Majesty. The errand is done. The provinces have the news."_

Nothing answers. She sees the empty throne and the ash, and she understands: there was no coronation, and there is no emperor. Frost spreads across the gallery floor. The cracked mirrors chime. Her voice breaks:

> _"Then there is no one to report to. I will wait here, until he comes back."_

**She means to stay, and the Star seal stays with her.** The way home is a persuasion under siege.

**Persuade her.** Run an opposed Influence test against her Persistence 50. One PC leads; help gives +1B in total. Apply these modifiers, then cancel and cap at +3B or -3P:

| Factor                                                                       | Dice     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Each letter delivered tonight                                                | +1B each |
| Each letter on her route left undelivered                                    | -1P each |
| **Ilen**, or any courier, speaks to her as a fellow courier                  | +1B      |
| Someone promises to deliver her letter to Tam, and means it                  | +1B      |
| Mirelle leads the Star Lamp's rite for the dead: "Star guides the dead home" | +1B      |
| Anyone has grabbed the satchel or threatened her                             | -1P      |

**Or give her new orders.** Pebb can draft a proper Errand order at the gallery's courier desk, with real forms and sealing wax: _"Courier Vael: deliver the Last Wagon Company to the Ninth Milestone."_ Run an opposed test of Deception against her Perception 40, at +1B for the genuine materials. She knows it is not from her emperor, but she wants orders more than she wants the truth.

**If she agrees:** _"One last road. The Star seal takes a courier home."_ The circle lights and the gate leaves at the end of the round. Everyone inside goes; anyone outside stays in the Hall of Measures, to be found by three angry governments.

**If she refuses:** they can try again once per bell. If the third bell has rung and she still refuses, use **the Courier's Last Road**. Any courier holding the satchel inside the circle can speak the Errand's words from the arch inscription and burn the Star seal without her. Ilen is the obvious courier; a PC who has delivered a letter tonight also qualifies. The arrival home is rough, and Aster stays behind in the Hall, kneeling before the empty throne forever. That ending is bittersweet but survivable.

**If she turns hostile,** because someone grabbed the satchel or attacked her, she attacks with Spirit Combat. If she reduces someone to 0 PP, she can possess them with a later Combat Action. A defender who wins with Persistence deals her `1D4` PP. Remind the players that talking is easier.

### Last call

In the round the gate leaves, whether Aster agreed or the third bell forced the Courier's Last Road, put everything on the table at once. Each of these can happen if it fits:

- **Brack** scoops the ash crown into his hat. It falls through his fingers as dust, and he wails.
- **Sabel** must choose whether to keep the truce or take the satchel to the Castaine door. If the company saved one of her people tonight, she keeps the truce. If not, she tries for the door, and the company must stop her or let the satchel go. Without the satchel, no seal.
- **Mirelle** will not leave until she has finished the rite.
- **Gannet** needs the horses calmed; they hate the dust and the bells.

Then the guards break in, the circle flares, and the gallery is gone.

## Epilogue: the Ninth Milestone at dawn

> _Birdsong. The rain has stopped. Pale sunlight falls through the roofless hall of the Ninth Milestone, and the mosaic circle is just stone again. Aster stands in the arch, clearer than she has ever been. She looks at you the way a courier looks at the next rider in the relay._

If the company took the Courier's Last Road, the arch is empty, and the satchel is only leather and paper now. If she came home willingly, she gives each of them a courier's nod. To whoever delivered the most letters she says, _"The errand is yours now,"_ and fades. The arch cracks and settles into gravel. No one will ever use this waygate again.

**Close what they opened:**

- **The bones** still lie in the vault. Mirelle can say the Synod rite. If the company carried Mother Rhull's acorn, they plant it in the hall above her: _where the courier died._ One day the Ninth Milestone will have an oak growing through its roof.
- **Tam's letter:** Tam Vael's grave is in the Synod burial ground at Vellbridge. Delivering the letter there takes ten minutes of play and is worth it.
- **The remaining letters,** to the Saffron Reach, the Wester Capes, the Pearlwater and the Salt Coast. Brack wants to sell them. The company can let him, buy them, or put them somewhere safe: the Synod, the Crown Survey at Third Measure Hall, or the March Council. The Salt Coast letter is addressed to "the Steward of the Salt Coast at Vellbridge". Who holds that office now is a fine argument to leave unresolved.
- **The Gilded Spur:** if the truce held, Sabel shakes hands and calls the satchel debt settled. If Tarn swore his new oath, he heads east.
- **Crownseat:** within days, rumour reaches Vellbridge that the Hall's bells rang, and every claimant calls it an omen. Someone may have seen faces at the grille. Nobody knows whose.

**Rewards:** Brack pays 50 SP each on arrival, if he gets home with something to sell. Each delivered letter makes a friend: the Verdant Court's thanks; the gratitude of the Compact and the Warrens, or of one of them; a Banner token that guarantees hospitality at any Banner yard. Award **4 IP** each, or **5** if everyone came home and Aster was at peace. Award one Hero Point to anyone who went back for someone.

## Where it can lead

- **The Banner's debt.** Urza's court may summon the company as witnesses when the Banner presses its ninety-three-year claim against the claimant houses.
- **Castaine's interest.** Castaine's archivist knows the company handled coronation-night documents.
- **What Aster saw.** "Every road refused the Crown in the same breath." For a Seven Bells campaign, this is an early clue to the coronation's failed override. See [running notes](04-running-notes.md#using-it-with-seven-bells-for-no-king).
- **The other dead stations.** Somewhere a fourth letter is addressed to a steward of the Pearlwater who may still exist.
