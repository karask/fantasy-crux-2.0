# Twelve Below

A four-hour convention one-shot for **Fantasy Crux 2.0 Beta**. A flooding mine, twelve trapped miners, a clock everybody can see, and something old and hungry in the dark. Players need to know nothing about the setting, and nothing about the rules beyond what the GM explains in five minutes.

**Players:** three to six; four is ideal. **Length:** one four-hour slot, including a break. **Characters:** the [Last Wagon Company](../../pregenerated-characters/README.md) pregens. **Rules:** the full rules or [Fantasy Crux Lite](../../player-handbook/lite/README.md); every number is the same. **Status:** complete scenario, NPCs, handouts and maps; not yet playtested.

> _Twelve miners are trapped below. The water reaches the top at dawn. Something else is down there._

The company's wagon reaches the mining village of Thornwick just as the mine bell rings the alarm. The night shift is trapped, the pump is crippled, and every trip of the lift cage lets the water rise faster. Level by level, the company frees the cage, repairs the pump underwater, swims a flooded chamber and pushes through a tunnel full of explosive gas. All the while, something answers whenever the bells ring three times.

At the bottom of it all is a sixty-year-old bargain, a mine owner's written order, and a choice at dawn about who pays for it.

But the cage rings three bells every time it goes down, and something deep in the dark has learned what three bells mean. A tense rescue horror with a visible clock, hard choices and a lot of water. No knowledge of the game or its setting is needed.
>
> _Content: drowning, confined spaces, darkness, injury, a child in danger, animals in danger._

## Why it plays well

- **A clock everyone can see.** Twelve boxes of water on the table, and twelve tokens on the map. Every decision costs boxes, and the players do the arithmetic themselves.
- **The cage is the puzzle.** It carries the injured faster than any ladder, but every trip lets the water rise, and every trip down rings three bells.
- **The monster is a mystery first.** Claw marks, a glimpse, a wake in the water. By the time Old Wat explains the goats, the players have half worked it out.
- **Fire is the answer, and fire is forbidden.** The troll's wounds close unless they are burned, and the deepest tunnel explodes at a naked flame.
- **The company's own promise is the stakes.** Nobody gets left behind: not the boy hiding in the ore tub, not the old man who cannot climb, not the pony.
- **It ends with a choice.** Read the owner's order to the village, hand it to his daughter, or sell it back to him. Feed the troll, or kill it and let the owner have his silver.
- **Failure costs time, water or blood, never the game.** Every scene has fallbacks, and every rescue has more than one way out.

## Preparation

1. Read [the adventure](01-adventure.md) and [the people](02-npcs.md), about an hour. The [running notes](04-running-notes.md) are your table reference.
2. Print the [player map](map/player-map.svg) as large as you can, and the [handouts](03-players-and-handouts.md): the bell code, the ledger, the brass plate, the bill, the Water track and the twelve tokens.
3. Print the pregens; Dori, Tamsin, Orren and Pebb are recommended for four players. See [who shines where](04-running-notes.md#who-shines-where). Print a copy of _How to play_ for each player.
4. Bring a bell. A hand-bell is best; a spoon and a glass will do. Bring D100 dice and counters for Hero Points.

To redraw the maps after an edit, run `node adventures/twelve-below/map/build-maps.mjs` from the repository root.
