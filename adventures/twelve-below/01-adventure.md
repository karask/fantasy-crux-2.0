# Twelve Below

**GM only.** A four-hour convention adventure for three to six players, beginners welcome. Nobody needs to know the setting. Read this file and [the people](02-npcs.md). Print [the handouts](03-players-and-handouts.md) and [the player map](map/player-map.svg), and keep [the GM map](map/gm-map.svg) behind your screen.

## The adventure in a minute

At nightfall the company's wagon rolls into **Thornwick**, a mining village in the Ironspine foothills, just as the mine bell starts ringing the alarm. **Twelve miners** of the night shift are trapped in the Thornwick Deep. The great pump is crippled, and by dawn the water will fill the mine to its top level.

The company goes down. Level by level they free a jammed lift cage, carry out the injured, dive to repair the pump, cross a flooded chamber to reach a stranded group, and push through a gas-filled tunnel to the deepest seven. Every trip of the cage stops the pump, and the water keeps rising.

Something else is down there. A **troll** has lived in the sealed old workings for sixty years, and it is starving. It comes whenever the bells ring three times. Its wounds close unless they are burned, and the deep tunnel is full of gas that explodes at a naked flame.

The truth is simple and shameful. For sixty years the Dask family kept the troll fed: one goat on the first of every month, lowered through an iron grate on the hillside after three rings of a bell. The new owner, **Corvin Dask**, stopped the goats to save money. Then he ordered the night shift to break into the troll's old workings for their silver. His daughter is among the twelve.

The night ends at dawn at the pithead. The company counts who came up, decides what to do with Dask's written order, and decides what happens to the hungry thing below.

## GM truth

Sixty years ago, Thornwick's miners broke into natural caverns full of older workings: **the Old Seam**. A troll lived there. It killed two men before old **Aldo Dask** drove it back with fire and then made a bargain with it. He walled the Old Seam off. On the first of every month his family lowered a goat down the **Goat Grate**, a narrow barred shaft on the hillside above the troll's den, and rang a hand-bell three times. The troll learned that **three bells mean food**. It kept to its caves, and the mine prospered.

The Dasks never talked about the pact. Old miners still tell it as a story for children: _"First of the month, one goat, three bells."_

**Corvin Dask** inherited the mine and its debts. He never believed the story. At midsummer, three months ago, he cancelled the goats. Last week a surveyor told him the richest silver in the hill lies behind the old wall. Tonight, on the first of the month, he gave the night shift a written order and double pay: _break the Old Seam wall._ His daughter **Linnet**, an apprentice overseer, went down with them and wrote the order into the shift ledger.

An hour into the shift, the wall came down. The Old Seam's trapped water burst into Level 3. **Firedamp**, a gas that explodes at a naked flame, seeped into its tunnel. The surge rushed up through the old workings and dropped a beam on the pump's valve at Level 2. Then the troll came out of the breach.

The Level 3 crew splashed back to their station and rang nine for help. Hulda, at the engine, lowered the cage. Before every lowering she rings **three**, which is the mine's signal for "cage coming down". The troll heard three bells. As the cage reached Level 3, the troll surged up out of the flooded shaft bottom, expecting a goat. The crew fled the only way they could, back along the tunnel and through a low crawl to the high platform of **the Chapel**. The troll found an empty cage and tore at it. The engine drum kicked back and broke Hulda's arm, and she wound the cage up empty, with claw marks in its iron gate.

The troll then climbed to Level 1, killed Clover the pit mule, threw her down the shaft and climbed down after her. It has eaten. It is not full. Three months of hunger will bring it back to the bell all night.

**What the troll is.** It is not evil. It is old, half-blind, territorial and starving. It sees warm bodies in the dark, holds its breath for twenty rounds, and moves through flooded tunnels with ease. It climbs slowly and cannot fit in the cage or the Chimney. Fed, it goes home. Wounded, it heals unless burned, and it retreats only when badly hurt. It will kill anyone who stands between it and food. See [its profile](02-npcs.md#the-troll).

## The clocks

### The Water

The **Water track** has twelve boxes, and dawn is box 12. Put it on the table beside the map, where everyone can see it. Fill **one** box when:

- **a scene below ground begins** (Scenes 2, 3, 4 and 5). This covers the company's own trip down;
- **the cage makes a round trip,** down and back up, for any other reason: carrying casualties up, sending a goat down, or luring the troll;
- **a long task is done:** bringing one casualty up the ladders by one level, hauling one casualty up the Chimney, retrying a failed repair, digging, resealing, or stopping to rest;
- **the table argues for five real minutes** without acting, at most once a scene. Say so, kindly, and fill the box.

When the gas explodes, fill **two** boxes. When the company repairs the pump, **erase three** boxes, once. The track never drops below zero.

| Water reaches | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Start         | The Level 3 tunnel is knee-deep.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Box 4         | The Level 3 tunnel is waist-deep: `-1P` to physical tests there.                                                                                                                                                      |
| Box 8         | The Level 3 tunnel is flooded to the roof. The Chapel's platform becomes an island. Reaching it now means swimming the whole 60 m tunnel underwater, or the Chimney.                                                  |
| Box 10        | Level 2 floods, with the ledge in the stope. If the pump is not yet repaired, it never will be. The water reaches the Chapel's platform: anyone still there must be on the Chimney, or swim.                          |
| Box 12        | **Dawn.** The water reaches Level 1, and the engine can no longer wind the cage. Anyone at Level 1 climbs out by the shaft ladders. Anyone below is lost, unless they are already escaping (see [Cut off](#cut-off)). |

The old workings below Level 3 swallow the first water slowly. Once they are full, it climbs fast. Say so when the track reaches box 8.

Read the water aloud every time it rises: _"The pump's thud has stopped. Water is chuckling up the ladderway."_ The players should hear the clock.

### The cage and the ladders

The cage hangs in the shaft, and Hulda winds it from the engine house. It holds **four people.** A stretcher or the pit pony counts as two. The company's ride down at the start of each scene is free, because the scene's box covers it. Any other round trip costs a box.

**Ladders** run up the shaft beside the cage in short flights, with a wooden landing every 6 m. Anyone who can climb uses them without spending water. Climbing needs no test unless the climber is hurried, burdened or attacked. Then a failed Athletics test is a slip to the landing below: `2D6` damage, ignoring armour.

**Casualties** are the people who cannot climb: Brask with his crushed leg; Della with her broken arm; Corrie, unless someone wakes her; and Old Wat, who needs a shoulder on every rung. Bringing a casualty up the ladders by one level is a long task. Biscuit the pony cannot climb at all: the cage or nothing.

That trade-off is the real puzzle of the night. Let the players do the arithmetic.

### The bells

One wire runs down the shaft. Pull it at any station and every bell in the mine rings, while Hulda's board in the engine house shows which station pulled. Give the players [the bell code](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-2-the-bell-code): **1** stop, **2** raise the cage, **3** lower the cage, **5** read the slate in the cage, **9** emergency.

**Three bells call the troll.** Every time the cage goes down, the bells ring three, whoever asked for it. The troll then comes to the station where the cage stops, arriving `1D6` rounds after it, expecting food. If it finds none, it takes the nearest warm body it can reach.

- If the troll has been driven off during this scene, it does not answer again until the next scene.
- Once fed, or walled back into the Old Seam, it stops answering.

Do not explain any of this. Let it happen twice, let Old Wat tell his story, and let the players work it out. Once they do, the bell becomes a tool to lure the troll, to call it into a trap, or to feed it.

## Keep it to four hours

| Table time | Scene                                         | Purpose                                                                |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–0:15  | Welcome, characters and rules in five minutes | Pregens, [the rules script](04-running-notes.md#rules-in-five-minutes) |
| 0:15–0:35  | 1. The Pithead                                | The alarm, the job, the gear, the map and the bell code                |
| 0:35–1:00  | 2. The Upper Drift                            | The jammed cage, the first rescue, the first glimpse                   |
| 1:00–1:40  | 3. The Pump Gallery                           | The pump repair, the flooded stope, the troll in the water             |
| 1:40–1:50  | Break                                         | Leave the Water track on the table                                     |
| 1:50–2:45  | 4. The Deep                                   | Gas, the Chapel, the truth and the troll in the dark                   |
| 2:45–3:30  | 5. The Rising                                 | Getting everyone up                                                    |
| 3:30–3:45  | Dawn                                          | The count, Dask's reckoning and the pact                               |
| 3:45–4:00  | Reserve                                       |                                                                        |

These are ceilings. A fast table banks time for the Deep. **Scenes 3 and 4 can be played in either order.** If the company goes straight to the Deep, play Scene 3 on the way back up, and remind the players that box 10 floods the stope.

**If running late:** at 1:40, if the pump is not repaired, Ama shouts the trick across the water and the next attempt gets `+1B`. At 2:45, if the troll is still a problem, it takes whatever food is nearest and goes home. At 3:15, skip to the last trip up. Always keep fifteen minutes for Dawn.

**Keep them on edge:**

- **End every scene on an alarm,** such as the cage lurching, a scream in the water, or a lamp flame turning blue.
- **Keep the tokens in sight.** Twelve miner tokens and a pony stand on the map. Move them as people are found and brought up. The players will count them all night.
- **Telegraph the troll before it strikes:** claw marks, a torn harness, a wake against the current, a stink of wet animal. Then strike.
- **Never let failure stop the game.** A failed test costs time, water or blood, and the story moves on.

## Scene 1: The Pithead

**Purpose:** a fast start; the job, the gear and the clocks.

> _Dusk, a cold wind, the smell of coal smoke. Your wagon of mine stores is just rolling into Thornwick when the mine bell starts to ring and does not stop: nine strokes, again and again. Up the hill, the engine house is shrieking steam, and brown water is pouring out of the drainage tunnel. People are running towards the pithead, and somebody is shouting names._

The company is hauling a delivery for the mine: lamp oil, rope and pit timber. Ask each player one of these: _What were you looking forward to tonight? Who in the company would you trust with your life down a hole?_

### The captain

**Hulda Brannoch**, the dwarf mine captain, is at the engine with her right arm splinted against her chest. She will not leave the engine. Give the players [the player map](map/player-map.svg) and [the bell code](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-2-the-bell-code) as she talks:

> _"Twelve below. The night shift. Level 3 flooded an hour ago. The pump's crippled: something's jammed the valve down on Level 2. It's lifting a third of what it should, and it lifts nothing while I wind the cage. Water reaches Level 1 by dawn."_

Asked what happened to her arm, she nods at the cage:

> _"Level 3 rang nine. I sent the cage down for them. Something pulled it. Pulled it! The drum kicked like a mule and broke my arm. When I got it wound up, it was empty. Look at it."_

The cage stands at the pithead. Four deep gouges score its iron gate, and the bars are bent outward. It stinks of wet animal, and long grey hairs are snagged in the hinges. Hulda has no explanation. Somebody in the crowd starts muttering about old stories and is hushed.

She warns the company about **firedamp**:

> _"Down deep, the rock breathes gas. If your lamp flame goes tall and blue, no fire. No torch, no flint, no pipe. Or you'll blow the whole Deep, and yourselves with it."_

### The owner

**Corvin Dask** pushes through the crowd in a good coat, pale and shaking.

> _"A hundred silver each. Bring them up. Bring my daughter up. Linnet. She's down there. Please."_

He means every word about Linnet. Asked what happened, he lies: _"The old workings gave way. Nobody could have known."_ A Perception test opposed by his Deception 60 shows that he is lying. Hulda's face says the same. If anyone presses her, she says it quietly: _"The night shift had orders from him to break the Old Seam wall. Written orders. Linnet keeps the ledger."_

### Gear

Hulda's lamp room and the company's own wagon hold everything the players reasonably ask for. Suggest:

- **a safety lamp** for each person. Its flame burns behind glass and wire gauze, lights 3 m like a lantern, and cannot set off gas. In firedamp the flame stretches tall and blue: that is the warning;
- **two torches** and **four flasks of lamp oil.** These are naked flames, deadly in gas and deadly to a troll;
- **60 m of rope,** a crowbar, a pick, two stretchers, splints and a Healing Kit;
- **a slate and chalk** for messages sent in the cage.

**The lamp room** opens off Dask's office. Anyone who looks around while the others gear up sees three things that mean nothing yet:

- a **brass plate**, recently unscrewed from the lamp-room wall, lying face-down on Dask's desk ([Handout 4](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-4-the-brass-plate));
- an iron **key labelled GRATE**, on the hook where the plate used to hang;
- a goat-dealer's bill with a line through it ([Handout 5](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-5-the-goat-dealers-bill)).

**In the yard,** a nanny goat is tethered by the lamp-room door, with a label tied to her collar: _W. FENNER. NOT FOR SALE._ Mention her once and no more.

When the company steps into the cage or onto the ladder, move to Scene 2.

## Scene 2: The Upper Drift

**Level 1, 40 m down. Purpose:** the cage or the ladders; the first rescue; the first glimpse. Fill one box as the scene begins.

> _Hulda rings three to lower you. The shaft swallows the last of the daylight. Water drips everywhere. Then the cage judders, shrieks against something, and stops: hanging six metres above the lamplit Level 1 station, swaying._

**The jam.** The kick of the drum cracked a guide timber at Level 1, and it now juts across the shaft. Someone must climb out onto the ladder beside the cage and free it: an Athletics test to break it loose (brute force), or a Mechanisms test to lever it clear. The timber is person-sized, so Nerin's measured shove moves it. Each failure costs a long task. Until the timber is cleared, the cage cannot pass Level 1 in either direction.

**The glimpse.** While the cage hangs there, the troll answers the three bells. Anyone with heat sight sees a huge warm shape come up out of the ladderway onto the station below. Everyone else hears heavy, wet footfalls, smells wet animal, and sees a lamp on the station go out. It sniffs. It looks up. It reaches towards the cage, and cannot reach. Then it slides back down the ladderway and is gone. It has eaten recently. Next time, it will not leave.

**The Upper Drift** runs east from the station: wet timber and darkness beyond the lamps.

- **Brask Ironjaw**, an orc miner, sits against the wall with his leg crushed under a fallen prop. He is Wounded and cannot walk. He jokes through the pain and is heavy: two people or the cage.
- **Hobb Lane**, sixteen, is hiding in an empty ore tub and will not come out: _"It took Clover. It took her like a sack, and it threw her down the shaft."_ Talk him out with an Influence test opposed by his Persistence 30, or with a kind, practical argument and no roll.

**First signs.** In the mule stall by the station: blood, a torn harness, and drag marks to the shaft. Deep claw marks in the station timbers. Prints in the mud: bare, five-toed, longer than a forearm, heavy enough to crack the boards. Seris can read them with Natural Lore: _one animal, very big, old, and in no hurry._

**The Chimney.** At the far end of the drift, an old boarded door breathes cold air. It is the top of the Chimney, the old air raise that runs down to the Chapel. Brask knows it: _"Rotten rungs. Nobody's used it in thirty years."_

**Getting them up.** The players can ring 2 and send Brask and Hobb up in the cage (a box), carry Brask up the ladders (a long task), or leave them for later. Hobb will not stay alone.

**End the scene** on a decision: _down to Level 2, by cage or ladder?_ If the cage came back down to Level 1 for them, three bells have rung again: end the scene with heavy footfalls climbing the ladders below.

## Scene 3: The Pump Gallery

**Level 2, 80 m down. Purpose:** a hard-won victory, a rescue across the water, and the troll revealed. Fill one box as the scene begins.

> _Level 2 sounds like a waterfall. To the west, the pump gallery: great iron rods vanish into a churning pool. To the east, the drift opens into a flooded chamber, and from somewhere across the water, a pony screams._

### The pump

The **pump gallery** is waist-deep. A fallen beam has pinned the pump rods and cracked the **clack valve**, the iron flap that keeps the water moving up. The repair takes two steps:

1. **Shift the beam:** Athletics (brute force); helpers can give `+1B`. The beam is person-sized, so Nerin's measured shove moves it.
2. **Fit the spare valve,** which hangs on the gallery wall: Mechanisms at `-1P`, working underwater. Each attempt is one long dive. Nobody needs a breath test unless something grabs or pins them. Dori, Nerin, and Ama across the water all know how.

Up in the engine house, Hulda hears the rods change their beat and puts full steam to the pump. Success **erases three boxes.** The roar in the gallery turns into a steady _thud, thud, thud_, and the water begins to fall. Make it feel like a win. It is the only clear win the night gives.

### The flooded stope

East of the station, the drift opens into **the stope**, a tall worked-out chamber flooded twenty metres across. Its drowned floor opens into the old workings below, and the troll swims up through it. On a ledge across the water:

- **Ama Tuck** and **Ketter Tuck**, goblin sister and brother. Ama is the pump fitter's mate, quick and sharp-tongued. Ketter cannot swim and says he can.
- **Rolf Barrow**, a big man, soaked and shivering.
- **Biscuit**, the pit pony, up to her knees and terrified.

**Getting across:** swim (Athletics, because the water is rough; armour and heavy loads add `-1P` each), string a rope for others to pull themselves along, or float an empty **ore tub** from the drift with two people aboard. Biscuit swims if someone leads her; a frightened pony needs a Riding or Natural Lore test to calm her first.

### The troll in the water

Telegraph it: a wake moving against the current, bubbles, a stink of wet animal, and Biscuit going rigid with fear.

Then it surfaces beside the swimmers or the tub: huge and grey-green, with moss in its hair and eyes milky with age. Across its shoulders run old burn scars, puckered and white: the only wounds it carries. It grabs for the nearest warm body. Play one or two rounds. The players should learn three things:

- **It is strong.** A claw deals `1D6 + 2D6`, and for Parry the claw counts as Heavy.
- **It heals.** Show a wound closing at the start of its turn.
- **Fire hurts it.** There is no gas on Level 2. A torch or a flask of burning oil works, and burned wounds stay open. It shrieks.

Once it is down to half its HP (11 or less), or has taken any fire, it dives and withdraws into the flooded dark. It will be back.

If the troll seizes a swimmer, it holds them under: see [the troll in rules terms](04-running-notes.md#the-troll-in-rules-terms). Make it a rescue problem, not a death sentence.

**End the scene** with Level 2's people safe or on the move, and the next question: _the Deep, by cage, ladder or Chimney?_

## Scene 4: The Deep

**Level 3, 120 m down. Purpose:** the gas puzzle, the truth, and the troll where fire is forbidden. Fill one box as the scene begins.

> _The Deep is cold, and silent except for water. Your lamps show a flooded tunnel, its timbers sagging overhead. Then the flame in the first lamp stretches tall, and turns blue._

Check the Water track for the depth: knee-deep at first, waist-deep from box 4.

### The gas

The **Level 3 station** gets fresh air down the shaft. Fire is safe there. From 10 m along the tunnel to the far end of the Chapel, the air is full of firedamp, and every safety lamp burns blue.

**Any naked flame in the gas explodes it:** a torch, burning oil, a struck flint. Everyone in the gas zone takes `3D6` damage, ignoring armour, and falls prone. At the station, or more than 10 m up the Chimney, people are knocked about but unhurt. The timbers at the crawl come down, and only the Chimney or an hour's digging (a long task) reaches the Chapel. Fill **two** boxes. The blast burns the gas away, so fire is safe on Level 3 for the rest of the night.

Warn the players once, clearly. Hulda already did. After that, it is their choice.

### The tunnel

The tunnel runs 60 m from the station to the Chapel. The gas begins 10 m in.

**The breach,** 25 m in. In the tunnel wall, the broken seal: a ragged hole into blackness, breathing cold air and the stink of animal. A rotten board in the water reads _SEALED BY ORDER OF A. DASK._ Huge prints go in and out.

**The crawl,** 40 m in. Fallen timbers leave a gap low enough to crawl through. Dori's Earth Sense says the roof beyond is sound. Passing a stretcher through the gap takes an Athletics test, or counts as a long task. The troll is far too big for it. To get through, it must tear the timbers apart, which takes it two rounds and makes a terrible noise. The crawl is why the seven in the Chapel are still alive.

### The Chapel

At the tunnel's end, a tall stope rises above the water: **the Chapel**, named for its arched roof and the little shrine to the Hammer, the lamp of honest work, cut into its wall. The shrine's candle is cold; the miners pinched it out when the lamps went blue. Seven people huddle on a timbered platform above the flood. They have breathed bad air for hours and are all **Fatigued**.

- **Linnet Dask**, nineteen, the owner's daughter and an apprentice overseer. Fierce, frightened and furious. She carries **the shift ledger** with her father's order in it ([Handout 3](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-3-the-shift-ledger)).
- **Old Wat Fenner**, sixty-four, coughing, with forty-nine years in the mine.
- **Tolly Fenner**, Wat's grandson, who has not let go of his grandfather's arm all night.
- **Sprat**, a twelve-year-old goblin door-boy. His heat sight sees the troll before anyone: _"It's warm. It's big. It came up out of the water when the cage came."_
- **Della Marsh**, with a splinted broken arm. She is Wounded, not Bleeding.
- **Corrie Stone**, unconscious from the bad air. A Healing test wakes her, and then she can walk.
- **Nan Gorrow**, an orc miner, strong and steady, who will carry whatever she is asked to.

The Chimney starts here, behind the Hammer shrine. A faint, cold draught breathes down it: the only good air the seven have had all night.

### The truth

Old Wat tells it if asked what is down here, or the moment the bells ring three:

> _"Three bells. Hammer keep us, that's the goat bell. When I was a boy, old Master Aldo fed the thing in the Old Seam. First of the month, one goat, three bells. Young Master Corvin stopped the goats at midsummer. Said it was a tale for children. I bought a goat myself, I did. She's tied up in the yard. Meant to do it tonight, after shift. But he's got the key to the grate."_

Linnet goes white. She did not know about the goats. She did know about the order, and argued against it, and went down anyway, because her father said she was the only one he trusted.

Now the players understand the bells, the troll and the owner. If they have not seen [the brass plate](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-4-the-brass-plate), describe it.

### The troll comes

It comes through the breach while the company is in the tunnel or the Chapel, or soon after anyone rings three. It wants food. It goes for the weakest, or for Biscuit if she is here. **In the gas, fire means an explosion.** That is the puzzle. Ways through:

- **Feed it.** Write _SEND THE GOAT_ on the slate and ring 5. Hulda sends Wat's goat down in the cage, which costs a box and rings three. The troll takes the goat, calms, and carries it back through the breach to eat. A miner's lunch will not do; a whole goat will. So would Biscuit, a terrible choice the table will remember for years.
- **Lure it.** The cage rings three whenever it goes down. Have Hulda lower it, empty, to Level 2, or bring it down to the Level 3 station, where fire is safe. Each lure is a round trip and costs a box.
- **Trap it.** Bring the crawl's timbers down when the troll is on the far side. That is Dori's or Nerin's moment. It holds the troll for a while, but it will dig through.
- **Hide from it.** Its eyes are failing and it hunts by heat. A person lying still under thick, cold mud or soaked sacking counts as heavily insulated, and its heat sight misses them. It still has Perception 20 for sound and smell.
- **Fight it.** Without fire it regenerates `1D6` at the start of each of its turns, even at 0 HP. Magic hurts it but does not stop the healing, so Nerin's force bolts are welcome but not decisive. If it drops to 0 HP, it gets up on its next turn and flees.
- **Blow it up.** Clear everyone out of the gas, lure the troll into the tunnel, and throw a torch from the station. The blast counts as fire, so it does not regenerate on its next turn. It flees, broken, back into the Old Seam. It costs two boxes and the crawl. It is heroic, reckless and memorable.
- **Kill it.** Drop it to 0 HP and keep it burning, with a torch held to it or burning oil, so that it never regenerates. It dies after three rounds. This can only happen where fire is safe.

### Resealing the breach

Once the troll is fed and back in the Old Seam, the company can wall the breach up again. They haul timber from the crawl (Athletics) and set it (Craft (Mason) or Mechanisms). It is a long task, and Dori shines here. A troll with a full belly, walled in, will not come back tonight. It only needs its goat next month.

### Getting them out

Seven people, three of whom cannot climb. They can go up in the cage, in two trips that cost two boxes, or by **the Chimney**:

- each climber tests Athletics once on the way up; a rope belay gives `+1B`. A failure means a slip to a landing (`2D6`, ignoring armour), stuck until helped;
- each person who cannot climb is hauled up on ropes: a long task each;
- Nerin's _hold to the wall_ lets one climber climb the rock itself instead of the rotten rungs, with no test unless something else makes the climb hard;
- everyone who climbs the whole Chimney makes a Fatigue test at the top.

The Chimney comes out at the far end of Level 1, where the cage and the ladders take over. The troll cannot follow anyone up it.

**End the scene** as the last group leaves the Chapel, with the Water track in front of the players.

## Scene 5: The Rising

**Purpose:** the race to the top. Fill one box as the scene begins.

Put every token on the map. Whoever is not yet at the surface has to get there before box 12. Use these beats as the board demands:

- **The cage stalls between levels.** The rope has slipped on the drum, and Hulda needs two minutes with one good arm. The people inside wait in the swaying dark, or climb out onto the ladders. Ilen's moment.
- **The troll follows,** if it was never fed or walled in. It climbs slowly up the ladders, and it reaches the last group at a station just as the water does.
- **The troll comes up.** If it follows the company to the surface, the pithead has light, open air and fifty frightened people with torches. Fire is free here. It is the place to finish it.
- **Someone is missing.** Hobb has run back for Brask's lucky pick, or Sprat has gone back for Biscuit. _Nobody gets left behind._ Somebody goes back.
- **Dask comes down.** If the Water reaches box 10 with Linnet still below, Corvin Dask rides the cage down himself, with Wat's goat in his arms if she is still in the yard. He can be a burden or a redemption. Facing the troll with the goat in his arms is his last chance to do the right thing. What he deserves is the players' call at dawn.

### Cut off

Nobody drowns because of one bad roll. When the water cuts people off, they still have ways out:

- **The shaft ladders** work for anyone who can climb and can reach a station.
- **The Chimney** works from the Chapel until box 12. After that, its top is underwater.
- **The troll's way.** If the troll has been fed and is calm, it has gone home through the Old Seam to its dry den under the Goat Grate. Anyone brave enough can follow: swim 10 m underwater through the breach (holding breath; a guide rope helps everyone through), climb the dry caves past the sleeping troll, and force the grate's hatch from below (Athletics; the crowbar gives `+1B`, and the GRATE key opens it from outside). They come out on the hillside at dawn. The troll does not stop them. It has its goat.

Anyone who cannot get out by any of these ways is lost. Count it honestly. The players should feel the weight of every token.

## Dawn

> _Grey light over the spoil heaps. The engine house has stopped shrieking. Families crowd the pithead as the last of you climb out, black with mud and soaked to the bone. Somebody starts counting heads aloud._

**Count the tokens** with the players: twelve miners and one pony. Let the crowd call every rescued name.

**The reckoning.** The ledger is the company's to use:

- **Read it aloud** at the pithead. The miners turn on Dask, and Hulda backs the company. If the company asks, the village makes Dask pay compensation out of the silver he wanted.
- **Give it to Linnet.** She confronts her father in private, and the mine will run differently from now on.
- **Keep it.** Dask pays well for silence. That is legal, and ugly.

**The pact.** Somebody must feed the troll on the first of every month, or it will come back. If it lives, Linnet takes the GRATE key: _"One goat, three bells. I'll ring them myself."_ If the company lets her, she does it at once, with Wat's goat. Down in the dark, something takes it. It does not come up again.

If the troll is dead, Thornwick is safe and the Old Seam's silver belongs to Dask after all. Ask the players whether that feels like a victory.

**Rewards.** If Linnet came up alive, Dask pays the promised 100 SP each, with the miners watching. The haulage fee is 20 SP each. Award **4 IP** each, or **5** if all twelve came up. During play, give a Hero Point on the spot to anyone who goes back for someone at real risk, once per character.
