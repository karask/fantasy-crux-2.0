# Lullaby for a Dragon

**GM only.** A four-hour convention heist for three to six players, beginners welcome. Nobody needs to know the setting. Read this file and [the people](02-npcs.md). Print [the handouts](03-players-and-handouts.md) and keep [the GM map](map/gm-map.svg) behind your screen.

## The adventure in a minute

A collector hires the company to steal a golden music-cylinder from old imperial works sealed under the **Warm Hill**, a vineyard in the Crown Vale. The doors of the works open only to a song: the harvest song the vineyard family sings at their grape-treading festival. So the company arrives at the festival with a wagon of wine and a secret job. They have to learn every verse, slip past the family, and go under the hill at midnight.

Under the hill they find a great imperial music machine, still playing the same tune, and they find out why. A **dragon** has slept beneath the vineyard for two hundred years, held asleep by the machine's lullaby. The golden cylinder they came to steal is what keeps her asleep.

Then a rival thief, the family's estranged son, takes the cylinder anyway. The music stops. The hill starts to shake.

The finale is a chase, a fight on slippery rocks, a race to put the music back, and the whole company singing a harvest song to a waking dragon. If she wakes anyway, they will have to talk to her. She is very old, very hungry, and has a grievance two hundred years long.

## GM truth

### Vermeil

**Vermeil** is a red-gold dragon, older than the empire that used her. Two centuries ago an emperor sent her to burn a rebel city. Afterwards she was tired, and he promised her one night's sleep before she could fly free again. His Shapers built **the Lullaby** under the Warm Hill to sing her to sleep. They never let it stop. For the empire she was a weapon in storage, to be woken when it needed her.

She has slept on her hoard under the hill ever since. Her warmth rises through the rock and ripens the grapes weeks early, and that is why Warm Hill wine is the best in the Vale. Nobody living knows she is there.

### The Lullaby

The Lullaby is an organ of brass pipes and bellows in a cavern under the hill. A waterwheel on an underground stream drives it. At its heart turns **the Golden Score**, a gilded brass cylinder a metre long, studded with pins, that plays the lullaby over and over. The Score is also the anchor of the enchantment: while it turns in its cradle, she cannot wake. Take it out and the spell ends. The music stops, and she begins to wake.

The keepers also made a practice copy, **the Silver Score**. It plays the tune, but it carries no enchantment, and it was never finished: the last verse is missing. Years ago Vermeil took it in her sleep, as she takes all bright things. It lies in her hoard, under her right forefoot.

### The Vasse family

The engine had keepers, and the last of them was **Aubin Vasse**. When the empire fell, ninety-three years ago, the oil and brass and wages stopped coming. Aubin kept the Lullaby turning alone for another twenty years. When he grew too old for the stair, he taught his family the lullaby as a harvest song, with all four verses, and told them it was for the grapes. Then he sealed the works. He died three years later.

His great-granddaughter **Odile Vasse** runs the vineyard now. His granddaughter, **Gran Perrine**, is eighty-four. She is the last person who knows the fourth verse, and she sings it at midnight at the end of every harvest festival. The family thinks the song is tradition. Nobody living remembers what it is for.

Seventy years without a keeper have worn the Lullaby down. It misses notes. When it does, the hill "turns in her sleep": the ground trembles and the vineyard's casks crack. Every year the tremors come more often.

### Tonight

Odile's elder son, **Lucan Vasse**, walked out fourteen years ago after a fight with his mother. He called the hill-song "peasant superstition" and refused to learn the verses. Now he is a professional thief for **Lady Isaura Harrow**, a collector of imperial masterpieces. Lucan remembered his great-great-grandfather's "golden music drum under the hill" and sold the story to her.

Harrow wants the Score. She does not believe in dragons. Lucan cannot open the song-locked doors and cannot go near his family, so Harrow has hired the company to open the way. Tonight is the **Crush**, the grape-treading festival, the one night when strangers are welcome on the estate. Lucan and two hirelings will follow the company down the stair through every door it opens. For the way out, Lucan has cut through the old iron grate where the underground stream leaves the hill at the Warm Spring: he swam that cave as a boy. His orders are to take the Score once the doors are open and to pay the company nothing.

## The clocks

### The Waking

The **Waking track** has ten boxes. When it is full, Vermeil wakes. Put it on the table when the company enters the hill, where everyone can see it. Fill **one** box when:

- **a scene inside the hill begins** (Scenes 3, 4 and 5) while the Lullaby is unrepaired. The engine misses a note;
- **something loud happens** under the hill: a fight breaks out (one box, and one more if it lasts past three rounds), someone shouts, a door is forced, or something heavy is dropped;
- **a stealth test fails** in the hoard cavern;
- **anyone takes anything** from the hoard, once per item or handful;
- **the Golden Score is not turning at the start of a beat** in the finale (see [Beats](#beats)), unless someone is singing the fourth verse to her.

**Erase** boxes when:

- **the Lullaby is repaired:** erase two boxes, once;
- **someone sings her the lullaby** and succeeds at Performance: erase one box, or two on a Critical. See [the song](#the-song).

| Waking reaches | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Box 4          | **Dreaming.** She talks in her sleep: from now on, read the next [dream line](02-npcs.md#what-she-says-in-her-sleep) each time a box fills. The hill trembles. `1D3` dream-sparks spill out of the rock near the loudest noise.                                                                          |
| Box 7          | **Stirring.** Her eyes move under their lids. The hoard slides and the air shimmers with heat. Each time a box fills from now on, she sighs flame at the loudest place in the hoard: `2D6` fire damage, with a Dodge at `-1P` to halve it; armour applies. In the finale she acts once per beat instead. |
| Box 10         | **Awake.** See [If she wakes](#if-she-wakes). There is no fight to win.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

She sinks back into deep sleep when the track is at 3 or less **and** the Golden Score is turning, or the Silver Score is turning and someone has promised to sing to her every harvest (see [Dawn](#dawn)).

Say the track aloud when it moves: _"Somewhere under your feet, something enormous breathes out."_

### Quiet

Under the hill, whispering, walking and careful work make no noise worth counting. **Singing the lullaby never fills a box.** It is her song, and the deeper she hears it, the deeper she sleeps. Everything else that is loud costs a box. Tell the players that rule plainly the first time they go under, then let them plan around it.

### The song

The players get the Warm Hill Song on the festival songsheet ([Handout 2](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-2-the-warm-hill-song)): the chorus and three verses. The fourth verse ([Handout 3](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-3-the-fourth-verse)) is sung only by Gran Perrine, at midnight. Each verse does something under the hill:

| Verse         | Under the hill                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I. The Water  | Opens the first song-lock, under the tower's beacon.                                                                                                        |
| II. The Stone | Opens the second song-lock, at the foot of the keepers' stair.                                                                                              |
| III. The Fire | Dream-sparks that hear it settle and sink back into the rock. Anyone who sings it holds every spark within 10 m quiet for a scene.                          |
| IV. The Sleep | Opens the third song-lock, into the Lullaby. In the finale, anyone singing it holds the track still for a beat, and singers can try to erase boxes with it. |

**A song-lock** is a brass mouth in a door. Sing the right verse into it and the bolts draw back. That needs no test, only the right words. A song-lock can also be forced with Athletics (brute force) or Mechanisms, both at `-1P`, but forcing is loud: fill a box, success or failure. Nerin can draw the bolts quietly with his measured shove if he can see them through the brass mouth.

**Singing to her:** a singer spends a beat singing and tests Performance. Success erases one box, a Critical two. Apply these modifiers, then cancel and cap as usual:

- `+1B` if the singer learned the fourth verse properly, from Gran Perrine or the family ledger;
- `+1B` if a score is playing in the Lullaby, or the singer holds the keepers' tuning fork;
- `+1B` for a chorus of three or more people singing together;
- `+1B`, at the GM's discretion, if the player actually sings a line at the table.

Gran Perrine sings at 70%. The pregens sing at about 20%, and that is fine: in the finale, **simply singing the fourth verse holds the track still**, with no roll at all. The roll is only for erasing boxes.

## Keep it to four hours

| Table time | Scene                                         | Purpose                                                                |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–0:15  | Welcome, characters and rules in five minutes | Pregens, [the rules script](04-running-notes.md#rules-in-five-minutes) |
| 0:15–0:55  | 1. The Crush                                  | The festival, the song, the family, the way in                         |
| 0:55–1:30  | 2. The Keepers' Stair                         | The tower, the song-locks, the lodge and the keeper's log              |
| 1:30–2:00  | 3. The Lullaby                                | The engine, the truth, the repair, and Lucan                           |
| 2:00–2:10  | Break                                         | Leave the Waking track on the table                                    |
| 2:10–2:45  | 4. The Hoard                                  | A dragon on her gold, the Silver Score under her foot                  |
| 2:45–3:30  | 5. The Waking                                 | The music stops: chase, fight, repair and sing                         |
| 3:30–3:45  | Dawn                                          | The crossroads, the family and the dragon's answer                     |
| 3:45–4:00  | Reserve                                       |                                                                        |

These are ceilings. A fast table banks time for the hoard and the finale.

**If running late:** at 0:55, if the company still lacks the fourth verse, Gran Perrine sings it within their hearing. At 1:30, cut to the third door: the stair is behind them. At 2:45, Lucan takes the Score, wherever the company is. Always keep fifteen minutes for Dawn.

**Keep them on edge:**

- **Make the quiet loud.** Whisper when they whisper. Pause before every footstep in the hoard. Let the players hear their own breathing.
- **Let the hill breathe.** Every few minutes, warm air sighs up through the floor, and the brass in their packs ticks with the heat.
- **Show them she is real before they see her.** Scales the size of shields in the stair. A claw-groove in the rock. A dream spoken aloud through a wall.
- **Never let failure stop the game.** A failed test costs a box, a burn, or time, and the story moves on.

## Scene 1: The Crush

**Purpose:** a joyful start; the job, the song and the family.

> _Dusk on the Warm Hill. The vines run up the slope in long terraces, heavy with dark grapes, and the whole hillside smells of crushed fruit and woodsmoke. Lanterns hang in the rows. In the yard of the old stone press house, villagers stamp barefoot in great vats of purple, laughing, while a band of fiddles and drums plays a tune that everyone seems to know. At the top of the hill, black against the sunset, stands the stump of an old tower. Your wagon is still ticking as it cools. It is a warm evening for so late in the year. Too warm._

The company has driven twelve casks of Lady Harrow's wine to the festival, a gift from her house to the Vasse family. That is the public job. The secret job is in [Handout 1](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-1-lady-harrows-commission). Give it to the players now, along with the [player brief](03-players-and-handouts.md#player-brief-a-gift-of-wine).

Ask each player one quick question: _What is your character doing at the festival before the work starts? Who in the company is enjoying themselves too much?_

### The festival

Make it generous and loud. There is dancing in the yard and treading in the vats. There is a stall of honey cakes, an old fiddler named Bartholomew who takes requests, and a bonfire of vine cuttings. The ground is warm through boot soles. Children dare each other to touch the warm stones at the foot of the terraces.

**The songsheet.** The family hands out printed sheets of the **Warm Hill Song** ([Handout 2](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-2-the-warm-hill-song)). The whole yard sings it after every vat is trodden: the chorus and three verses. Let the players hear it two or three times. The tune is slow and rocking, and it sounds more like a lullaby than a drinking song.

**The tremor.** Halfway through the scene, the music falters and a vat splits. The ground shudders, a few hundred slow heartbeats long. Wine sheets across the yard and the band stops. Then everyone laughs. _"The hill's turning in her sleep,"_ says Odile Vasse, and the band starts again. Old people make a sign with two fingers. It has happened four times this harvest.

### The family

- **Odile Vasse**, the vintner, runs the estate and the festival with a ledger in one hand. She is cool to strangers who ask about the tower. Her elder son sold the family's secrets once, and she has not forgiven anyone for it since.
- **Gran Perrine Vasse**, eighty-four, sits in a carved chair by the bonfire with a cup of the new wine. She is sharp, funny, and nosy, and she dances with anyone handsome who asks. She is the only person who knows the fourth verse.
- **Tobin Vasse**, seventeen, Odile's younger son, guards the path to the tower with two hounds, Pip and Must. He takes the job very seriously, because nobody else does.

### The fourth verse

The company needs it for the third song-lock. Gran Perrine sings it only at midnight, at the **last pressing**, when the family gathers alone at the press-house door and outsiders are sent back to the bonfire. Ways to get it:

- **Charm her.** She loves a dancer, a flatterer, and a new face. An Influence test, with `+1B` for a good dance or a good story, gets her talking. A success gets her to teach the verse "to someone who'll sing it properly" (give Handout 3). A failure gets a lovely story about her grandfather and no verse.
- **Listen at midnight.** Hide near the press-house door: Deception (stealth) opposed by Tobin's Perception, with `-1P` because the hounds are there. Pip and Must love honey cakes.
- **Find the family ledger.** Gran's late husband wrote all four verses in the back of the household ledger, which Odile keeps in the press-house office. Deception to slip in, or Pebb's quick hands.
- **Tell her the truth.** If anyone tells Gran Perrine about the commission and the doors under the hill, she goes very still, then says: _"Grandfather said the hill was asleep, and it must stay asleep. He said it with his hand on my head, like a promise."_ She teaches them the verse and asks to come with them. See [Gran under the hill](04-running-notes.md#gran-under-the-hill).

### Signs

- **A stranger in a hood** watches from the edge of the lantern light and slips away whenever Gran Perrine looks his way. Perception spots him, and Ilen or Pebb can follow him. He is Lucan. If confronted, he is charming and evasive: _"Just an old friend of the family."_ Early in the evening he goes down the lower road to the Warm Spring with a bag of tools and comes back wet to the knees. He has been cutting the grate.
- **The warm stones.** At the foot of the terraces, warm air breathes out of cracks in the rock. Seris or Dori can tell it is not a hot spring: it is dry, and it pulses, slow as breathing.
- **The tower.** It was an imperial signal tower. Locals say the old keepers were the Vasses' ancestors, and that the tower is unlucky. Nobody has climbed it in living memory.

### The way in

At midnight the company has to get past **Tobin and his hounds** on the tower path. They can sneak (Deception against Tobin's Perception, `-1P` for the hounds), distract him (a girl from the village has been trying to dance with him all night), bribe him with festival wine and a good story, or recruit him with the truth.

**End the scene** at the tower door, under the stars, with the festival singing the chorus far below.

## Scene 2: The Keepers' Stair

**Purpose:** wonder, a puzzle, and the truth in an old man's handwriting. The Waking track goes on the table now, at **0**.

> _The tower is a hollow stump of black stone, open to the sky. In the middle of its floor stands a great iron brazier, cold for a century. Beneath it the floor is not stone but bronze: a round plate set with a brass mouth, like a speaking-trumpet. Words are cut around the rim._

The rim reads **SING ME THE WATER**. Singing the Water verse into the brass mouth unlocks the plate, and it swings down on silent hinges. A spiral stair goes down into warm darkness. See [the song](#the-song) for forcing locks.

### The stair

The stair winds down through the hill for a long time. Make the descent feel deep:

- **The warmth.** The rock grows warm, then hot to the touch. Brass in the company's packs ticks as it expands. Dori's Earth Sense says they are a hundred metres down and still going, and says something else that makes no sense: the rock is moving, very slightly, in and out.
- **The scale.** Wedged in a crack, a single scale the size of a shield, red-gold, warm as a loaf. Natural Lore or Seris recognises the shape: not a fish, not a snake, nothing alive should have scales this big. It can be carried out. It is worth a fortune, and it is evidence.
- **The music.** Faint at first, then clearer: a slow tune played on pipes far below. It is the Warm Hill Song.

### The steam gallery

Halfway down, the stair crosses a gallery where hot air vents from the rock in bursts. Anyone who crosses without timing it takes `2D6` damage from scalding steam (metal armour does not help). A Perception test times the bursts and lets a person through safely. Someone who has timed it can talk the others across, and each of them gets `+1B` on their own test.

### The keepers' lodge

At the foot of the stair, behind a plain wooden door, are the keepers' rooms. Six bunks. A long table with plates still on it, under a century of dust. Oil jars, tools, spare brass pipes and a box of lead for patching. Portraits on the wall of keepers in imperial blue. The last portrait is of an old man with a ring of keys at his belt. Anyone who met Gran Perrine sees the same nose and the same eyes.

On the desk lie two things:

- **The keeper's log** ([Handout 4](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-4-the-keepers-log)). Its last entries are Aubin Vasse's. Give it to the players to read aloud. It tells them what is down here.
- **The keepers' plan** ([Handout 5](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-5-the-keepers-plan)), drawn on a board on the wall: the stair, the lodge, the Lullaby, the stream, and a vast chamber marked only HER CHAMBER. Give the players [the player map](map/player-map.svg).

The spare parts matter: the lead and the spare pipe give `+1B` to repairing the Lullaby. A bunk-room locker holds a keeper's tuning fork. Anyone who hums the song with it in hand gets `+1B` on Performance under the hill.

### The second door

The lodge's inner gate leads to the lower works. Its brass mouth reads **SING ME THE STONE**.

**Dream-sparks.** In the passage beyond, the first dream-sparks drift along the ceiling: small living flames, the size of a fist, shaped like nothing. They are her dreams leaking out of the rock. They drift toward noise and heat. Singing the Fire verse sends them back into the stone. Otherwise they are a nuisance that can turn into a danger: see [their profile](02-npcs.md#dream-sparks).

**End the scene** at the third door, where the music is loud now, beautiful and slightly wrong. Its brass mouth reads **SING ME TO SLEEP**.

## Scene 3: The Lullaby

**Purpose:** the engine, the full truth, a choice, and the rival. Fill one box as the scene begins: the engine misses a note.

> _The door opens on music. You stand on an iron gallery high in a cavern, and the cavern is full of pipes: brass pipes like the trunks of trees, rank behind rank, rising into the dark. Below, an underground stream turns a great waterwheel, and the wheel drives a forest of rods and bellows that breathe in and out, in and out. At the heart of it all, in a cradle of iron and crystal, a golden cylinder turns slowly. As it turns, the pipes sing. It is the song from the festival. Through an arch at the far end of the cavern comes a red glow, and the sound of something breathing like a forge._

### The engine

The Lullaby is failing. Every few minutes a note goes wrong: a cracked pipe in the third rank shrieks instead of singing, and the drive belt slips on the wheel. Two repairs fix it:

1. **The cracked pipe:** climb the pipe ranks (Athletics, or Nerin's _hold to the wall_) and patch it with lead from the lodge (Mechanisms, `+1B` with the lodge's spare parts).
2. **The belt:** hold the slipping belt on the turning wheel (Athletics) while someone tightens the tension bar (Mechanisms). A failure on the wet wheel can mean a fall into the stream, which is cold and fast but survivable: `1D6` damage and a long swim back.

When both are done, **erase two boxes**, and the engine misses no more notes for the rest of the night. Make the moment beautiful: the music swells, steady and whole, and from the red archway comes a long, contented sigh.

### What it is

By now the company has the log, the scale, the breathing hill, the music and the red glow. Nerin's Sense Magic finds a working of the Mind form centred on the golden cylinder; a Critical also shows that it is a Bend working of enormous Magnitude, anchored to the cylinder itself. Let the players put it together: **the Golden Score keeps something asleep, and taking it wakes it.**

Then let them argue. The table may want to:

- **Take the Score anyway** and run. That leads straight to the finale, with Lucan right behind them.
- **Leave it** and go home without pay. Lucan takes it instead.
- **Swap it.** The log says the Silver Score is in the hoard. If they can fetch it, they can put the silver one in the cradle, deliver the gold one, and keep the music playing. This is the clever plan, and the hoard is where it leads. The log also warns that the silver score lacks the fourth verse.
- **Look.** Through the red archway, the hoard. Nobody can resist looking.

### Lucan

At some point in this scene, whenever it is most awkward, **Lucan Vasse** and his two hirelings step through the door behind the company. They have followed them down the stair, through every door the company sang open. Lucan's way out is the stream: it runs from the foot of the waterwheel through a long cave to the Warm Spring, where he cut the grate this evening.

Play the meeting as a **silent standoff**. Nobody wants to be the first to make a noise down here. Lucan whispers his offer: _"Take the Score, come out with me, and we split Harrow's money. Or step aside."_ He does not believe in dragons. He thinks the breathing is the bellows and the glow is a furnace the old empire left burning.

- **Showing him the truth** changes things: the log in his great-great-grandfather's hand, the scale, a look through the archway. Influence opposed by his Persistence 50, with `+1B` for each of those he has seen. Success makes him hesitate. He will not betray Harrow yet, but he will not take the Score while the company is watching.
- **Fighting him** here is loud. Fill a box when it starts.

Whatever happens, Lucan's job is the Score, and he is good at his job. If the company does not take it, he takes it the first moment they look away: usually when they go into the hoard. See [Scene 5](#scene-5-the-waking).

## Scene 4: The Hoard

**Purpose:** the most dangerous room in the world, and the Silver Score under a dragon's foot. Fill one box as the scene begins if the Lullaby is still unrepaired.

> _The chamber is bigger than a cathedral, and it glows. Gold covers the floor in dunes: coins, cups, crowns, shields, armour, the plunder of a dozen kingdoms, all of it warm. On the gold, coiled like a cat in front of a fire, lies a dragon. She is red and gold and the size of a barn. Her flank rises and falls, slow as the tide, and each breath out puffs two small flames from her nostrils. One great eye is closed, a wrinkled lid of scales as big as a door. Far above, roots of the vines hang down from the roof like dark lace._

Give the players a moment to take it in. Then remind them of [the rules for quiet](#quiet). Every failed stealth test here fills a box.

### Crossing the gold

Walking on the hoard means sliding coins. Each person who crosses it tests **Deception (stealth)** at `-1P`. A failure fills a box. Ways to help:

- **Choose who goes.** Only Pebb (61%) and Seris (58%) are really good at this. A smaller team is a quieter team.
- **Lay a path.** Cloaks and blankets laid over the coins remove the `-1P`. Laying them is slow, and it costs a box of time if the Lullaby is unrepaired.
- **Sing.** Anyone singing the lullaby softly while the others cross gives them `+1B`. Nothing a singer does with their voice wakes her.

### The Silver Score

It lies half-buried under her **right forefoot**: a silver cylinder as long as an arm, its pins glinting between her claws. Getting it out without waking her is the heart of the heist. Ways to do it:

- **Lift the claw.** One claw weighs as much as a person, and it is warm. Lifting it is an Athletics (brute force) test at `-1P` with up to one helper. Orren and Mara are the people for this. A failure fills a box, and she twitches.
- **Lever it.** A crowbar and a wedge of coins: a Mechanisms test. Dori's game.
- **Move it with magic.** The Score is smaller than a person, so Nerin's measured shove can draw it out from under her foot without anyone touching her, from up to 15 m away. The claw is pressing on it, so the shove takes `-1P`.
- **Ask her to move.** Sing her the lullaby and, in the singing, ask her to turn over. She sighs and shifts in her sleep, and her foot lifts. This needs a successful Performance test, with the usual singing bonuses. It is the most beautiful way, and the GM should reward anyone who thinks of it.

The Silver Score weighs half what the Golden one does. One person can carry it.

### Temptation

The gold is everywhere. Each item taken fills a box, but let the players weigh that themselves. A handful of coins is worth `2D6 × 10` SP. A jewelled cup is worth 200 SP and is pressed into the coins near her nose.

### Her dreams

From box 4 on, **she talks in her sleep** each time a box fills. Use the [dream lines](02-npcs.md#what-she-says-in-her-sleep) in order. In the second, she mistakes whoever is nearest for **Aubin**, the last keeper, and scolds him for being late with her song. A player who answers her in character, gently, can calm her: an Influence or Performance test, success erasing a box. A player who lies to her gets nothing. She knows, even asleep.

**End the scene** when the Silver Score is out, or when the company decides against it, or the moment the music stops.

## Scene 5: The Waking

**Purpose:** everything at once. The music stops.

At the moment that hurts most, Lucan's hireling **Wick** lifts the Golden Score out of its cradle. Usually that is while the company is in the hoard; otherwise it is the moment they turn their backs. If the company took it themselves, Lucan tries to take it from them. The pipes die one rank at a time. The silence afterwards is the loudest thing they have heard all night.

If the company has already swapped in the Silver Score, the music does not stop. It plays on, and every time it reaches the end of the third verse it falls silent where the fourth should be. The hill notices. Run the finale the same way: Lucan still wants the Golden Score, and somebody still has to sing.

> _The last pipe sighs and stops. In the red chamber, the breathing changes. The gold begins to slide. Somewhere in the dark, two eyes as big as cartwheels move under their lids._

Lucan's crew heads for the stream and the back way out, carrying the Golden Score in a rope sling between two of them at half Movement.

### Beats

Run the finale in **beats** of about a minute each. In each beat:

1. **The hill.** If the Golden Score is not turning in its cradle and nobody is singing the fourth verse, fill one box. Read a dream line at each threshold the track crosses.
2. **The company.** Each character does one thing: a test, a run, a repair, a song, or up to three rounds of fighting. Singers do nothing else that beat.
3. **The dragon.** At Stirring (7 or more), she does one thing in her sleep: a sigh of flame at the loudest place in the hoard, a shift of her coils (anyone on the gold tests Athletics or falls and slides), or a question spoken aloud to whoever is singing.

Tasks the company will juggle:

- **Catch Lucan.** His crew is two beats ahead in the stream cave, slowed by the Score. Each beat of pursuit is an Athletics test on slippery rock; two successes catch them. Ilen runs it best. Seris can track them in the dark with Natural Lore.
- **Take back the Score.** A fight on the stepping-stones (loud), or words. Lucan can be turned: Influence against his Persistence 50, `+1B` for each piece of proof he has seen and `+2B` if he has heard her speak. If Gran Perrine is there and calls him by name, he stops.
- **Carry it back.** Forty kilograms, two people at half Movement, one at a quarter.
- **Put a score in the cradle.** A Mechanisms test seats either score and engages the drive. Nerin's measured shove can drop one into the cradle from 15 m away. The Golden Score restores the enchantment: no more boxes from silence. The Silver Score restores the music but not the last verse: someone must still sing the fourth verse each beat to stop the track rising, but singers get `+1B`.
- **Sing.** Anyone singing the fourth verse holds the track still for that beat. A singer who tests Performance and succeeds erases a box (see [the song](#the-song)).
- **Get out of the way.** She sighs flame at the loudest place. Fighting near her draws it.

**She sleeps again** when the track is at 3 or less with the Golden Score turning, or with the Silver Score turning and someone kneeling by her head promising to sing every harvest. She settles, her breath slows, and the gold stills. The company may be standing in the hoard, scorched, with a thief, a golden cylinder and an old woman, singing a harvest song to a dragon. That is the ending most tables remember.

### If she wakes

At box 10 she opens her eyes. **She does not attack.** She is groggy, starving and curious, and she has been asleep for two hundred years. She lifts her head, and the whole chamber turns to face her.

> _"Who stopped my song?"_

She will find the thief: whoever holds the Golden Score. She knows a lie when she hears one (Perception 100%), and she cannot be out-talked (Influence 100%, Persistence 100%). Nobody can fight her: she ends any fight in one breath. Use [her profile](02-npcs.md#vermeil) only if someone is foolish enough to try.

What she wants, in the order she asks for it:

1. **The truth.** How long has she slept? Where is the emperor who promised her one night? (Gone, ninety-three years ago, into the Hall of Measures, never seen again. She laughs, and the laugh is terrible.)
2. **The sky.** Her door is above her: the **Sky Door**, an iron hatch in the roof of the chamber, bricked and buried under the top terrace of the vineyard. She is stiff from two centuries of sleep, and she cannot reach the bolts. She asks the company to open it: a long climb up the hanging roots (Athletics, or Nerin's _hold to the wall_) and a Mechanisms test to draw the rusted bolts. Then she pushes it open herself, and earth and vines come tumbling in.
3. **Not to be alone.** She has dreamed of the keepers' voices for two hundred years.
4. **The thief, to eat.** She asks for Lucan. This is the company's test. _Nobody gets left behind._ Refusing to hand him over is right, and she respects it, if they offer something else. She has never tasted the wine her warmth has made. A cask of Warm Hill red, rolled down the stair, is the answer she will remember.

**A fair bargain** (honesty, the Sky Door opened, a promise that someone will come and sing every harvest, Lucan spared): at dawn she climbs out through the broken terrace and flies once over the valley, red-gold against the sunrise, while the whole village stands in the vines and stares. Then she comes back down into the dark of the hill. She sleeps because she chooses to, and because someone promised to sing.

**A bad bargain** (lies, threats, Lucan handed over, nobody willing to come back): she bursts out through the vineyard, burns the press house and the top terraces, and flies away north. The valley lives, but the Warm Hill goes cold forever, and somewhere in the Crown Vale there is a dragon with a grudge.

## Dawn

> _The sky over the Crown Vale goes grey, then gold. On the Warm Hill the vines steam in the first light. Down at the crossroads, the lamps of the Empty Chair are still lit, and a carriage with Lady Harrow's crest waits in the yard._

**What do they bring Lady Harrow?** She waits in the inn's best room with two guards. The Golden Score earns the promised 300 SP each, if the company can make her pay: Lucan's orders ([Handout 6](03-players-and-handouts.md#handout-6-lucans-orders)) show she never meant to. Shown the orders, she pays to keep them quiet. Shown the dragon's scale, she goes pale and offers 500 SP for it. Bringing nothing earns nothing. If she did not see the dragon fly, she never believes the story, and she says so.

**The family.** Odile Vasse learns what her family has kept without knowing. If the dragon sleeps, the Vasses become keepers again. Every harvest, from this one on, Gran Perrine and whoever comes after her sing all four verses at the tower door, knowing exactly what they are singing.

**Lucan.** If the company turned him, he stays to learn the fourth verse from his grandmother. If they did not, he is gone with whatever he carried out.

**Rewards.** 300 SP each from Harrow if she pays. If the dragon sleeps, the Vasses give each of the company a cask of Warm Hill red (worth 50 SP) and a standing welcome. The dragon's scale, if they kept it, sells for 500 SP to any collector, or Odile buys it for the family. Award **4 IP** each, or **5** if Vermeil sleeps by her own choice and nobody died. During play, give a Hero Point on the spot to anyone who goes back for someone at real risk, once per character.
