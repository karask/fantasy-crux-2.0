# Players and handouts

The **player brief** and **how to play** are safe to share before play. Give each **handout** only when [the adventure](01-adventure.md) says so. Print them on rough paper, or read them aloud.

## Player brief: a gift of wine

You are the **Last Wagon Company**. A few weeks ago, a flooded caravan was ordered abandoned with wounded people still aboard. You refused, and you brought everyone home. Afterwards you registered a charter with equal shares and one promise: **nobody gets left behind.**

Tonight's job is a strange one. **Lady Isaura Harrow**, a noble who collects the art of the old empire, has hired you to drive twelve casks of her wine to the **Crush**: the grape-treading festival at the **Warm Hill**, a vineyard in the hills. The wine is her gift to the Vasse family, who own the vineyard. That is the job everyone can know about.

The other job is in her letter. It pays **300 silver each**, on delivery, at dawn.

Lady Harrow mentioned that the locals tell stories about the hill. She said she has heard a great many stories in her life, and none of them was ever true.

**Bring:** your character sheet and your two Hero Points. Everything on your sheet is with you, and so is a wagon full of very good wine.

## How to play

**Tests.** When the outcome is uncertain, roll D100: one ten-sided die for the tens and one for the units. Roll your skill or less to succeed. A roll of a tenth of your skill or less is a **Critical**; your sheet gives the range. A roll of 99 or 00 is a **Fumble**.

**Bonus and Penalty dice.** `+1B` means roll an extra tens die and keep the better result. `-1P` means roll an extra tens die and keep the worse. Bonus and Penalty dice cancel one for one, up to three either way.

**Opposed tests.** Both sides roll. Critical beats Success, which beats Failure, which beats Fumble. If both succeed, the higher roll wins.

**Help.** A friend with 26% or more in the skill, and a way to help, gives you `+1B`.

**Hero Points.** You have two. Spend one to reroll a failed test, to turn a terrible wound into an ordinary one, to stay alive at 0 HP, or, if the GM agrees, to make a lucky detail true.

**Sneaking.** Moving unseen and unheard is Deception, usually against someone's Perception. Tonight, mostly, it will be against your own nerves.

**Fighting.** A round is five seconds. On your turn you have one Combat Action (attack, cast, or do something), one Movement Action, and one Reaction to Dodge or Parry when attacked. Your armour stops some damage. A single hit that reaches your Major Wound Level leaves you **Wounded** (`-1P` to everything) and **Bleeding** (1 HP a round). At 0 HP you are **Dying**, and your friends have three rounds to save you.

**Singing.** Most of you sing at about 20%. That is all right. Tonight, knowing the right song matters more than singing it well.

## Handout 1: Lady Harrow's commission

_Give at the start of Scene 1. The company received it yesterday, with the wine._

> **From the hand of Lady Isaura Harrow, at Harrow House**
>
> To the chartered Last Wagon Company, greeting.
>
> You will carry twelve casks from my cellar to the Crush at the Warm Hill, as my house's gift to the Vasse family. That is your errand in the light.
>
> Your errand in the dark: beneath the old signal tower on the hilltop lie imperial works, sealed since the fall of the empire. Within them turns the **Golden Score**, a gilded music-cylinder made for the imperial court, the finest instrument the old empire ever built. It belongs in a collection that will honour it, not under a hill of grapes.
>
> The doors of the works open to a song. The Vasse family keeps that song, and sings it at the Crush. Learn every verse. The last is sung only by the eldest of the family, at midnight.
>
> Bring the Score to me at the Empty Chair, the inn at the crossroads below the hill, before dawn. On delivery I will pay three hundred silver pieces to each of you.
>
> Speak of this to no one. The Vasse family are proud, and the hill is theirs only by a claimant's charter.
>
> _I. H._

## Handout 2: the Warm Hill Song

_Give in Scene 1, when the family hands out the songsheets._

```text
THE WARM HILL SONG
as it is sung at the Crush, as it always has been.
Sing the chorus after every verse.

Hush now, the hill is warm,
the vines hang low and the grapes are sweet;
sleep through the frost and sleep through the storm,
we'll sing you down from your head to your feet.

I. THE WATER
Down in the dark where the cold stream runs,
round goes the wheel and the water sings;
round for the moons and round for the suns,
sleep, little ember, and fold your wings.

II. THE STONE
Old is the stone, and the stone is deep,
older than kings and the crowns they wore;
stone keeps the dark, and the dark keeps sleep,
sleep, little ember, and dream no more.

III. THE FIRE
Low burns the fire in the hearth tonight,
red in the embers and gold in the grate;
lie down, little sparks, and put out your light,
sleep, little ember, the hour is late.

The last verse belongs to the eldest of the house,
and is sung at the last pressing, at midnight.
```

## Handout 3: the fourth verse

_Give when the company learns it: from Gran Perrine, from the family ledger, or by listening at midnight. If they only overhear it, read it aloud instead._

> **IV. The Sleep**
>
> Close your eyes, my golden one,\
> the sky will keep till the night is done;\
> nobody leaves and nobody's gone,\
> we'll sing you through to the morning sun.
>
> _Grandfather sang this one last, and softest. P. V._

## Handout 4: the keeper's log

_Give when someone opens the log on the desk in the keepers' lodge._

> **The Log of the Lullaby. Keeper Aubin Vasse.**
>
> _Spring, the first year without a crown._ No courier from the capital. No oil, no brass, no wages. They say the Emperor walked into the Hall of Measures and never walked out. The Lullaby does not care who wears the crown. It plays.
>
> _Midsummer._ A cracked pipe in the third rank; patched with lead. The belt slips on the wheel when the stream runs low. She talked in her sleep again, and asked for the sky. I told her: in the morning. I tell her that every night.
>
> _The twentieth harvest without a crown._ I am too old for the stair. I have taught the children the song, all four verses, and told them it is for the grapes. Let them think so. The wheel will turn and the hill will stay warm, and nobody need ever know.
>
> _Last entry._ The silver score is in her hoard. She took it in her sleep, as she takes all bright things; it lies under her right forefoot. It plays the tune, but it was never finished, and the fourth verse is not on it. The fourth verse must be sung by a living voice. If the golden score ever stops, she will wake. If she wakes, she will be hungry and she will be angry, and she will be right to be. Forgive us, little ember. We promised you one night.

## Handout 6: Lucan's orders

_Give when someone searches Lucan, or takes his coat._

> Lucan,
>
> The company opens the doors; you take the Score. If they hesitate, take it from them. Pay them nothing. A chartered company has no one to complain to who matters.
>
> As for your grandmother's dragon: I have bought a great many things that were said to be cursed. Not one of them ever was.
>
> _I. H._
