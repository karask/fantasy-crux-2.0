# Seven Bells for No King

[Download the mobile reading PDF](../../output/pdf/seven-bells-for-no-king-mobile.pdf) - one bookmarked file containing the campaign, introductory adventures, and creature references. Transfer it to your phone for offline reading. GM spoilers throughout.

> **Gamemaster campaign.** A sandbox for Fantasy Crux 2.0 in the Crownless Realms, designed for approximately **25 sessions of 3–4 hours**, usually with four or five characters. Only [The company’s invitation](09-player-primer.md) is safe to give players before play.

_At the bridge, a woman holds up a falling arch with a shaking hand. She asks you to get the children out. Months later, you may learn why the arch fell—and why she was there to save them._

The seven Crownworks are failing. Chartered companies can go where armies cannot, and your company’s first successes make it valuable to people who want the realm saved, bought, freed, or ruled. A master Shaper manufactures crises to make emergency government inevitable. A celebrated commander supplies its army. An ancient vampire intends to inherit its officials. A dragon intends to collect its debts.

The company can expose the conspiracy, defeat its leaders, rescue threatened communities, bargain with monsters, establish an independent power, or help restore a crown. Its choices determine which enemies survive to become the final threat. There is no required sequence of seven repairs and no inevitable coronation.

## Start here

1. Read **The truth behind the bells**, below, and [Running the sandbox](01-running-the-sandbox.md). Put the first three deadlines on the table.
2. Give players the [invitation](09-player-primer.md). Establish a company, two personal ties, and a place they want to protect. The existing [Last Wagon Company](../../pregenerated-characters/README.md) works without revision.
3. Begin with [A01: The lamp that went dark](04-whitewater-adventures.md#a01-the-lamp-that-went-dark), or offer the three alternative openings in the running guide.
4. Read only the chosen node, its destination links, and the relevant [adversaries](03-adversaries.md). At the end of each session, ask where the players intend to go next.
5. Record elapsed days, completed objectives, and the next visible threats in the [campaign ledger](10-campaign-ledger.md). A session ending never advances a clock by itself.

## The truth behind the bells

**This campaign’s adopted explanation.** The seven works suffer the different failures described in the Crownless Realms proposal: a substituted rod, a moved foundation, withdrawn treaty consent, unprotected water connections, root blight, falsified weather signals, and stolen grave registers. These are real problems. Solving the conspiracy does not cure blight or settle a treaty.

**Master Aveline Sere**, the Office of Continuance’s celebrated restorer, directs its hidden Pale Cabinet. During a famine twenty years ago, she watched rival rulers refuse a joint relief operation. She now believes divided sovereignty kills more people than any temporary tyranny could. She chooses victims, engineers shortages, and falsifies the necessity for occupation. Her courage at disasters is real; so is her responsibility for them.

Sere intends to reconnect the Crownworks’ surviving imperial command couplers in a ceremony called the **Concordance**. Local services—bridges, water protection, weather observation—have their own workings. The couplers connect their operating permissions to the Hall of Measures. Reconnecting them would let a central authority direct participating maintenance systems and exclude unapproved operators. It grants neither control of minds nor unlimited new magical power.

She tells each claimant that the restored network can recognise a rightful sovereign. Her draft actually appoints the **Office of Continuance** as permanent custodian until all emergencies end. Sere will manufacture reasons that day never comes. The commanders, crews, institutions, and contracts maintaining the works would become its practical government.

**The second twist:** Sere needs competent repairs. The company’s early successes can help her unless it also changes custody or isolates the command couplers. Those rescues remain victories. Saving people is never revealed to have been pointless. Once the company understands the couplers, it can preserve local service while denying imperial control.

**The third twist:** the failed coronation ninety-three years ago included an attempt to override incompatible local permissions. Evidence establishes that dangerous act, not what ultimately happened to Caldras IV. His fate and the metaphysical nature of magic remain unresolved. No necessary answer comes from a prophecy, the emperor’s resurrection, or a god appearing to explain the world.

**The fourth twist:** the villains have separate plans. Marshal Rovan Kest expects Sere to submit to military supervision once the works are secure. The Ash Chancellor intends to replace key custodians with dependent clients. Varkesh-of-Cinders expects any successor government to honour its debts. Each can betray the others for reasons the party can discover.

## What the players can change

- **Custody:** Who holds an anchor, operates a work, protects its workers, or possesses a copy of its records?
- **Capacity:** Which factions have food, transport, trusted messages, engineers, witnesses, and soldiers?
- **Legitimacy:** Who has a defensible public claim, and who merely has enough armed people to enforce one?
- **Relationships:** Who trusts the company enough to attempt a dangerous task in its absence?
- **The final conflict:** Which surviving enemy has the means and desire to force a confrontation?

The party is never required to collect all seven objects, visit every site, trust a particular patron, or spare a recurring villain. Captured evidence stays captured. A dead villain stays dead except for the vampire’s explicitly published Death Mist and the hag’s established anchoring rules. If the players stop the entire plan early, that is a campaign victory. Remaining sessions can follow their chosen reconstruction, dragon conflict, or regional ambitions; do not invent a more secret mastermind to reclaim the ending.

## Intended scale and challenge

Begin with ordinary starting characters and the published wounds, recovery, and advancement rules. This campaign becomes epic through allies, preparation, influence, dangerous magic, and extraordinary objectives. It does not assume that twenty sessions turn an ordinary person into someone who can safely trade blows with a dragon.

There are **15 substantial adventure nodes**, several of which can occupy two or three sessions, plus space for company projects and consequences. A typical group plays 11–14 nodes and two to five sessions of its own initiatives. Twenty-five sessions is a planning estimate, not a quota or a promise that every printed encounter will appear.

All major threats have warnings, aims, and withdrawal routes. There are also committed enemies who can be defeated decisively. Sere’s concern for strangers does not excuse her murders; Kest’s care for his troops does not excuse his hostage policy; the Chancellor knowingly feeds on people. Players do not have to negotiate away their opposition to these acts.
