## Reading these profiles

Numbers assume ordinary Fantasy Crux HP and current published rules. These are experienced NPCs, not starting characters built from creation pools. They have **no Hero Points, legendary actions, automatic saves, scripted escapes, or immunity to an inconvenient defeat**. Named creature variants explicitly state their changes. General unlisted human skills are 20%, unfamiliar Lore 10%; unlearned Shaping and languages are unavailable. Major Wounds, reactions, armour, cover, and PP apply normally.

The default party is four ordinary PCs. Early encounters use two or three ordinary opponents, with other dangers separated by terrain or objectives. For three PCs remove one supporting fighter; for six add an objective, a wider rescue, or one supporting fighter. Never add a second dragon, troll, or master Shaper merely to match party size. Inspect the actual party’s attacks, defences, magic, and recovery before selecting an optional fight. These encounters are authored challenges, not playtested guarantees.

For creature abilities and full combat procedures, the linked published profiles govern. In particular, attack and Parry outcomes use the combat rules, not an invented “higher roll always hits” shortcut.

## Master Aveline Sere

**Human master Shaper; leader of the Pale Cabinet.** Copper pins hold one sleeve over an old burn; she keeps a list of disaster victims folded inside her working coat. During rescues she remembers every helper’s name. “How many people must we lose before someone is allowed to decide?”

**Wants:** permanent emergency custody of the Crownworks. **Will do:** order targeted sabotage, imprison witnesses, sacrifice a district to secure the remainder. **Refuses:** pointless cruelty and destruction of irreplaceable works. These limits constrain her tactics without absolving her.

**First contact:** she helps at A01 or arrives to inspect a later rescue site. Show exactly what she can accomplish with prepared tools and limited PP. She offers the party honest work with an inspector-custody clause. **Exposure:** A04’s dispatch trail, A08’s jack orders, or A13’s model and draft. **Pressure:** public evidence plus withdrawal of engineers forces her to choose negotiation, flight, or open coercion.

**Fair resource example:** warned of an attack shortly beforehand, she casts protection for 4 PP, beginning with **17 current PP and M4 active**. If she also flies, she has **13 PP and M8 active**. Two M4 attacks leave 5 PP; an M3 retreat leaves 2. Counter consumes the target’s full Magnitude and may make that escape unaffordable. On Criticals and failures use the actual payment rules and update the ledger; do not pretend every cast cost the example amount. Precasting requires real warning and Duration; an ambush may catch her without a Ward.

**How she fights:** behind complete stone cover with one accessible route to her targets, helped by two guards. She first asks the party to stop, then uses a shove to separate an attacker from a vulnerable operator. She avoids striking an anchor. At 6 HP or fewer, or with fewer than 5 PP, she seeks a negotiated withdrawal or a visible escape route. Catching her is possible. She heals only if able to pay and spend the Action.

**How she can lose:** expose the draft, deny crews, separate her from the Hall, exhaust her PP, interfere with Practice, outflank her cover, or defeat her bodily. If captured, she can explain the couplers and testify. If persuaded to abandon the programme, she must publicly release prisoners and surrender control; regret in private is insufficient. She is the only default master antagonist, so removing her truly cripples T11.

## Marshal Rovan Kest

**Human commander and expert fighter.** A handsome broken nose, an old soldier’s careful knees, a plain cloak mended by a unit widow. He eats last in camp. His banner shows an open hand above a drawn sword. “You may keep your council. You may not keep a second army.”

**Wants:** reunification under permanent military supervision. **Will do:** hostage-taking, punitive seizure of food, detention without trial. He knows Sere conceals records; he learns the extent of deliberate sabotage through play. That discovery can make him arrest her while continuing the occupation himself. His disagreement with her is not automatic redemption.

**First contact:** a cavalry escort genuinely protects a convoy. He invites the company to inspect his orderly camp. **Exposure:** his quartermaster’s contradictory requisitions, hostages at A14, and the contingency orders at A13. **Bargain:** a defined evacuation or ceasefire, backed by supplies and witnesses. He will keep a signed local ceasefire unless the company breaks its terms; he does not promise permanent disarmament.

**HP 17; MWL 9; PP 14; Order 9 in chainmail; Move 15 m; AP 5; DM +1D6.** Close Combat 92%, Ranged Combat 65%, Unarmed Combat 75%, Dodge 75%, Persistence 85%, Resilience 80%, Athletics 75%, Riding 85%, Perception 75%, Influence 80%, Lore (Military Tactics) 90%, Deception 55%.

He cannot shoot the bow while wielding sword and shield. **Talents:** Weapon Expertise (Arming Sword), Disarm, Protector, Defensive Stance, Rally, Tactician. No Shaping or extra attack talent.

**How he fights:** make one attack or use Rally/Defensive Stance, with ordinary Movement and reactions. He Disarms a dangerous weapon when capture serves his purpose, uses Protector for an adjacent officer, and fights through a defensible doorway rather than being surrounded willingly. Weapon Expertise removes one remaining penalty on one eligible sword test each round; it does not create a Bonus die. He has to choose whether his Reaction protects himself or someone else.

**How he can lose:** three or four PCs can exploit his limited actions, draw away guards, secure routes around his shield, or challenge him where reinforcements cannot reach him. His personal HP remain human. A duel is optional and its terms are witnessed, not a compulsory single-player finale. At 8 HP or fewer he orders withdrawal if an organised retreat remains possible; trapped, he offers surrender only with protection for his wounded soldiers.

**His army:** starts as 120 infantry and 30 mounted scouts near Crownseat, including support soldiers; it is not 150 elite duellists. After supplying guards and detachments, he can bring at most **100 field troops** to Vellbridge. The remaining fifty hold his supply line and Office depots. Further recruitment is a visible operation requiring wages, time, and equipment. If armies fight, use [Mass Combat](../../src/content/rules/gm-tools/mass-combat.md), including his Tactician, rather than rolling 100 individual attacks against PCs.

## The Ash Chancellor

**Named vampire variant.** It wears a smoke-grey coat and a rose-coloured mourning ribbon; its hands are always pleasantly cool. It speaks of old rulers with the impatience of someone who endured their meetings. “We have tried kings. We have tried their absence. Perhaps continuity deserves a turn.”

**Wants:** a government of dependent clients who supply blood and protect its access to the Gate. **Will do:** murder, coercion, bribery, and predation. **Limits:** it prefers influence and surviving witnesses to indiscriminate slaughter. It bargains honestly about selected facts while concealing the people imprisoned below its dining room.

Use the published [Vampire](../../src/content/rules/creatures/vampire.md), with these explicit individual changes: **Close Combat 80%, Unarmed Combat 75%, Influence 90%, Lore (Imperial Administration) 95%, Shaping 80%; no worn armour, so AP 0 and Order 12** from its published DEX 11 and INT 13. All other characteristics and abilities remain: **HP 18; MWL 9; maximum Blood Reserve 18; POW 0; DM +1D6; Move 15 m**. It has 12 current Reserve when undisturbed at A12. Record feeding and spending; it has no natural recovery of Reserve or HP.

**Correction for the current magic rule:** Blood Reserve substitutes for POW where relevant, including paying costs. The campaign uses the current general **INT-based active limit, 13**, rather than treating the older vampire ability’s POW-reference as permission for a higher active total. This is an explicit campaign reconciliation of the two texts.

**Weapons:** arming sword 80%, `1D8+1D6`, Medium; bite 75%, `1D6+1D6`, Medium. A medium shield is in its study, not invisibly equipped at dinner. **Talents:** Shaping and Counter, added for this individual. **Cells:** Bend·Mind, Alter·Mind, Ward·Force/Motion. Safe 4, maximum I4. **Practice:** touches its mourning ribbon while naming an office. **Tell:** a second shadow bows a moment late.

Use two prepared workings: Ward·Force/Motion I2 D1 **M3**, 4 applicable Ward AP for a scene; or Bend·Mind I2 R1 D1 **M4**, one `-1P` distraction to a named mental task, Persistence opposed. This is not automatic obedience or ownership of a PC. At dinner, it has no pre-cast Ward unless warned. A hostile spell reveals its Tell and costs a normal Action.

**Predatory abilities:** its published Bite can establish a grapple after HP damage. Drain then consumes a separate Combat Action, draining actual PP first, and only draining HP if the victim started the action at 0 PP. Life Blind Sight is blocked by solid walls. Mist Form takes a Combat Action and permits only movement or reforming. It cannot bite, cast, Counter, or carry a living captive in mist.

**Defeat and escape:** at 0 HP it is disabled until the start of its next turn, then Death Mist occurs at 1 HP. Follow the published adjacent full-round staking-and-decapitation procedure in that interval; use normal timing, never an extra cinematic grace round. If the party cannot complete it, keep tracking the mist’s movement, HP, and available routes. **Direct natural sunlight deals 2 HP each round**, ignoring AP; artificial “sunlight” has no unpriced extra effect. Its chosen Holy Aversion is the **Rose Lamp**, shown in portraits and old trial accounts; presenting the symbol requires the published opposed action. The party can know all of this before hunting it.

The house’s roof shutters, sealed vents, and morning exposure provide another credible way to finish the hunt. Locking a door does not stop mist if an observed crack remains. Blocking a vent means actually covering it. A coffin is a refuge and evidence cache, not a secret resurrection machine or mandatory destruction condition.

## Varkesh-of-Cinders

**Ancient dragon; independent power.** It lowers its head to listen as carefully as it raises its wings to intimidate. Small bronze memorials surround its court, each bearing the name of a person who honoured a bargain with it. “You call it ancient history when the debtor dies. I find that convenient.”

**Wants:** recognition and payment of an imperial debt, with control of selected tolls as security. **Will do:** occupy property, seize hostages, burn armed resistance and accept civilian deaths as the cost of enforcement. **Limits:** it keeps witnessed terms, respects a brave refusal, and prefers durable tribute to ashes. It is neither secretly benevolent nor obliged to fight the company.

Use the published [Dragon](../../src/content/rules/creatures/dragon.md) unchanged: **HP 50; MWL 25; PP 26; Order 18; AP 12; DM +7D6; Move 30 m ground / 45 m flight**. Unarmed Combat 100%, Persistence 100%, Resilience 100%, Perception 100%, Influence 100%, Athletics 100%, Dodge 30%. Retain all published Exceptional Skill ranks. Bite `1D10+7D6`, Huge; claw `1D8+7D6`, Huge; tail `1D20+7D6`, Huge. Double Claw 2 is its published multiattack option, not additional actions after a bite and breath.

Its 26 m cone breath deals `4D6`, with aware Dodge at `-1P` halving damage and AP applying; repeated use within one hour needs the published cumulative Resilience tests. Fire Resistance 12 remains. **No additional Shaping is assigned in this campaign**: its natural abilities and political reach are already formidable.

**Warnings:** cattle burned through their armour harnesses, a stone court scored by claws longer than a forearm, and survivors who explain that a shield may stop a claw’s reach without making a direct duel sensible. Varkesh’s first meeting is offered under witnessed safe conduct. Do not punish accepting that invitation with an unannounced breath attack.

**Defeating its programme:** contest the debt at A11; remove seized hostages and revenue; organise resistance it cannot profitably occupy; or secure a settlement that leaves it no legal claim to the works. These are substantial victories. Its loyalty to an agreement can let the PCs remove it from the finale without buying its friendship.

**Fighting it:** this is an optional strategic expedition, not a balanced end-of-session encounter. A11 supplies a constrained battleground, survey information, and ways to recruit capable allies. Its ordinary body remains lethal. Treat it as a **room-sized subject, Reach 2**, for size-priced Shaping in this campaign; an M4 person-targeted spell cannot silently affect the whole dragon. Prepared casters must rebuild their effects and respect ritual thresholds. Blocking flight and separating it from captives are objectives, not automatic reductions to its skills or AP. It retreats when badly hurt if a route remains. If the company earns its death, keep it dead.
