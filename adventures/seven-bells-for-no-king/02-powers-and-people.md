# Powers and people

## Playing a power through a person

An institution is not a single mind. Each representative below can promise only resources they actually control. Record promises in plain language, including the price and deadline. A successful Influence test can win a concession when a person has a reason to make it; it does not make them abandon a core interest. Useful proof or a solution to their problem can secure agreement without rolling.

For social opposition, use the listed **Influence / Persistence / Deception** scores where relevant. These are individual campaign ratings, not complete combat profiles. Their guards use [supporting profiles](03-adversaries.md#supporting-profiles); do not turn a frail official into an expert fighter because their Influence is high.

## The Claimant Houses

**Lady Maud Veyran — 75 / 80 / 35.** Close-cropped silver hair; old campaign boots beneath formal clothes. She peels apples with a soldier’s pocketknife while listening. “A road is a promise you can walk on.” She wants functioning roads and a defensible succession. She can lend twenty road wardens and publicly recognise company testimony. Her family benefited from atrocities recorded at Hollow Ward, but she did not order the present sabotage. She will release embarrassing records if the company protects living patients’ identities. She refuses collective punishment of Veyran subjects.

**Prince Oren Ordel — 85 / 60 / 75.** Beautiful handwriting, poor sleep, an unfashionable affection for public arguments. He offers the company a meal and then asks it to criticise his proposed constitution. He wants an elective settlement in which he expects to win the first election. He can obtain a hearing and a public printer, but cannot promise the votes. He will publish evidence against allies; he will also attempt to frame publication around his candidacy. A free election that he loses tests whether he means what he says. He is not Doctor Oren Vey of Hollow Ward.

**Duchess Sal Castaine — 80 / 80 / 65.** Impeccable manners, a repaired porcelain cup, and an astonishing memory for people’s dependants. “Of course I will help. Let us write down what help means.” She wants custody of the works before the truth becomes public. She can fund a regional expedition, guarantee the quarry loan, or buy a standstill from a creditor. Her aid carries precise concessions: inspection access, revenue shares, or nomination of an operator. Her word is unusually dependable. She will back a shared settlement if it protects her legitimate investments, but never surrender property simply because the company dislikes creditors.

**Their fracture:** the houses unknowingly funded the Cabinet through the Office. Proof of that funding is not proof they ordered murder. Maud will help investigate; Oren wants publication; Sal wants to secure vulnerable assets first. If all three withdraw support, the Cabinet loses lawful staffing and must expose its dependence on Kest.

## League of Open Cities

**First Factor Ysabet Dorn — 80 / 65 / 70.** Broad laugh, ink on her thumb, no jewellery she cannot pawn. She remembers the name of every ship that failed to return. Her goal is a toll-funded trust that makes the League the practical heir to imperial infrastructure. She sees expensive maintenance as an argument for predictable revenue and gives the best rescue terms in the room—if she also receives control of the books.

She can supply a sloop’s passage, 2,400 SP to resolve Blackglass, warehouse grain, and competent accountants. Smaller League cities resent the founding ports’ controlling votes. Offer them seats and they may support a different trust. Ysabet will honour a fair loss in court; stealing her seal makes later contracts difficult. She becomes dangerous when the company hides sabotage to destroy her investment rather than exposing the actual defect.

**Current operation:** purchase the quarry and its conduit. **If redirected:** the same credit can pay workers to keep an independent crossing open. **Personal hook:** her brother died in a preventable storm; Jessa Kelm’s false warnings could make her a furious ally.

## Crown Survey

**Chief Measurer Tal Orsic — 55 / 75 / 45.** An exhausted dwarf with seven pencils of identical length and a different stain on each cuff. He answers a question precisely enough to avoid answering what it meant. “That is an accurate measurement. It is not a safe conclusion.” He wants the works functioning and dangerous knowledge kept out of claimant hands. His silence helped Sere act.

Tal controls survey access, instruments, apprenticeships, and Dena’s field team when it is available. He cannot personally restore every Crownwork or accompany every expedition. He admits the suppression once shown two independent records of the command couplers, apologises without demanding forgiveness, and gives the party his complete remaining files. If they expose him, he resigns and continues teaching privately. Do not make every mentor a hidden villain.

**Dena Hallow — 45 / 60 / 25.** A field surveyor with a cracked thumbnail and a habit of offering strangers her last dry biscuit. She wants her team alive and their work public. If rescued in A01, she volunteers for one bounded survey operation at a time. She will not conceal an unsafe structure to protect Tal’s reputation. Her specialty is Mechanisms 65%, with Shaping 60% in Scry·Paths and Scry·Earth/Stone when casting becomes necessary; use the full existing adventure profile if it supplies a different detail.

## Synod of Seven Lamps

**Canon Prelate Jeren Sorn — 80 / 80 / 65.** Gentle hands, immaculate lamp cord, an unfailingly soothing bedside voice. He personally washes the feet of frightened patients, then decides which books those patients may read. He wants hospitals open and the Synod’s claim to spiritual authority preserved.

He can offer sanctuary, surgeons, burial records, and entry to the Hall’s chapel. He initially refuses to release the imperial hymn because it supports traditions his examiners condemn. Proof that its suppression enables murder can divide him from those examiners. He will release it in exchange for protecting the library and accurately distinguishing historical evidence from claims about the gods. Threatening to burn it makes him an enemy. He may back an independent hospital charter even while opposing a secular government.

**Doctor Samel Ancor and Sister Irra Vale** remain the company’s medical contacts. Samel wants enough beds and clean supplies; Irra wants patients admitted regardless of their Shaping tradition. Their request is concrete: escort a surgeon, pay for a wagon of linen, or secure an agreement that occupied hospitals remain open. Neither becomes a hostage merely because the party likes them.

## Deep-Road Compact

**Speaker Droma Kel — 65 / 75 / 50.** An expansive dwarven host who feeds visitors before delivering devastating news. She keeps a miniature of every bridge she has closed. “Stone does not forgive hope.” She wants the northern trade route preserved, even if one district must be abandoned and traffic diverted through Compact toll galleries.

Droma has six deployable engineers, equipment, and access to Dor-Morak’s council. She conceals the evacuation plan from affected families. Exposing that secrecy can remove her from office; helping her announce a viable evacuation can redeem her standing. Her deputy wants arrests in the Nine Warrens because blaming sabotage by goblins is easier than admitting delay. Evidence of Rusk’s jack orders lets Droma oppose that deputy without conceding every policy question.

## Verdant Court

**Advocate Serel Thorn — 70 / 75 / 40.** A young elf who collects exact promises in a little green book and reads them back without sarcasm. They laugh readily with children and almost never with officials. They want smaller negotiated protections to replace the imperial orchard system.

Serel can provide guides, blight expertise, and a hearing with living-domain representatives. They cannot order a dryad to sacrifice its tree or erase tenant claims. Their allies include human orchard towns whose livelihoods depend on the Bell; deep-grove delegates want it allowed to fail. The company can broker phased change with food support, preserve the Bell under tenant control, or help a voluntary relocation. Serel opposes both forced imperial restoration and abandonment disguised as liberation.

## Nine Warrens League

**Convenor Ekkit Nine-Nails — 75 / 60 / 80.** A cheerful goblin with a little notebook for every coat and no interest in denying smuggling. “A secret road is a toll you cannot see.” He wants the imperial route archive published and usable by ordinary travellers.

He can offer printers, translators, scouts, discreet transport, and the Hall’s service-route plan. His radical friends want everything released immediately; his farming constituents fear the maps expose their water and homes. Supply a redaction plan with independent custodians and he becomes a powerful communications ally. Seize his printing equipment and he will cooperate with almost anyone who returns it. He distrusts Sere because her “open access” plan requires every operator to register with her office.

## Iron Banner March

**Marshal Korva Ash-Tusk — 70 / 85 / 35.** A deliberate orc jurist who lets a silence become uncomfortable before answering. She sharpens a reed pen with more attention than her sword. She wants proof of the Red Field massacre and freedom from inherited service obligations.

Korva can escort a witness, arbitrate an oath, and bring forty disciplined Banner soldiers to a coalition whose terms her court accepts. She cannot prevent every clan raid through personal authority. Her nephew leads a faction that sees all claimant documents as tricks. Evidence plus a credible hearing restrains him; promises of “justice later” do not. Korva will oppose Kest’s occupation but refuses to become another claimant’s hired cavalry. Her troops travel from Banner Ford and require food like everyone else.

## Saffron Scale Throne

**Calendar-Queen Tessara IX — 75 / 75 / 60.** A lizardfolk sovereign whose clothes change with the hour’s office. She taps a sun-clock before agreeing to anything. “I can grant that until noon. Who will grant it after?” She wants the Observatory of Two Noons surveyed before foreign claimants strip it and the water diversion controlled by her court.

She provides canal access, survey barges, and astronomical expertise. Her office ends on Day 49. **Dry Magistrate Ashal** (Influence 60, Persistence 70) has the following season’s authority and fears the flood court will leave him ruined farms. The PCs can obtain his co-signature early, making a plan survive Tessara’s departure. Taking sides in their personal rivalry is less useful than making both responsible for the same published water schedule.

## Pearl Compact

**Tide-Speaker Oloa Sar — 80 / 80 / 45.** A gracious merfolk diplomat who keeps a bowl of still water beside her during storms and watches it too often. She fears drowning people through a political decision and will not surrender her people’s right to make decisions at all.

Oloa controls a rescue-house team, treaty access, and introductions to navigators. She has withdrawn consent to the Tide Lock’s old terms, not ordered its destruction. Reef wardens demand removal of the treaty pearl; rescue houses fear the repercussions. A temporary thirty-day operating consent, with rescue-house oversight and a dated renegotiation, is within her authority if the harbour stops unlawful dredging. Permanent renewed consent requires the Compact assembly, reachable by normal sea travel, or authorised delegates at the congress.

Merfolk breathe air. Their halls have surface access and breathing wells; underwater passages are not indefinite safe hiding places. Oloa can negotiate on dry land.

## Thistledown Post

**Keeper Brindle Quill — 65 / 85 / 30.** An elderly pixie who speaks politely even while grounding the entire priority network. He labels pens so others can find them. He wants authenticated warnings preserved, and knows that copying private dispatches risks the neutrality that makes the Post useful.

Brindle can authenticate evidence and distribute an agreed bulletin. He will not knowingly spread a useful forgery. **Postmaster Pella Underleaf** runs Vellbridge’s Station Seven and puts the company in contact with him. Her unpaid-postage indignation conceals genuine concern for exhausted couriers. Protecting a relay earns reliable delivery, not instant messages. If the PCs publish sealed private letters unrelated to public safety, Brindle insists on safeguards before carrying their next packet.

These people do not automatically agree with the company. A useful disagreement is “I will help evacuate, but I will not abandon my neighbours’ land claims,” followed by something the PCs can act on.

## Relationships to bring into scenes

- **Tal and Sere:** professional admiration damaged by Tal’s suspicion; she uses his caution to justify withholding evidence.
- **Sal and Ysabet:** rival creditors who can finance a joint rescue while arguing over whose contract survives it.
- **Korva and Maud:** mutual respect and a historical crime neither can politely ignore.
- **Serel and Jeren:** capable healers on both sides; conflict over custody of the Orchard Bell and its records.
- **Droma and Ekkit:** mutual dependence, private affection, public accusations. A bridge needs both their communities.
- **Oloa and Tessara:** both govern water by negotiated authority; each understands why a mechanically simple solution can be politically impossible.
- **Brindle and Neris:** she sells secrets; he protects messages. They share a fierce objection to forged testimony.

When several factions want the same discovery, bring two representatives into the same room. Let them make concrete offers and disagree in front of the PCs. Avoid making the company carry twelve sequential quest requests before anyone will talk.
