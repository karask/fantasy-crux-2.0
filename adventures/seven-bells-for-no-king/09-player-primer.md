# The company’s invitation

> **Player-safe introduction to Seven Bells for No King.** Read this before choosing characters. Other campaign chapters are for the Gamemaster.

The old empire left behind roads, bridges, aqueducts, and wonders no living ruler can maintain alone. Three houses still claim its throne. Free cities defend their charters. The peoples of the forests, mountains, plains, rivers, and reefs negotiate with their neighbours as powers in their own right.

Now a lamp on the White Span has gone dark. Surveyors are missing, grain barges are delayed, and everyone with a seal has an opinion about who should take charge.

You are a chartered company based in **Vellbridge**, in the Whitewater March. Your charter names your partners and your salvage shares. It lets an authority hire you without admitting a rival’s soldiers. It grants no immunity, but it gives people a practical reason to ask for your help.

There will be dangerous expeditions, old secrets, monsters, powerful Shapers, hard fights, and people worth protecting. You choose your employers, your routes, your promises, and the causes you make your own. You can acquire allies, a home, influence, and eventually a voice in how the realms are governed.

## What you already know

- **The seven Crownworks are failing in different ways.** The White Span shortens journeys; the Deep Causeway protects mountain roads; the Tide Lock guides coastal water; the Lantern Aqueduct carries clean supplies; the Orchard Bell shelters orchards; the Storm Needle watches weather; the Gate of Names guards imperial burial ways.
- **The claimants are not the whole world.** Dwarven engineers, goblin communes, elven grove courts, orc frontier jurists, lizardfolk seasonal magistrates, merfolk rescue houses, and pixie couriers all have their own interests and disagreements.
- **Magic leaves signs and costs effort.** Experienced Shapers can do extraordinary things. They still need knowledge, preparation, strength, and sometimes help.
- **Time and distance matter.** You can often reach one place while another problem develops elsewhere. Allies can act in your absence if they know the task and have the means to do it.
- **Danger can be assessed.** Ask about tracks, witnesses, routes, tools, retreat, and terms. A successful expedition may involve fighting, bargaining, rescuing, investigating, or bringing back enough knowledge to return prepared.

## A company worth following

If using the **Last Wagon Company**, your bond already exists: you brought wounded passengers home together when a caravan was ordered abandoned. Keep the promise, “Nobody gets left behind,” as an aspiration you can interpret and argue about together.

For a new company, agree on four things:

1. What did you accomplish together before the first session?
2. Who in Vellbridge will welcome you home?
3. What work will you refuse, even if it pays well?
4. What do you hope to build if you become successful?

Each character should have a reason to travel with the others and an interest beyond being paid. You might be a displaced worker seeking a safe home, an apprentice eager to understand old works, a soldier who has seen too many “temporary” occupations, a negotiator protecting a community, a hunter drawn to dangerous ruins, or someone who simply refuses to leave strangers in trouble.

## Your first contacts

**Hesta Coil**, at the Brazen Mule, supplies equipment and knows which companies bring their hirelings home. Your company has modest rooms above her shop for its first month in exchange for repairs and news.

**Doctor Samel Ancor and Sister Irra Vale**, at the House of the Rose Lamp, treat serious injuries. A safe bed and a surgeon can matter as much as a sword.

**Tal Orsic**, at Third Measure Hall, can commission surveys and introduce suitable candidates to Shaping training. He values accurate work and restraint near unstable structures.

**Aunt Neris Skell**, at the Bent Copper bathhouse, can arrange discreet introductions. Her prices are negotiable; her memory is excellent.

**Postmaster Pella Underleaf**, at Station Seven, carries authenticated messages through a physical relay network. She treats neutrality seriously and unpaid postage personally.

## At the table

Expect approximately **25 sessions**, with room for your own projects and unexpected directions. Tell the GM what you intend to pursue next so that the places and people you choose can receive the most preparation.

Keep a shared record of names, promises, dates, and discoveries. Ask what you know about travel time before committing to an urgent journey. You can recruit help, negotiate delays, split responsibilities safely, abandon an employer, return to an unresolved place, or attempt an approach nobody anticipated.

The realms will continue around you. Your successes will continue too.
