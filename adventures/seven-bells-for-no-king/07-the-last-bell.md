## A15: The Hall of Measures

**Place:** Crownseat, where three governments guard different approaches to the sealed imperial hall. **Play:** 2–3 sessions, often one for assembly and infiltration, one for the decisive operation, and one for consequences. **Fictional time:** determined by the chosen approach. Day 84 is the Cabinet’s proposed congress, not an unavoidable supernatural appointment.

**Independent entrances:** a claimant issues a commission; Brindle brings invitations to the congress; Ekkit offers a service-route map; Jeren can admit a burial delegation through the chapel; Sere’s draft names the Hall; or the company follows enemy personnel. At least three of these remain available without solving earlier nodes. The Hall can be visited before the announced congress.

The final question is **who has the means to impose what outcome?** Before preparing this node, copy the current ledger. Do not assume all villains survive, all works were repaired, or the party wants a republic.

## What is actually at stake

The Concordance can reconnect the seven command couplers to an existing imperial operator registry. The Office’s scheme makes it the registry’s sole custodian, able to exclude other maintenance crews and coordinate participating gate and ward operations. Political control then follows through food, water, roads, staff, and soldiers.

It cannot dominate populations, create power without cost, change every local treaty, resurrect Caldras, or cure the works’ different faults. People and institutions still have to enforce the settlement. A local work with its command coupler isolated can continue its existing local functions.

This is why the PCs’ accumulated relationships matter. A defended isolated coupler defeats a seven-station command attempt. A rescued clerk can expose a false appointment. An independent engineer can refuse the sequence. A hungry army may accept a paid withdrawal. These are concrete ways to win, including before the ceremony begins.

### A rules boundary for the old enchantments

The command couplers and their narrow acknowledgement channel are **campaign additions to existing ancient anchored infrastructure**. Their creators’ permanent commitments and mythic prices are historical facts, not powers the party acquires for free. The channel carries only station acknowledgement and the established operator-registration sequence; it is not an instantaneous general messenger network.

Each coupler’s dedicated command enchantment is treated as **M8, original Shaping100**, for Sense Magic and Dispel. This magnitude describes a pre-existing single-person operator link—Scry·Paths I3, R1, D4, Reach0—within that station; other ancient workings carry the network between stations. It is not an M8 continent-sized spell. One coupler’s destruction or Dispel does not Dispel all the other Crownwork enchantments. Touch Dispel costs M8, takes an eight-hour ritual where the caster’s limits require it, and opposes Shaping100. An Expert with Safe4 can attempt it as a ritual; a Veteran with Safe3 cannot casually exceed their ritual maximum of6.

**Mundane alternative:** with the correct diagram, tools, and safe access, a competent operator can physically isolate an existing command collar in four hours. Mechanisms51+ or help from a Survey engineer is sufficient in safe conditions; a lower-skilled team takes an additional day of guided inspection. Rolls matter under danger or haste. This maintenance action interrupts a physical connection; it creates no new enchantment. Reconnection takes an equipped crew two days because alignment must be resurveyed. Cutting a collar blindly may damage local service, with the risk explained first.

**Restoration is different:** stopping sabotage, returning a genuine still-active anchor to its seat, operating manual spillways, renewing consent, treating blight, and repairing mundane structures can stabilise existing service. Recreating destroyed enchantments or enlarging a work’s Reach requires an actual rules-legal ritual, capable Shapers, full cost and commitment or Enchantment Capacity, and a declared lasting price if mythic. A political speech never pays those costs. The campaign’s victory condition does not require reconstructing every ruined enchantment.

| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| White Span       | Original rod recovered; custody agreed; crossing stabilised            | Hallow’s team can refuse a Cabinet inspector or isolate the collar; public rescue evidence undermines a fabricated panic         |

| Tide Lock        | Spillways prepared; consent renewed or temporarily negotiated          | Oloa can withhold authority from the Office while continuing rescue operations; a coerced pearl removal cannot replace agreement |
| Lantern Aqueduct | Dirty connectors isolated; clean supply arranged; observatory recorded | Saffron delegates provide reliable timing and a lawful operator across the seasonal transfer                                     |
| Orchard Bell     | Blight treated or livelihoods protected through a new arrangement      | Serel and Eren can refuse reconnection while maintaining agreed local protection; tenants have a voice in the settlement         |
| Storm Needle     | False selector exposed; manual signals restored                        | The coast has trustworthy warning and a station that can visibly announce an aborted sequence                                    |
| Gate of Names    | Registers returned; Chancellor’s clients exposed                       | The crypt is safer and burial clerks can certify the registry; missing registers instead create a real local undead problem      |

For each, track **service: damaged / stable / restored / lost**, **command: connected / isolated / destroyed**, and **custodian**. Stable means the specific immediate fault has been contained, not an invisible guarantee of permanent magical renewal. A work can be stable and independent, damaged and coupled, or otherwise mixed.

If any required station is unavailable, tell the GM truth plainly: **Sere cannot complete her seven-station programme.** She may bargain, send a known crew to repair it, seek military seizure, or abandon the attempt. None happens by fiat. If the whole programme is already defeated, use the Hall for a settlement or a surviving enemy’s independently supported move; a ceremonial boss battle is unnecessary.

## The Hall as an adventure site

Draw six spaces and their connections:

```text
                 [Mirror gallery]
                 /              \
[Three-guard square]--[Measure floor]--[Operator dais]
         |                    |               |
    [Chapel archive]------[Service undercroft]
```

The square, chapel, and service route are distinct entrances. Doors can be barred using ordinary object rules. The mirror gallery is an upper balcony with two stairs, not a teleport maze. The dais stands 3 m above the measure floor; the undercroft passes beneath it. Establish where all people, exits, and cover are before a confrontation.

1. **Three-guard square.** Each house controls one checkpoint. A commission, public delegation, disguise, crowd movement, or the chapel avoids having to defeat all of them. Six guards serve at each post, but only two question entrants while the others handle traffic. They are not automatically Cabinet loyalists. A civil order withdrawing consent changes their instructions.
2. **Chapel archive.** Jeren’s people shelter delegates. The hymn’s complete version gives the local-service isolation sequence, including a warning against coercive overrides. It can be accessed through a temple favour, proof of public danger, or careful intrusion. Its essential technical facts are duplicates of other clues, not the sole finale key.
3. **Mirror gallery.** Old mirrors show fragments of different claimants’ images, as in the setting’s founding mystery. They supply atmosphere and historical traces, not a test of the “true heir.” Brass plaques describe the failed override ninety-three years ago. A broken maintenance panel reveals the master brake linkage in the undercroft. The gallery’s stone balustrade gives substantial cover; falling is the normal 3 m hazard, not an invented instant death.
4. **Measure floor.** Seven small station indicators and a public seating ring. Local delegates and observers can see which acknowledgements are missing. A copy of the current custodian draft is on the clerk’s lectern; reading it exposes the permanent Office appointment. Guards can try to remove readers but cannot make the text inaccessible to all observers once copied.
5. **Service undercroft.** Three accessible routes from the chapel, floor stair, and external service passage. The latter’s diagram comes from Ekkit, A10, A12, or the original inspector contract at A11. The **master brake** is mundane: lift the safety cover, withdraw the retaining pin, then pull the lever. Reading its directions takes a minute. Under combat pressure an adjacent character spends **one full round** removing cover and pin, then a **Combat Action** to pull; no roll is needed with unobstructed access. It cancels the current registration attempt. It does not destroy local wards. Sere’s guards know this and may hold the room. Restarting requires two hours of resetting plus a fresh four-hour ritual; enemies cannot resume on their next turn.
6. **Operator dais.** Copper geometry, a reader’s desk, two side stairs, and four stone piers providing complete cover from appropriate angles. The operator has line of effect through the gaps, not through the piers. Two Cabinet guards attend Sere if available. Bringing the Bailiff requires physically transporting it from A13 beforehand and removes it from that site; it is never an automatic extra monster here.

## The six-hour attempt

Run this only if the Cabinet has all its requirements and persists. The default start is **Day 84 at noon**, ending at **18:00**. If the party forces postponement, date the next real attempt. A celestial window does not force the original appointment.

| Time                     | Action                                                                                                                                 | Opportunities                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 12:00–13:00              | Crews begin alignment; delegates inspect the registry and proposed custodian                                                           | Expose the draft, win a withdrawal, seize a local station, or enter through ordinary delegation                                       |
| 13:00–14:00              | Seven station acknowledgements are checked                                                                                             | Any missing one halts preparation. Forging a civic signature cannot fabricate a missing physical acknowledgement                      |
| 14:00–18:00              | The trained master performs the Hall’s four-hour interface-reading ritual                                                              | Full Practice is vulnerable. Removing the master, interrupting the rite, applying the brake, or losing a station stops completion     |
| After successful reading | Before the mechanism resets ten minutes later, the master must use a Combat Action at the dais to commit the written operator registry | A final chance to replace the draft openly, prevent access, Counter the just-resolved reading, or operate the brake before commitment |

**The interface reading:** Scry·Paths, I3, R1, D0, Reach0 = **M4**, performed as a four-hour ritual with full Practice and ritual +1B. The target is the local interface within 15 m. It costs 4 PP on ordinary success and gives one instantaneous reading of its deeply hidden alignment; it creates no ongoing effect or new continent-wide function. The Hall’s surviving calibration mechanism takes four hours to cycle, requiring the reader to follow the sequence throughout; an ordinary fast casting supplies a snapshot but does not complete that procedure. The ten-minute reset interval belongs to this existing mechanism, not to a free magical Duration. Its established operating restriction requires a trained master (Shaping100) who knows the complete sequence. The subsequent mechanical commitment uses the ancient registry’s existing function. A Counter against the completed Shaping follows normal range, reaction, opposition, and PP rules.

The master cannot fight and maintain four hours of uninterrupted ritual at the same time. If Sere abandons it to confront the PCs, the attempt stops and the next ritual must start afresh. This is a valid way to win the immediate objective, even if she survives the ensuing fight. Do not force the ritual to complete in the background while its only operator is duelling.

During the attempt, clearly identify which areas are reachable in minutes and when combat rounds begin. Do not cut from “you debate your options” to “six hours have passed” without agreement about elapsed time.

## Choose the confrontation that the campaign earned

**Sere still controls the operation:** she tries to preserve the rite until confronted with loss of a station or a credible immediate threat. Once interrupted she fights using her real PP, cover, and limits. Allow rescue of operators, removal of the registry, and reaching the brake alongside combat. The objective need not be killing her, but a decisive victory can include it.

**Kest has seized the Hall:** he places two veterans at the dais and two at the undercroft. Other troops hold streets outside; they cannot all fight through one door at once. He demands an emergency military appointment and may have Sere under guard. If she is unwilling to operate, he cannot replace her skill with an order. The party can free her under terms, reject both rulers, isolate him, or defeat his guards and commander. Apply prior supply losses and defections.

**The Chancellor has infiltrated custody:** it tries to exchange the approved registry for one appointing its clients. It needs a living operator or Sere’s cooperation; its Shaping80 cannot lead the Hall. A clerk the party rescued can identify the substituted names. If exposed, it seeks the service crypt through the paths actually available. Its own foreknowledge of sunrise and shutters matters; it does not willingly stand in daylight because the finale needs a fight.

**Varkesh is still an adversary:** its pressure may be an announced seizure elsewhere, an ultimatum delivered to the congress, or a physical arrival if scouts have reported its journey. It does not teleport from its court into the climax. Fighting it uses A11’s scale of preparation, not an added dragon on top of a master and army. Enna, a treaty delegation, or earlier settlement can remove its claim from the agenda without resolving every debt in the world.

**The Gate is still unsecured:** six skeletons and one [mummy](../../src/content/rules/creatures/mummy.md) attempt to follow an old evacuation route from the crypt into the square. They are a local consequence of missing registers, not a spontaneous army. Three skeletons appear first at a barred door; the others remain behind the mummy in the narrow stair. Warnings arrive from burial workers ten minutes before the first door gives way. Returning registers, providing the documented evacuation route into an empty courtyard, re-barring the passage, or fighting can contain them. If the Gate was secured, this incident does not happen.

Do not stack every surviving enemy into one combat. Decide who is present based on travel, interests, and prior information. Other factions act elsewhere through their own limited teams.

## Settlements the party can build

These are starting proposals, not a menu limiting player invention. Ask who operates each work, who pays its workers, who can inspect it, how affected communities consent, and what happens during the next emergency. A few enforceable answers are more useful than a perfect constitution.

| Settlement                    | Concrete requirements                                                                                                               | Gain and continuing cost                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Independent local stewardship | Keep or make couplers isolated; recognised local operators and a maintenance agreement                                              | No central seizure through the registry. Coordination depends on trustworthy messengers, budgets, and cooperation                                               |
| A crown under limits          | A willing claimant or other credible candidate; delegates accepting defined powers; independent inspection and local refusal rights | Faster common decisions. The new ruler has real influence and future ambitions; consent is negotiated, not revealed by a mirror                                 |
| A chartered common council    | Several major powers and affected communities agree to shared custody; no Office monopoly; paid crews and public records            | Broad legitimacy and shared expertise. Creditors, small states, and tenants need actual representation                                                          |
| A temporary repair compact    | Freeze the succession dispute; date a review; fund and supervise practical stabilisation                                            | Immediate relief without settling every ancient claim. The review date is a future political task, not a hidden doom clock                                      |
| The company becomes custodian | PCs obtain the same acceptance, staffing, and funding required of any other government                                              | Genuine player power, with responsibility and opponents. Being protagonists supplies no automatic obedience                                                     |
| The Cabinet or army prevails  | Its actual prerequisites survived and opposition failed or accepted terms                                                           | Public works may function, but detention and restricted access grow. Decide whether to end on costly compromise or continue a player-chosen resistance campaign |

Restoration plans can be scheduled as epilogue work with willing specialists. If the players want to make a new mythic enchantment during play, build that ritual openly using the core rules and declare every cost before commitment. A unique treasure, binding oath, permanent POW, or consequential quest may be proposed as a lasting price; never take an unannounced PC sacrifice to “make the ending epic.”

## Give the ending back to the people

Resolve an aftermath for each community the players engaged with: travellers on the Span, tenants in the Greenward, residents below the Causeway, coastal rescue houses, Saffron water users, and people named in the Gate registers. Mention both what the party achieved and what it chose to leave unresolved.

Give each surviving major NPC one final action consistent with their agenda. Maud can relinquish a claim, Ysabet can fund a work and demand an audit, Ekkit can publish the safe atlas, Oloa can sign a treaty, and Tal can teach apprentices under open inspection. Defeated villains do not all escape in an epilogue montage.

Then ask each player what their character does when nobody is paying them to save the realms. Let the last scene reflect the answer: a bridge reopening, a dragon’s empty toll fort, a courtroom with a witness who lived to speak, or a company kitchen where the wounded are finally home.
