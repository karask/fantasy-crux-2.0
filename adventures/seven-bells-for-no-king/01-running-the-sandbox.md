# Running the sandbox

## Preparation and the first session

Use ordinary creation or the existing pregens. Assume four PCs for encounter numbers; advice for other groups appears in [Adversaries](03-adversaries.md). Give the company an existing charter and modest lodging above the Brazen Mule. Hesta lends the rooms for the first month in exchange for repairs and news of missing caravans. This is a home they can keep, not a compulsory debt trap.

Each player names one person they would travel to help and one institution they distrust. Tie those choices to established NPCs where possible. Ask the group whether it prefers investigation, exploration, or military leadership, and put two immediately useful opportunities of that kind in front of it. The conspiracy develops independently of that preference.

**Default opening:** run A01, including the existing bridge rescue. After the rescue, Sere arrives with a relief crew and helps evacuate the approach road. She uses finite PP and accepts help. Do not make her sabotage detectable in this extra scene; the clues concern the earlier replacement of the rod. Give out Handout H1 before the company decides its next destination.

**Other starts:** A03 for a personal rescue; A05 for immediate disaster and diplomacy; or A02 for salvage and dangerous exploration. If A01 has already been played, start the campaign on the following morning, treating its actual outcome as history. If the horse-fair adventure has been played, Serrik can recommend the company to a caravan needing passage. Neither adventure has to be replayed, and Copperback does not become a true dragon.

## The calendar belongs to the world

Day 1 is the campaign’s first morning, in a wet season approaching a colder Greenward month and the Saffron dry-season transfer. Dates below are **this campaign’s forecast**, not setting-wide calendar canon. Write an actual date beside each once play begins.

Record travel, investigation, recovery, ritual, and rest. Round land travel up to half-days. A four-hour scene uses four hours, not a day. Two conversations in the same city do not consume two adventure-days. A printed node’s playtime estimate is not its fictional duration.

At each overnight stop:

1. Advance time by the amount actually spent. Resolve only deadlines or operations whose prerequisites are still present.
2. Move named NPC teams along their real routes. One team cannot repair a work and escort a prisoner in different regions simultaneously.
3. Deliver news that can have arrived. Show a visible consequence or an approaching decision, not a list of hidden clocks.
4. Record what an enemy actually learned and how. There is no universal villain awareness of the party’s plans.

Present at most three immediate decisions in a briefing, but keep the complete known calendar available. Players may investigate any other lead. Remote fronts keep developing even when the party has not visited them; most missed opportunities change an adventure’s situation rather than erase the adventure.

### The Cabinet’s actual requirements

For T11, Sere needs **Hall access; all seven command couplers connected and operable; seven local crews who will perform the sequence; and herself or a trained master at the Hall**. Ordinary public service can still be imperfect. The three claimants’ civic seals are needed to obtain lawful cooperation, not as supernatural bloodline keys. Coercion can replace cooperation only where the Cabinet has people physically present.

At start the Cabinet has Hall access through the Office, and its own crews at the Gate and Aqueduct. It has no automatic right to staff the other five works. Repairs commissioned through the Office include a request to accept a Cabinet inspector; the PCs can read, negotiate, or refuse that clause. Record every work’s custody.

Sere has **two mobile repair crews**, each eight engineers and two [Cabinet guards](03-adversaries.md#supporting-profiles). On Day 12 Crew North leaves Crownseat for Dor-Morak; on Day 20 Crew West leaves Crownseat for Leth Arain. Use the route times below. Once committed, neither crew is available elsewhere until it travels. Other works require contracted local support, negotiated in play or recorded in the ledger. If the company isolates a coupler, a crew needs access and two uninterrupted days to reconnect it; watchkeepers can prevent that. Announce discovered attempts. Do not undo isolation offscreen without a recorded attempt and available personnel.

**Principal movements:** in the default opening Sere is in Vellbridge on Days1–2, leaves on Day3, and reaches Crownseat at the end of Day5 by ordinary riding. Thereafter she works at the House of Tomorrow until the planned Day77 move to the Hall, unless play changes her itinerary. Kest is camped outside Crownseat; an ordinary march toward Vellbridge must leave by Day65 for the Day70 occupation, with cavalry scouting ahead. The Chancellor is based in Crownseat and Varkesh at the Cinders court. Record any changed journey before an NPC appears elsewhere. If another opening puts the company far away, Sere offers a letter or a later meeting instead of arriving impossibly fast.

Only Sere knows the full sequence at start. She is teaching her assistant **Nema Vale**, an Expert Shaper at 80%, but Nema cannot lead the existing master-only Hall interface. Nema will reach neither 100% nor instant competence because the plot needs a replacement. If Sere is removed, a replacement master must be independently recruited, taught for fourteen days from surviving complete notes, and announced through visible negotiations. Tal refuses. The party can win by making the plan impossible.

For mixed mountain routes, calculate separate travel days for the mountain and road legs; these figures round up rather than promise an optimistic arrival. For light woods, use the core rule’s worst substantial terrain multiplier across the day. Fast riding reaches 90 km/day before terrain, with Fatigue tests for both rider and mount. Hiking reaches 50 km/day without animals or wagons and tests Fatigue. Ordinary riding reaches 50 km/day; Marching reaches 30.

If the White Span is shut, a Council ferry carries the party to Gull’s Rest in **two days**, including loading, for **60 SP per person**; this is an explicit subsidised local exception to ordinary passenger fares. The ferry is established transport, not a last-minute rescue. It runs every morning and carries one horse per passenger for another 60 SP. A one-day emergency crossing needs League priority or Pearl pilots and uses the same fare. Vellbridge’s river route therefore remains available after A01.

At sea use 20 miles/day per knot. The hired sloop has Speed 6, Structure 20, ten competent crew at Sailing 45%, a captain at 60%, and **Marines** as its special feature so the company has quarters. It has no naval armament. Normal passenger fares are **1 SP/km**, approximately 274 SP per person for the 170-mile route. League sponsorship can cover passage in exchange for a stated favour. Loading adds half a day when departure is not prearranged. Apply actual weather and Sailing rules; no arbitrary storm every voyage.

### Messages and recovery

Use **60 km/day** along a prepared Thistledown land relay as a campaign logistics assumption for successive fresh couriers. Allow half a day for acceptance and sorting; round delivery up to half-days. This is a relay’s effective progress, not an individual pixie’s unbroken flight speed. Across the sea, packets wait for the next scheduled vessel and travel at its speed. A messenger cannot deliver a response before the original arrives. Remote instructions require a recipient, a secure address, and transit time.

Major Wounds require Surgery or equivalent treatment before natural recovery. The Rose Lamp in Vellbridge, Synod hospitals in Crownseat, Dor-Morak’s miners’ infirmary, Leth Arain’s road abbey, and Banner Ford’s field hospital offer it. Core natural recovery requires light activity; riding on an urgent expedition is not bed rest. Let an unchosen Last Wagon companion temporarily join when a player’s PC convalesces. They receive no duplicate rewards for running both.

## Delegation that gives allies real value

Track a named team, its current location, its agreed task, departure, travel, work time, and promised price. The company can fund and equip teams, ask a faction for help, hire specialists, or send a willing unchosen companion. A friend’s gratitude does not create an army.

| Korva’s six escorts                 | Protect or transport a known witness                                         | A route, a destination, and assurance of lawful treatment. Their presence in claimant territory must be negotiated |

**Resolve ordinary adequately supported work without a roll.** If one meaningful uncertainty remains, agree on it and roll the leader’s relevant skill once (usually 60%); success completes the task, failure brings a stated partial result and a delay, never an automatic unseen massacre. If the task involves enemy opposition, use the actual opposed skills or play the decisive scene. Tell the players before departure if the proposed team cannot plausibly do it. A secret obstacle must have evidence that scouts could discover; it cannot exist solely to invalidate delegation.

A task accomplished by allies stays accomplished. Their promised reward or political claim also matters. Allies can become disagreeable stakeholders without becoming traitors.

## Pacing twenty-five sessions

Award **3–5 IP per completed adventure**, including successful investigation, rescue, diplomacy, or withdrawal with meaningful gains. Default to 4 IP; award 5 for a major achievement. A multi-session node is normally one adventure, not automatic IP every session. Use the published limit of one increase per skill between adventures. Award a Hero Point for an exceptional costly rescue or pivotal heroic achievement, normally no more than one per PC in a single adventure. Never require combat for advancement.

Early commissions pay **300 SP per PC**, regional commissions **600 SP per PC**, and major stronghold or war objectives **1,000 SP per PC**, half in advance. These are campaign commissions, not a new price list. Agree travel, surgery, and specialist supplies separately. Successful unpaid community work earns equivalent practical support—a crew, passage, sanctuary, or equipment—not imaginary gratitude in place of every reward. Monster plunder uses published ratings where appropriate; public anchors and patient records are not unowned loot.

Example routes illustrate capacity, not scheduled chapters. Adjacent names here need not happen immediately; travel and rest continue between them.

If progress is faster, let the campaign end sooner or spend time on the company’s chosen future. If it is slower, ask which unresolved ambitions matter and focus preparation there. Never advance an unseen catastrophe merely to force a finale on session 25.

## Keeping choices fair

Give essential clues when characters investigate the relevant place or person; tests determine detail, speed, privacy, price, or danger. A failed test does not make a ledger cease to contain its text. Respect plans that bypass a prepared battle. Make enemies retreat when their stated aims require it, and let the PCs pursue, bargain, or refuse.

One scene may contain rescue, evidence, and pursuit opportunities that cannot all be completed by four people. Explain distances and costs before asking for a choice. Reward extra hands, prior preparation, a clever tool, or an unexpected use of Shaping. The point of a deadline is to make decisions matter; it is not a rule that the party must lose something every time.
