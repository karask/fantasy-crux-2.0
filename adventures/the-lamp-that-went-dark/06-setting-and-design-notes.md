# Setting and design notes

**GM only.** This record distinguishes published setting facts, adopted material from the setting proposal, and details created for this adventure. It is a preparation reference, not a player handout. The adventure lives in its own folder and does not revise or publish the source setting.

## Source hierarchy

The [published gazetteer](../../src/content/rules/gazetteer/index.md), especially **The Whitewater March**, **Vellbridge**, and **Four satellite settlements**, governs public setting facts. Vellbridge stands inland; the Crownroad crosses the White Span south of the city and continues to Gull's Rest. The Span shortens travellers' journeys, but its lamps dim, distances lurch, and a parapet weeps salt water. No numerical bridge length or journey time is asserted here.

The Council governs municipal business, House Veyran appoints the road warden, the League holds river customs, and the Synod claims the north-gate chapel. Their competing authority explains why a company can act when sending faction soldiers would invite accusations of annexation. A charter establishes legal partners and salvage arrangements; it grants no immunity. The Brazen Mule, House of the Rose Lamp, Third Measure Hall, Bent Copper, and Thistledown Station Seven retain their published functions and personalities.

The [Crownless Realms proposal](../../potential-worlds/the-crownless-realms/the-crownless-realms.md) contains a separate GM section. This adventure explicitly adopts its **The lamp that went dark**, **The ninth rod chamber**, **Vellbridge behind the public story**, and relevant **Faction dossiers** material. Those secrets are preparation material adopted from a proposal, not facts already disclosed by the published gazetteer.

## Adopted secrets and new connective detail

The proposal establishes the darkening lamp and coronation-bell disturbance, missing Survey Team Hallow, forged evacuation order, a mundane copy replacing one of nine brass route-rods, and conflicting attempts to acquire the original. Its **Twelve rumors** establishes that Hallow recovered the original after discovering the copy; they did not steal it. Its chamber description ties the replacement to a Castaine clerk, Cale Rusk's restoration order, and a Ninefold archive mark.

This adventure supplies a concrete recovery sequence: after the copy was installed, Hallow opened an outbound crate, found the original, and secured it. **Dena** is a new first name for the team's leader; **Fen Kel**, **Tessa Mar**, and the fourth member **Jory Pell** are new names. Jory surfaced separately and sold a copied notebook to Neris offscreen, elaborating the proposal's **Ready character premises**. That transaction supplies a later witness hook rather than another required investigation during this session.

Tal Orsic's exacting, exhausted personality and Survey affiliation come from **Crown Survey**. His Council-countersigned commission is an adventure detail. **Sergeant Ina Tern**, Veyran's watch representative, is new. **Maelin Voss** is the newly named Castaine clerk: she supplied the copy unknowingly under Rusk's restoration order and now seeks to impound the original. **Bren Marr** and his hired recovery crew are new. Their forceful attempt to obtain the notes creates the main combat encounter; neither Voss nor the crew knows the conspiracy.

These distinctions matter to the twist. Apparently incriminating possession does not establish theft, and genuine official paperwork can authorize a harmful act. The recovered order supports investigation of its supply chain. It does not prove that Castaine's ruler, every claimant house, or the whole Office of Continuance knowingly ordered sabotage. In the proposal's **The Claimant Houses**, only the compartmentalized Pale Cabinet engineers failures; house leaders believe its work concerns repair and emergency planning.

## Deliberate encounter adaptations

The original chamber contains two gargoyles attracted by vibration. Both remain present with their existing creature profiles unchanged, behind shut acoustic shutters and outside the session's intended route. They are not faction servants. This moves their potentially lethal confrontation out of the introductory session and gives the fight to motivated, retreat-capable hirelings. Do not add the gargoyles as a surprise second battle or quietly weaken their profiles.

The lowest door's dependence on the bridge's “short” direction comes from **The ninth rod chamber**. The local, reversible, one-way shortened circuit used in the puzzle is an adventure elaboration confined to that entrance. It teaches comparison and measurement, admits mundane investigation, and avoids demanding a particular pre-generated character's Shaping. It does not define how every door or every stretch of the Span behaves.

## Scope of success and continuation

The original rod remains a wrapped, portable anchor requiring careful custody. It is neither ordinary salvage loot nor a new spell or magic-item benefit. **The seven failing Crownworks** distinguishes mundane stabilization from true restoration: access to anchors, capable Shapers, appropriate ritual, and potentially a lasting price are still required.

The adventure's maintenance baffling can quiet dangerous noise and help clear people. It does not restore the missing enchantment. The bridge stays **closed** pending a full survey and restoration work. Completing the rescue, preserving originals and copies, settling their custody, and establishing a public account give this session a definite ending.

Expansion follows existing threads: **Blackglass undercut** contains the backward screw-jack linked to a Rusk-signed Office loan; Neris can introduce the fourth Hallow witness; and the Survey can commission formal restoration. The proposal's larger failures have different causes exploited by a coordinated conspiracy. Do not reduce them to one curse, and preserve the unresolved metaphysics in **Three theories, none confirmed**.

## NPC profile choices

The adventure now assigns fixed NPC skill bands, attributes, equipment, Talents, and Shaping cells using the published **Minor NPCs** convention. The additional defaults, skill tables, and characteristic arrays are a local presentation format; no core rule is changed. Current weapon-table damage takes precedence over the older shortsword example in Minor NPCs.

Physical descriptions, individual skill values, and the names Kett, Rill, and Orven are adventure additions. The proposed human identities of Hesta, Ancor, Irra, Jory, and Rusk are also local choices where the source does not specify them. Tal's surveying cells, Irra's medical cells, and Rusk's surveying cells are explicit individual selections consistent with their roles; faction traditions do not grant every member every listed cell. Nonhuman profiles retain their applicable innate senses or flight, and local language names follow the published gazetteer.

The NPCs are usable as written. The combat pairing still needs a playtest with completed pregen sheets; supplying exact statistics does not establish a guaranteed encounter difficulty.
