# The Lamp That Went Dark

**GM only.** A four-hour introduction for an established company of four pregenerated PCs; adjust for three or five using the encounter guidance. Use [the player brief](03-party-and-player-brief.md) for their shared history and [the NPC file](04-npcs-and-stat-design.md) for completed profiles, physical descriptions, and personalities.

## The adventure in a minute

One of the White Span's lamps dies. Everyone on the crossing hears a bell ring once, backward. The road watch closes the bridge, a Survey team is missing beneath it, and a Castaine clerk claims the surveyors stole an enchanted brass route-rod.

Chief Measurer Tal Orsic hires the company under a Council-countersigned rescue contract. They negotiate entry, confront a misleading official order, solve a door's directional puzzle, and encounter a hired crew trying to seize the missing team's records. Beyond the crew are three frightened surveyors and proof that the theft story runs backward: the original rod was replaced before the team arrived. They recovered it from the outgoing packing case.

The company can rescue the surveyors, quiet a dangerous local vibration, and preserve the evidence. At the gate, the same official who called the surveyors thieves demands custody. Her own restoration paperwork links the replacement to Cale Rusk, an Office of Continuance surveyor. She is an unwitting participant who now has reason to cooperate—or suppress her mistake.

The bridge stays closed pending a proper survey. The PCs' decision about the people, evidence, and public account settles tonight's crisis. The real repair and Rusk's wider operation are future business.

## GM truth

The White Span is a failing Crownwork, with one of its nine brass route-rods replaced by a convincing mundane copy. The original remains an active, vulnerable anchor; neither rod is a battery, and a stolen anchor does not grant its bearer control of the bridge.

Cale Rusk arranged the exchange as a supposed restoration procedure. He belongs to the Pale Cabinet, a compartment within the Office of Continuance that exploits different Crownwork failures to make emergency rule seem necessary. **Nobody encountered tonight knows that complete explanation.** Rusk does not appear, and his superiors cannot be identified from one signature.

Maelin Voss, a Castaine clerk, supplied the replacement and commissioned a recovery crew on Rusk's instructions. She believes the exchange was authorized and the returned original should have gone to a safe workshop. She knows she bypassed an independent inspection to meet his deadline. She does not know the replacement is mundane.

Team Hallow discovered that the installed ninth rod was silent. They opened the outbound packing case, found the humming original, and kept it from collection. A spatial lurch injured their leader, Dena Hallow. Three team members sheltered in the maintenance refuge; the fourth, Jory Pell, escaped with copied notes. Afraid to approach the same offices that authorized the exchange, Jory sold a copy to Aunt Neris for money to disappear. Tal has bought that copy. Jory's whereabouts are a later hook, not another missing person to locate tonight.

Bren Marr's recovery crew reached the chamber ahead of the PCs using maintenance directions supplied with their work order. They discovered Hallow's refuge and demanded the original and field notes. Hallow refused. Bren suspects a lucrative salvage dispute and is trying to take the records tube; he does not understand the anchor or know about sabotage.

The false evacuation statement originated in Rusk's office. The Post really delivered the envelope: a reliable courier does not guarantee a truthful document.

## Keep it to four hours

Start with the PCs already together, equipped, rested, and under the same charter. Each player needs only a short personal introduction. All action takes place around the north gate and its nearby maintenance works; nobody traverses the entire Span or travels to a satellite settlement.

| Table time | Scene                              | Purpose                                                        |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–0:10  | Company and the bell               | Establish relationships, stakes, and the D100 idea.            |
| 0:10–0:35  | 1. A contract with three seals     | Influence, competing interests, and a concrete rescue mission. |
| 0:35–1:05  | 2. An order that arrived too early | Evidence, Deception, and entry past the cordon.                |
| 1:05–1:35  | 3. The door that measures          | Exploration, teamwork, and the directional puzzle.             |
| 1:35–1:45  | Break                              | Keep the puzzle's solution visible for the return.             |
| 1:45–2:30  | 4. Recovery at any cost            | One short objective fight, with room for another solution.     |
| 2:30–2:55  | 5. The people called thieves       | Rescue, Healing, the twist, and a practical safety measure.    |
| 2:55–3:35  | 6. Who holds the proof?            | Return, custody negotiation, and consequences.                 |
| 3:35–3:50  | Epilogue                           | Pay, care, and one chosen continuation hook.                   |
| 3:50–4:00  | Reserve                            | Absorb rules teaching or a longer conversation.                |

These are table-time ceilings, not required durations, rounds, or a hidden game mechanic. A group may solve the puzzle in five minutes; move forward and save that time for the fight or final discussion. In the fiction it is late afternoon, and rival custodians expect a decision by dusk. Describe mounting queues and an approaching official hearing. A slow puzzle never secretly kills the missing team.

**If running late:** after ten minutes of gate discussion, Ina offers supervised rescue entry; after fifteen minutes on the puzzle, make the solution explicit through its physical indicators; do not add a pursuit after the fight. At 3:15, bring the custody discussion to the north gate immediately, including Tal if the PCs requested him. If combat reaches its budget, use Bren's stated surrender conditions as soon as they apply; do not invent extra enemies. Preserve at least fifteen minutes for the decision and epilogue.

## Scene 1: A contract with three seals

### Area A: The north-gate chapel yard

The Crownroad widens into a stone forecourt outside the Synod chapel. Seven shallow lamp niches flank its door. A waist-high toll rail divides waiting carts from the closed crossing. Beyond it, the pale bridge lamps recede into a distance that feels wrong. A low parapet sweats salt water despite the dry weather. Porters, drivers, and families argue under canvas awnings; the watch is trying to keep the center clear.

The chapel porch provides a public meeting place and later a quiet room for the injured. A side path reaches the maintenance gate without entering the long crossing. These are local works near the bridge's northern end.

Read or paraphrase:

> One bridge lamp goes black. The next burns blue. For a moment every wheel creaks twice, and a bell sounds as if someone has pulled its note back into the metal. A carter whispers, “That was the coronation bell.” A watch sergeant drops the toll rail. “Off the crossing. Walk. Leave the carts.”

Let each player name one practical thing they do. Helping someone away from the rail, calming a child, or steadying a cart succeeds without a test. If a player attempts something uncertain, state its stakes and use one appropriate skill test; do not make the introduction a hazard gauntlet.

Tal Orsic knows the Last Wagon Company by its charter and its refusal to abandon wounded companions. He approaches them in the porch. His contract carries the March Council's countersignature; he needs an independent rescue company because sending one faction's soldiers below would create a jurisdiction dispute.

**The offer:** 30 SP per PC, half now and half when the company returns with an honest report and accounts for the missing people as far as reasonably possible. Rescue-related treatment at the House of the Rose Lamp is covered. This is an adventure-specific contract, not a general wage rule. Named Crownwork components belong in documented public custody and are excluded from salvage. No reward depends on killing, recovering a particular object, or blaming a faction.

Give [Handout 1](02-handouts.md#handout-1-the-companys-contract). Tal supplies two Healing Kits, shuttered lamps and fuel, chalk, cord, charcoal, paper, and a folded copy of Jory's note. He identifies Dena, Fen, and Tessa as the three believed below; Jory is already out. Explain that all three may need rescue, not arrest.

### Influence with something to win

Ina Tern, the Veyran-appointed watch's sergeant at this gate, accepts the rescue contract but wants to send an armed watch escort. Tal objects: the other powers would treat soldiers entering the anchor chamber as a seizure. Her concern is practical—who stops the PCs reopening a dangerous bridge or vanishing with public property?

The company can persuade Ina to grant **unescorted access and keep the stairs clear for their return**. A promise to maintain the closure, identify recovered property in writing, and bring witnesses back addresses her concerns; if freely accepted, it works without a roll. If the terms remain uncertain or the PCs ask for more discretion, use Influence opposed by Ina's Persistence. Concrete safeguards or another PC's meaningful help can justify `+1B` under the ordinary rules.

- **Win:** Ina grants the concession, lends a litter, and agrees to convene a witnessed hearing when they return.
- **Lose:** she still permits the rescue but assigns a watch witness to wait at the top of the maintenance stair and record outgoing property. This witness does not accompany the expedition, make decisions for it, or join combat. The company has less privacy, not a failed adventure.
- **Fumble:** Ina also requires an inventory before departure, costing fictional time and revealing obvious equipment. It does not revoke the contract.

Maelin Voss listens nearby with a leather document case. She offers an additional **20 SP to the company**, payable on lawful delivery of the “stolen ninth route-rod” to her sole custody. This is an optional rival offer, not part of Tal's fee. She openly admits her office arranged a replacement for scheduled restoration. She says Hallow intercepted the returned original, leaving it unaccounted for. If asked why she does not fetch it herself, she points to the closed maintenance gate and her hired specialists below.

Her line: “You bring the people. I can take responsibility for the object.” She is useful, courteous, and wrong about the most important fact. Do not play her as obviously evil.

## Scene 2: An order that arrived too early

### Area B: The cordon and service gate

A timber wicket and iron maintenance gate sit between the chapel wall and a broad pier. The stair behind them slopes under the road. A shallow bench holds sign-in sheets, delivery receipts, a shuttered lamp, and the gate keys. A cloth screen shelters the same bench from the queue, leaving a private corner for argument. Everything the players need is here; a visit to the Bent Copper or Post tower would consume the session without improving the mystery.

An exhausted Post courier delivers a genuine envelope containing [Handout 2](02-handouts.md#handout-2-the-evacuation-order). Voss places the paper on the bench and asks Ina to suspend rescue entry: it says all Survey personnel were evacuated at the second afternoon bell, and reserves access for an Office recovery team. Ina does **not** immediately discard a live rescue contract; she pauses to compare records.

Give the company Tal's copied notebook passage and the gate record, [Handout 3](02-handouts.md#handout-3-two-records). The times contradict the order: a fresh help signal from three people was recorded at the third afternoon bell, after the claimed evacuation. The gate record also reports nobody emerging since Jory's departure. These facts are automatic when read aloud or examined. No Perception test is needed to receive or understand the essential clue.

The evacuation statement is false; its conflicting access demand exceeds Voss's current local authority. Its authentic seal alone does not make a rescue unnecessary. The contradiction shows a bad order, **not yet who falsified it**.

### A useful chance to deceive

Voss now asks to copy the company's written route notes and watch where Tal's original packet goes. The players can decide how much to reveal. Offer the following situations through her behavior rather than telling them to roll a particular skill:

- **Conceal the real notes.** One PC substitutes a plausible but uninformative sketch of the upper maintenance stair while another discusses the order. Paper, charcoal, and a quiet minute are available. Use Deception opposed by Voss's Perception if she scrutinizes the sketch or watches the switch. A convincing distraction can provide meaningful assistance. On a win, she believes she copied the relevant route notes; on a loss, she notices the trick and asks Ina to log the actual packet. She does not acquire it automatically.
- **Bluff access.** Claim the Council has already superseded the recovery restriction, or pose as the inspection detail Voss expects. Use Deception opposed by Ina's Perception for the false credential or disguise. On a win, enter before the custody question is settled; on a loss, Ina asks for the actual contract and grants the supervised access from Scene 1 once she sees the rescue evidence.
- **Quiet entry.** Use the screen and a distraction to slip through while the maintenance gate is opened for equipment. If the entire party must pass unnoticed, make one group Deception test using the weakest participant against the watch's best Perception. On a loss, the watch stops and identifies them; their contract still allows the lawful rescue route. Avoid repeated rolls for each guard or PC.
- **Open challenge.** Present the two records and insist on rescue. Ina permits entry without a roll once the contradiction is established; negotiate only extra concessions. The company may also deliberately show Voss its true notes, gaining transparency at the price of privacy.

Deception is useful, not mandatory. The false order itself demonstrates how documents and misdirection can shape the situation if nobody chooses to deceive. Do not take a lawful, successful approach away just to obtain a skill demonstration.

**Optional extra information:** Perception of the overwritten date or relevant Lore about official forms reveals the order reused a standard restoration template. This helps a later challenge to its reliability but never names Rusk as the culprit by itself.

Before the company descends, Voss warns, “My people are retrieving Survey property. Please identify yourselves.” She has already supplied Bren's crew with maintenance instructions; the PCs may obtain her copy by agreement, bluff, or a successful theft. If they do, that earned preparation gives the complete puzzle solution. Skip solving it again and let the environment demonstrate the shortcut.

## Scene 3: The door that measures

### Area C: The three-era stair

The upper steps are recent brick, the middle flight patched medieval stone, and the bottom pale imperial blocks with no visible mortar. The ceiling is low enough to stoop under two braces. Brass gauge-lines appear doubled in lamplight. Old repair sockets give easy handholds; steady walking needs no Athletics roll.

The stair ends in the Gate landing. There is no flooded passage, mandatory jump, or long descent to drain beginner resources. A return trip takes only a few minutes once the route is known. Chalk left by Hallow reads, “Listen before opening anything.”

### Area D: The counting walk

A narrow corridor loops around a massive bridge pier and returns to the same Gate landing. Three broad stations along it are labeled **GATE**, **LAMP**, and **RIVER**. Starting from Gate, one entrance reaches Lamp first; the other reaches River first. Each station has a recognizable carved symbol beside the writing, so language or literacy is not a barrier.

The maintenance door is in the outer wall at Gate. Its handle turns freely but does not catch. A short brass measuring chain hangs on a peg beside it. Marks along the floor let different-sized travelers compare the same distances. No wall shifts or crushes anyone.

Read or paraphrase:

> The lintel reads, “The long road accounts. The short road admits.” Both corridors bend around the same pier. Somebody has written thirty beside one chalk arrow and eighteen beside the other, then crossed out neither. From behind the door comes the knock of wood on wood.

Give [Handout 4](02-handouts.md#handout-4-the-counting-door). Tell players the physical layout and both entrances plainly; they should solve the rule, not guess your description.

### Puzzle: approach by the short circuit

**Solution:** depart Gate toward Lamp, continue to River, then return to Gate. This is the shortened direction. At the return threshold the latch clicks and the door opens normally. Two small labeled indicators beside the latch record passage through Lamp and then River; both must register before the return to Gate. Walking partway toward River and doubling back does not register a circuit. The indicators visibly explain this requirement, rather than hiding it from an inventive player. The opposite circuit does not engage the latch. The successful approach opens the door for everyone; holding or wedging it allows the whole party and later a litter through. There is no individual attunement, enforced party split, or timed dexterity challenge.

The enchanted local route measures eighteen standard paces in the sequence **Gate → Lamp → River → Gate**, and thirty in reverse. The labels on the floor measure distance, not actual footfalls; a pixie, a tall traveler, and a cord all reveal the same asymmetry. The one-way difference is a local expression of the Span's shortening, not permission to teleport or shorten any future journey.

### What investigation reveals

- Walking either complete circuit or laying out the chain gives its exact length automatically. Tell them the number; do not ask the players to estimate it from prose.
- Examining the latch shows a small open-hand indicator that points toward the return from River. Its travel-polished edge and Hallow's chalk marks can be seen without a test.
- Looking at both entrances reveals the floor symbols in the two different orders. The inscription refers to the shorter **journey**, not the shortest straight line to the door.
- An Engineering test, if the PC wants a quick technical diagnosis, recognizes that the chain and latch respond to the direction of approach. Success supplies the instruction “complete the short circuit, then open the door”; failure spends a few fictional minutes and still leaves all visible clues available.
- An appropriate, legal `Scry·Paths` can reveal the shorter approach according to its actual effect. Sense Magic alone can identify active magic and its Form, not the password, history, or solution. No pregen requires this cell.

**Other solutions:** accept deliberate measurement, tracking the previous crew's chalk, or simply trying both directions. If a PC proposes bypassing the mundane latch, tools and a few minutes allow a Mechanisms test; success opens it, failure leaves it intact and teaches which return indicator engages it. Do not invent an opposed creator statistic for a maintenance catch not designed to resist tampering. Destructive work on the surrounding Crownwork is unnecessary and Tal's warning makes its risk plain.

**Hints:** after roughly eight minutes, emphasize that both routes return to Gate; after twelve, give both measured lengths whether or not a player suggested the chain; after fifteen, the latch visibly advances as someone approaches from River, making the required circuit explicit. The team may also call through the door and hear Fen shout the sequence. Asking for help is a valid solution.

Wrong circuits cost only a little fictional time. They do not deal damage, erase clues, or summon monsters.

## Scene 4: Recovery at any cost

### Area E: The recovery gallery

Beyond the counting door lies a dry rectangular work gallery, about twelve metres long and eight across. The entry opens into one short end; Hallow's barred refuge door occupies the opposite wall. Two waist-high stone inspection benches break up the middle, leaving two clear passages about three metres wide. Treat a bench as partial cover only when it actually obscures the target. A hanging canvas screen at the far end can conceal a person but will not stop a missile. A side opening, presently closed by a wooden maintenance shutter, leads into the rod chamber.

A padded timber work-frame stands against the left wall near the entry. Its lever lowers a cloth-lined **acoustic baffle**, cutting vibration through the local maintenance gallery. This is ordinary equipment, not a magic suppressor. A person beside it can work its lever as a Combat Action. Operating it is reliable; reaching it while opposed is the challenge. A return hatch at the refuge is a narrow document-and-voice slot, too small to crawl through.

The counting door can be held open and the stair is the clear retreat route. There is no sheer drop. Sealed overhead vent shutters bear claw marks; behind these, outside tonight's route, are the GM seed's two gargoyles. The maintained shutters isolate their roosts. They do not join the fight or punish ordinary combat noise. Opening those unrelated shutters reveals fresh scratches and movement: Tal's job needs none of them opened, and the beasts are a later expedition threat. Use their actual creature profiles if the party deliberately pursues that danger; do not reskin them as weak starter opponents.

### The moment of contact

Bren and his crew stand near the far bench. Let the PCs identify themselves or call toward the refuge. Tessa brings a capped brass records tube to the hatch to show the arriving rescuers the team's Survey seal. Its carrying strap hangs outside; Bren steps in and catches it before she can withdraw. Tessa still holds the tube inside, protected by the hatch. If the PCs instead arrive covertly or announce an agreement already accepted by both sides, let that preparation change or prevent the seizure. This is a visible contest over property, not a surprise attack on an unaware party.

Read or paraphrase:

> “That is our record,” says a woman's voice behind the door. A broad-shouldered man has one fist around a tube's strap and a club in the other. “And this,” he says, tapping the stamped paper on his coat, “is a recovery order.” He looks at your charter marks. “Good. Witnesses. Help us finish before the bridge moves again.”

Give the demand, allow a short response, and make his intentions clear. Bren offers to leave the injured team behind for the party if he gets the tube and original rod. Hallow refuses to unbar the door for a private recovery crew. Bren's contract permits recovery work, **not arrest or overriding the Council's rescue contract**.

If the PCs step in to protect the hatch, Bren orders his crew to push them back and seize the tube. Begin Combat Order openly. He has made a bad decision under pressure; he is not compelled to fight to the death. A solid settlement—such as witnessed joint custody and payment of his ordinary fee by Voss—can avert combat if the PCs establish it before blows. Accept that success. The default scene is a fight, but the game also rewards avoiding one.

### Run an objective fight

**For four PCs:** Bren, Kett, and Rill. For three PCs, Bren and Kett; for five, add Orven. Use the completed [NPC profiles](04-npcs-and-stat-design.md). Bren has Close Combat 50%, Dodge 40%, HP 10, MWL 5, leather 2 AP, and Order 7. Each hireling has Close Combat 40%, Dodge 30%, HP 10, MWL 5, no armour, and Order 10. All use one-handed clubs for `1D6` damage, DM `+0`, DEX 10, and Movement 15 m. Do not add unseen reinforcements, high-damage attacks, or a hidden Shaper.

**Bren's objective:** get the tube, then leave with a claim on the original. **The company's likely objective:** get the crew away from the refuge and open a safe rescue route. Nobody needs to kill everyone or destroy the chamber.

- **First round:** if still holding the strap on his turn, Bren spends his Combat Action on one Athletics 50% test opposed by Tessa's Athletics 20%. A win pulls the tube through into his possession; a loss lets Tessa wrench it inside and shut the hatch. Tessa may instead let go to avoid the contest. No result drags or injures her through the narrow opening. This one contest settles possession; do not run repeated tug rolls. If the PCs intervene before his turn, resolve their declared actions first. Each hireling tries to block a passage and attacks intervening armed PCs normally. One weapon hit can be serious even with this equipment. Holding the strap grants Bren no extra actions.
- **Following rounds:** Bren tries to leave with a won tube, or demands a custody agreement if Tessa retained it. He uses ordinary movement or Withdraw as appropriate, not a free escape. A hireling covers him while another tries to reach the door. Enemies attack conscious combatants; they do not finish fallen people, damage the anchor, Charge, or improvise Talent-only manoeuvres.
- **Useful actions:** attack and defend; take cover; move to guard the hatch; turn the baffle lever; use a skill for a plausible five-second task; help a wounded person; attempt ordinary grappling by its rules. Cutting the exposed strap with a suitable readied blade takes a Combat Action if unopposed, or requires first dealing with Bren's interference; Tessa then retains the tube. It is not a free disarm or damage effect.
- **The baffle:** once lowered, the room stops humming and conversation becomes easy. If the crew releases its claim, the strap is freed, or Tessa wins the pull, she can pass the tube to a PC who controls the hatch. Lowering the baffle does not resolve contested possession. It gives a practical non-attack contribution, without stunning the crew or draining magic.

Use the fight to teach actions, movement, a defender's limited Reactions, and armour. Explain that an attack and a Dodge or Parry use the combat rules, not the general opposed-test tie-breaking procedure. A club is not automatically nonlethal; Subdue requires its Talent. Introduce Hero Points before the first dangerous hit, not after a player has already accepted an avoidable Major Wound.

### Morale, surrender, and setbacks

Bren voluntarily calls for a halt when one of his crew goes down with a serious injury, when the PCs control both the tube and an open refuge door, or when proof makes his attempted exclusive seizure indefensible. His limited recovery authority remains genuine. The surviving hirelings follow him. He offers his order as evidence in exchange for being allowed to carry out injured companions. Accepting surrender ends the fight; do not continue initiative just to consume remaining HP.

A PC can also use the standard combat Intimidate action. Apply its enemy-condition modifiers and the special restriction on forcing full-strength enemies to surrender. Bren choosing to bargain because his job is no longer viable is separate from a forced Intimidate result.

If the PCs retreat, Tessa retains the tube if she won the contest; if Bren won it, he takes it upstairs. The refuge remains barred and Bren cannot seize the original rod through a document hatch. The company can return with Ina as a neutral witness; her presence prompts a joint-custody settlement and a clean rescue, costing the company privacy and influence over the first official account. Do not run the entire combat again.

If the PCs lose the fight, Bren accepts surrender, leaves them alive, and demands the tube before letting the rescue proceed. If Hallow's team still holds it, they yield it to protect the company. He takes it upstairs; Hallow still has the original rod and her testimony, and Tal has the copied notebook. The final negotiation becomes recovery of separated evidence, not a campaign-ending failure. Resolve any Bleeding or Dying immediately using the rules; an NPC's intent to spare someone does not stabilize them by itself. Fen has **Healing 30%** and a Healing Kit with **four uses remaining**; use it if all PC healers are incapacitated, without guaranteeing a successful roll.

## Scene 5: The people called thieves

### Area F: The rod chamber

The wooden shutter opens onto a circular inspection chamber, roughly ten metres across, wrapped around a solid central pier. Nine sockets stand along its outer wall. Eight seated brass rods hum at slightly different pitches. The ninth looks newly polished and produces a dull tap when lightly touched. A label attached to its socket reads “RESTORATION EXCHANGE—RUSK.”

A low rail separates the walking aisle from the sockets. An open, empty padded packing case stands beside it, with a delivery docket caught under its lid. The air tastes of copper, and straight brass edges appear doubled. These are evidence of a strange, failing place, not a free diagnosis of every enchantment.

### Area G: The maintenance refuge

The refuge opens from the far end of the recovery gallery. It contains a water crock, a low cot, three stools, a tool cupboard, and a dry worktable. Its stout door bars from inside. Dena sits on the cot with a splinted leg; Fen has improvised a sling to keep it still. Tessa holds the records tube when it has not been seized. A cloth-wrapped brass rod lies in its padded carrying cradle under the table. It hums softly through the cloth.

Dena has **4 of 12 HP, MWL 6**, and is already Wounded and stable, not Bleeding or Dying. Nobody is on an undisclosed medical countdown. She can talk, but needs a litter or assistance to get upstairs. The task is safe extraction and proper care, not another combat. Opening the refuge after winning the crew's withdrawal needs no roll.

### Let the players discover the reversal

Give [Handout 5](02-handouts.md#handout-5-the-exchange-record) and let the party compare it with the earlier claims before Dena explains everything. The document dates the replacement before Hallow's inspection. The installed copy does not hum; the recovered original does. The outgoing case describes returning the original under the same restoration order. Those three clues independently challenge the accusation.

Dena's account:

> “They told you we stole it? We found a dead copy already in the socket and the real one boxed for collection. We stopped the collection. I would rather be charged with theft than sign my name under that bridge.”

She admits opening the case and withholding Survey property from the authorized collection. She did **not** remove the installed ninth rod. She will not claim certainty about Rusk's wider motive or Voss's intent.

Make these facts available without a roll:

1. The substitution preceded the team arriving; the replacement is mundane.
2. The real rod was in the outbound case, not stolen from an intact working bridge by Hallow.
3. Rusk's signed order instructed the exchange. Voss released the material without the independent inspection noted on the form.
4. The replacement bears a Ninefold archive mark. That mark alone proves neither a Nine Warrens plot nor where the object was made.

The practical difference between the rods is observable here; no PC must cast to believe an NPC. A Shaper may use their normal magic to learn more, but an active anchor's presence does not reveal its history or who lied. Any `Scry·Flesh` on Dena is about bodily condition, not reading memories or establishing honesty.

### What the company can fix tonight

Fen asks for help lowering the gallery's baffle, wedging its frame, and padding the loose mundane inspection cover. This damps local acoustic vibration and makes the evacuation steadier. It does **not** reinstall the rod, restore distance-shortening, or stabilize the whole Span. With tools and a few quiet minutes, competent assistance completes it without a roll. If the party insists on doing it during continuing conflict or other real pressure, one Engineering test resolves the job; on failure, leave the baffle down, clear people, and isolate the gallery until a work crew returns.

Fen explicitly warns: “Do not put it back because it fits.” Restoring any rod without a full survey risks a spatial lurch. Tal's instructions are to leave the seated copy untouched, document it, and recover the already portable original in its existing padded cradle with Hallow's guidance. The last move does not promise zero magical risk; for tonight's extraction, careful ordinary handling is sufficient and causes no scripted backlash. Carrying it gives no control over the Crownwork.

If the PCs prefer to leave the original in place, seal the refuge with a written inventory and bring the people and records out. That is a legitimate outcome; custody of the room becomes the finale's issue. If they propose an actual magical repair, explain the missing survey and ritual requirements before they commit resources. Record their proposal as a follow-up contract. Do not resolve a permanent Crownwork restoration with one Engineering or Shaping roll.

### Care and evidence

Use a kit for genuine injuries suffered in play. Ordinary post-encounter Healing restores HP only under its normal conditions; a Major Wound needs Surgery or a qualifying magical treatment. Dena's old injury offers a chance to examine and help a patient, not an excuse to demand an emergency roll. A PC with a legal `Alter·Flesh` working may treat her under the Shaping rules and pay its costs. Mundane transport to Doctor Ancor is always available.

Take rubbings of the seated copy's mark and copy the exchange order; Tessa has paper and charcoal. Copying legible evidence needs no test. Dena, Fen, and Tessa can each give testimony even if one document is lost. Nobody has to entrust every original to one custodian.

## Scene 6: Who holds the proof?

Return directly to the chapel yard. The counting door can be opened normally from inside; the solved mechanism does not create a second obstacle. Carrying the litter slowly needs no Athletics test. Ina clears a route and places the injured in the porch. Describe grateful relatives and angry merchants: the rescue mattered, and the closure has a cost.

Tal and Voss meet the company at the same public bench. If Tal has not been summoned, the company's return is visible from the chapel and brings him over. No distant messenger or instant Post service is required. Bren, if he left with records or was promised a hearing, is here too.

### The decision has three parts

Ask the players, as the NPCs, for a concrete proposal:

1. **People:** do the surveyors go to the Rose Lamp under rescue protection, or does someone insist on treating them as suspects? Ina accepts treatment first and any formal complaint later when shown the contract and witnesses.

### Voss's dilemma

Voss starts by demanding the rod and original records for Castaine custody. Her genuine fear is that another faction will hide them, then blame her alone. Once shown her release entry and the evidence that the installed rod is mundane, she goes quiet. She now understands she helped install a false component. She can identify Rusk's signature and offer her own copy of the order.

She can be persuaded to accept joint custody if the company offers her a place in the record and distinguishes her acknowledged bypass from a proven sabotage conspiracy. If contested, use Influence against her Influence. Documentary proof plus a credible proposal may justify `+1B`; make one decisive test for the disputed concession, not one for each NPC sentence.

- **Win:** Voss signs a shared inventory and supplies her order. She becomes a chastened, useful future contact.
- **Lose:** she refuses to sign or surrender her separate copy. Ina still recognizes the rescue contract and existing evidence. Voss lodges a formal custody challenge, creating a future hearing rather than confiscating everything by fiat.
- **Expose her publicly:** the party can truthfully identify her role without a roll. Reckless claims beyond the evidence make future allies less trusting. A public attack may make Voss an adversary even when the PCs keep custody.
- **Mislead her:** hiding a duplicate or substituting a mundane document uses Deception against her Perception when scrutiny makes it uncertain. Failure exposes that particular trick and weakens trust; it does not alter what the rods or records show. A fake rod does not survive competent examination automatically.

Tal is grateful but would prefer the wider readings kept within the Survey. If asked whether he can guarantee their publication, he admits he cannot bind Central Measure. This adds a choice rather than revealing a second villain. He accepts shared copies and a limited public warning when the company insists that a rescue cannot end with the same evidence disappearing again.
