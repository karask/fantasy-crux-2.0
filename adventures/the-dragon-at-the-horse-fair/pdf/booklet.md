@@page|PREPARATION|Run this tonight

An introductory **Fantasy Crux 2.0 Beta** adventure in Rillmarket, for **3-5 PCs** and one session of up to **four hours**. Four PCs are recommended.

The **Last Wagon Company** already forms a party. Its background and eight complete pregenerated character sheets are a **separate download: Last Wagon Company - Player Packet**. Use one character per player; Mara, Pebb, Seris and Tamsin make a useful starting group. No particular specialist or spell is required.

The company has delivered tack for Hesta Coil to the horse fair. The work is finished and supper paid for when a visiting handler's demonstration goes wrong.

**The job:** secure Copperback, recover her stolen clutch and make the road safe. Serrik offers **25 SP per PC**, reasonable injury care and the loan of **two full Healing Kits, rope and her familiar nose-band**. Award **4 IP** for resolving the theft and danger, including through negotiation.

## Session budget

Allow roughly **30 minutes at the fair**, **60 investigating and following the trail**, a **10-minute break**, **75 confronting the thieves and retrieving the case**, and **40 for the return and consequences**. The remaining **25 minutes** are a reserve. Successful shortcuts can finish the session earlier.

The maps and NPC profiles are for the GM. Give players the **hoist instructions** when they examine the covered board beside the winch, and the **buyer's note** when they open the recovered case. Ada's testimony is spoken; there is no witness-report handout.

## Gazetteer connections

Rillmarket, west of Vellbridge, has about **1,000 permanent residents**, a palisade and a thriving grain and horse trade. Its rumored drainage route to a lost imperial storehouse is real in the GM gazetteer. This adventure adds an orchard-side loading bay on that network. Serrik's giant-lizard caravan trade comes from the **Saffron Reach**. The blocked lower passage remains a hook for another expedition.

@@section|GM TRUTH|The theft behind the escape

**Copperback is a trained giant lizard.** The auctioneer calls her a dragon to draw a crowd. Her handler, **Serrik**, is demonstrating caravan work. Four eggs travel in a straw-lined case in his nesting wagon.

**Thirty minutes before play begins**, broker **Venn Rook** helped cart driver **Dask Pellin** remove the case under blue canvas. Dask took the orchard road to the old loading yard and lowered it into the freight bay for a buyer collecting after dusk. Venn stayed beside the sale ring to watch Serrik and divert inquiries. The cart and case are already at the yard when play starts.

At the end of the demonstration Copperback finds her nesting compartment empty. She breaks a worn strap, overturns Ada's stall and follows spilled nest straw toward the west gate. Serrik checks the wagon and announces the missing eggs immediately.

Dask discovers rubble blocking the buyer's underground approach. She waits for Venn to agree on a new collection point. She knows how to use the hoist and has left its brake engaged. If he never returns, she abandons the job at dusk.

**If Copperback is still at the trough**, anyone bringing the egg case down the road toward her will attract her at close range through its familiar scent. This applies to thieves trying to leave and to rescuers carrying it back. She cannot see into the shaft or detect the case through solid stone. People leaving without it can pass at a distance. If the party has secured her elsewhere, she does not obstruct the thieves' route.

**Evidence arrives in stages:** Serrik identifies the missing clutch; Ada saw the cart; tracks and discarded nest lining indicate its route. The eggs are seen only after the recovered case is opened. They then confirm Serrik's account. Venn's false directions and Ada's identification expose the apparently helpful broker's involvement.

## Follow the choices

**Catch Copperback early:** Serrik keeps her secured at that location. Continue with the egg recovery and reunite them there.

**Go straight to the yard:** the PCs can arrive before Venn. Dask, the labeled case and the buyer's note provide information they missed at the fair. Persuading Dask also obtains her help with the hoist.

**Let Venn get ahead:** once the party leaves or dismisses him, he takes the orchard road to Dask. If he reaches her and knows Copperback is secured elsewhere, they need ten minutes to raise and load the case, then depart along that same road. An interception uses the roadside instead of the yard; the case is on the cart and the hoist scene is unnecessary. If Copperback still blocks the route, they wait or flee without the eggs when threatened.

Travel from fair to trough is **800 m / 15 minutes walking**. The pedestrian culvert takes **2 minutes**; the broad road around the ridge takes **4**. Advance the thieves' plans according to that travel and the party's actions.

@@section|AT THE FAIR|The demonstration goes wrong

> Grain dust hangs in the warm air. Horses stamp beside a rope sale ring. Stalls crowd the lane with bread, harness leather and bright scraps of cloth. A fiddle struggles against shouted prices, while carts edge through gaps in the crowd.

Give players a few moments to absorb the scene and settle into the marketplace. Let any conversation or ordinary browsing arise naturally. Then draw their attention to the demonstration:

> "Descended from the dragons of the burning east!" calls an auctioneer. Inside the rails, a copper-scaled lizard turns beside a handler in a canvas waistcoat. He opens a covered wagon and gives a practiced signal. The animal thrusts her head into the wagon, pulls back and noses scattered straw. A strap snaps. A grain stall tips as she pushes through the rails and heads for the gate, ignoring a tethered goat.

**Ada Mell** is caught under the fallen stall frame. Teamwork frees her without a test; a hurried solo attempt uses Athletics, with failure costing time. Nearby fair workers help if the whole party pursues. PCs can also clear the lane or calm frightened horses.

**Serrik** checks the wagon: "Her eggs! Someone has taken her eggs!" He offers the rescue terms on page 2 and accompanies the company, unless staying with a Copperback they have already secured.

## Fairwarden Jessa Fen

Jessa is the town's appointed **market peace officer**. She handles theft, disputes, dangerous animals and crowd safety during the fair, detains suspects and brings cases before the claimant court. Her jurisdiction includes the nearby approach roads. Her immediate staff are **two watch assistants and two local hunters hired for livestock trouble**.

She acts at once: the assistants clear the gate and help casualties; the hunters go down the orchard road to warn travelers and move livestock away; Jessa questions witnesses about the stolen case. She asks the available company to accompany Serrik because he needs several hands to handle Copperback while her staff deal with the scattered crowd and road. She is requesting help, not granting the PCs permission to investigate.

**Influence:** a practical live-capture plan can persuade her directly. If her agreement is uncertain, use Influence vs **Persistence 43%**. Success has the hunters approach quietly under Serrik's signals and offer hands for ropes. Failure leaves them keeping travelers back with bows ready until the animal is secured; they do not obstruct the PCs or shoot without an immediate threat. If the party brings evidence against Venn, Jessa detains and questions him. If asked to accompany an arrest at the yard, she can follow once the injured and gate are in her assistants' care. Apply the time this takes and any help actually gained.

## Ada's spoken account

Once freed, Ada answers questions or calls after the rescuers:

> "Half an hour ago, a little cart took the orchard road. Blue canvas over a case, straw poking out. One wheel knocked every turn. That broker in the mustard coat told me it was feed and to mind my stall."

She points out Venn if he is present. She does not write a report or produce a document. The cart's wheel marks remain a lead if the PCs leave before speaking to her.

@@section|THE WAGON LANE|Venn's false directions

This encounter happens **immediately after the escape**, beside Serrik's wagon behind the sale ring, if the PCs pause to question Serrik or inspect the theft. Venn has been here throughout. He saw Copperback leave through the west gate, but buildings hide the road fork beyond it. He approaches when he hears the company offer help; he has not just entered town.

> A neatly dressed man in a mustard riding coat steps across the churned wagon lane. "She's taken the marsh road. Follow the ditch south and you'll find her."

**He is inventing the turn at the fork** to divert pursuit from Dask. If challenged about his view, he claims to have glimpsed it between wagons. If Copperback has already been caught, omit that false sighting: he instead says the driver told him Serrik had authorized a feed delivery, attempting to distance himself from the theft.

The empty compartment and straw need no Perception test. Serrik names what is missing. Ada identifies Venn's involvement; the cart's missing rim stud leaves a repeated gap in its wheel marks, leading to the orchard road. At the fork those tracks contradict his direction. A successful Perception test notices him checking who is listening before answering.

**Deceive him:** pretend to follow the marsh road, then double back out of his sight. Use Deception vs **Perception 43%**. Success makes him wait at the fair until convinced they have gone, giving them a lead to the yard. Failure makes him suspicious: he leaves promptly to warn Dask. He never volunteers a secret meeting place. PCs can also follow him if they prefer, contesting Deception against his Perception when concealment matters.

**Confront or bargain:** he denies knowing the case's contents. Influence vs **Persistence 37%** can obtain cooperation with a credible offer to accept surrender and report it fairly. Jessa decides custody; the company cannot promise immunity. Detention removes him from the yard encounter.

If the PCs rush straight through the gate, skip this conversation. Venn notices their departure and follows later; he does not appear ahead of them without time to travel.

@@section|THE ORCHARD|At the watering trough

> Apple branches lean over a shallow ditch. Torn harness threads catch on the thorns, and a broad tail groove follows the cart ruts. Beside a stone trough, Copperback worries a strap snagged in a hedge. Sheep graze farther off; she pays them no attention.

Omit the animal and harness details if she was secured earlier. The cart trail remains clear either way. A scrap of canvas and nest straw lie beside the hedge; Serrik recognizes them. One Natural Lore test can find a shortcut across dry ground; failure adds ten minutes following the visible ruts instead.

At the trough, Serrik can use familiar signals while someone cuts the snagged strap. Water, space and cleared livestock help; Natural Lore identifies the same precautions if he is absent. She hisses if crowded, giving people time to step back.

The **low culvert** reaches the yard through the ridge and admits crouching people only. The **broad road** curves around it and admits the cart or lizard. Bringing Copperback by that road may frighten the thieves into abandoning the case. She follows familiar scent and Serrik's routine, not commands to attack.

Hunters who arrived first are moving travelers and sheep out of danger. Once someone safely tends Copperback, a hunter can help with an arrest or rescue if asked. They are not there to monitor the company.

@@section|THE LOADING YARD|The thieves

Read this from either entrance; adjust the arch to the gate if needed:

> Grass pushes through cracked paving. A small cart stands by a broad gate. Two hounds raise their heads from a hitch post. A timber hoist straddles a railed opening, its chains descending out of sight. The woman beside the winch looks toward you and reaches for the dog leads.

**From the entrance:** the PCs see Dask, the dogs, cart, hoist frame and upper platform. They cannot yet see the case. **At the shaft rail:** looking down reveals the blue-covered case on the lower platform. Its freight tag reads "Load: 6 units." Looking toward the side of the bay also reveals the blocked passage. The eggs themselves remain inside the closed case.

Dask waits for Venn after discovering the blockage. If warned, she watches from behind the cart. Venn is present only if his actual movements brought him here; he stays near the gate.

## Resolve the confrontation

Venn offers a share of the buyer's fee to leave. Dask prefers a surrender that keeps her dogs alive. Use Influence against **Venn 37% / Dask 32% Persistence** for an uncertain concession. Deception against **Venn 43% / Dask 44% / hounds 40% Perception** can enable a quiet approach or distraction. Cooperative Dask recalls the dogs and explains the hoist.

If violence starts, Dask spends a Combat Action at the hitch post to release the hounds. The thieves fight for an exit. **Venn flees when Wounded or Dask yields; Dask yields when Wounded, a dog is badly hurt, or Venn deserts her.** Dogs retreat when Wounded and cease pursuit outside the yard if their handler falls.

For four PCs, use at most Venn, Dask and two hounds. For three, use Dask and one hound, with Venn keeping clear; for five, add a third hound. Remove anyone already detained, delayed or persuaded. Jessa or a hunter who genuinely joined the party can assist.

The cart and walls provide cover; the shaft has a waist-high rail. If the party withdraws, the thieves abandon the eggs when Copperback blocks the road, or use the ten-minute loading plan if it is clear. Follow actual injuries and surrender outcomes.

@@section|THE FREIGHT HOIST|Recovering the case

This was an **imperial storehouse loading point**, used to transfer grain sacks and supply crates between carts above and porters in the passage below. Local farmers maintained its timberwork long after the storehouse was abandoned. The buyer's advance porter serviced it last week, before fresh masonry fell across the lower passage. Worn stone, patched beams, greased gears and new rope repairs make that history visible.

It is a **hand-cranked cargo hoist**, with two identical flat wooden platforms on guides. Loose ballast reduces the effort needed at the winch. A mechanical balance linkage locks the brake when the loads differ by more than one freight unit. There are no special sockets: **1, 2, 4 and 8 are the weights of movable stone blocks; 6 is the total weight written on Serrik's case**, including its contents.

**The instruction handout is physically here:** lift the little weather cover on the board clipped to the winch post at the upper landing. Beneath it is the porter's replacement parchment instruction sheet. It describes general operation, not the stolen cargo. The party learns the current six-unit load from the case's tag when looking down from the rail; Dask or Serrik can also identify its weight.

Dask left all four blocks above (total **15**) to hold the brake locked over the **6-unit** case below. Remove whole blocks onto the landing, then crank the winch from there. The loaded upper platform descends as the case rises six metres.

| Leave on the upper deck | Load | Difference from the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 and 4** | 5 | 1 - brake releases |
| **2 and 4** | 6 | 0 - brake releases |
| **1, 2 and 4** | 7 | 1 - brake releases |

The 8 block must be removed. Wrong loads keep the lever locked; they do not drop the case. The blocks have handles and skids, so careful movement needs no test. Mechanisms success identifies a solution; failure still identifies the oversized 8. Give a direct hint if players stall. Dask can explain immediately after cooperating.

A rope anchored to the sound frame, measured substitute ballast or suitable Shaping can recover the case instead. It measures **1.2 x 0.8 x 0.65 m** and two people carry it safely. It is one human-sized object for Reach 0; magical range must span six metres and the effect must cover the full movement. Extra Duration does not repeat a single movement effect.

Once raised, the case can be opened: **four leathery eggs** rest in straw, with the **buyer's note** tucked under the lid. Give that handout now. Return by the broad cart road.

@@section|RETURN & CONSEQUENCES|Bringing Copperback home

@@art|04-orchard-reunion.png|A handover at the trough; use the fair or yard if Copperback is there instead.

Set the case down on clear ground and give Copperback room to approach. Serrik directs the handover; carriers step back, others keep onlookers away.

> Copperback lowers her head over the open case. Her tongue tastes the straw; the tension leaves her tail. She folds herself beside the eggs. Serrik releases a breath and reaches for the familiar nose-band.

Her established routine now lets him lead her home. If moving her tonight is impractical, he camps with her away from traffic under a watch. Jessa records the theft, receives suspects and evidence, and reopens the road when it is safe. Ada brings bread to the fair or camp in thanks.

**Payment:** safe recovery or safe overnight containment earns the full **25 SP per PC**, even if the thieves escape. Serrik also pays for honest rescue efforts and the safe clutch if Copperback dies. Knowingly selling or abandoning the eggs earns no rescue fee. Award **4 IP** for resolving the theft and danger. Used loaned medical supplies are covered by the agreed injury care.

**Next sessions:** the saffron-thread factor in the note leads to an exotic-animal buyer; Serrik can offer an introduction to caravan work; the blocked passage invites a properly equipped storehouse expedition. A surviving, cooperative Dask may become an informant. Choose a hook that follows the actual outcome.

@@placard
STOREHOUSE FREIGHT HOIST

Both platforms are flat cargo decks. Keep people on the landing.

Read the weight of your cargo. Use the loose marked blocks to bring the other platform within one unit of that weight.

Move unwanted blocks onto the landing using their skids and handles. Keep clear of chains and edges.

When the loads are close enough, the brake lever releases. If it stays locked, check the weights. Do not force it.

Turn the hand winch to raise the lower load. Bring the platform level with the landing before unloading.
@@endplacard
