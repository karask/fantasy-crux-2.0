The [basic test](/rules/skills/#basic-tests) says when a roll is possible. This section is about when it is worth making.

## When to test {#gm-when-to-test}

Test when the outcome of an action is in doubt, or when a character pushes past what they can reliably do. If it is not clear the character can manage the task, you are within your rights to call for a test.

Test when it is dramatically appropriate and raises the tension. A skill test should feel like the moment in a thriller where the story could go either way. If success would only yield something minor, such as a scrap of information from a Lore test, give it to the player without a roll. Where failure has a definite consequence — falling into a spiked pit on a failed Athletics test — warn the player before they commit.

## When not to test {#gm-when-not-to-test}

Do not use a test in place of storytelling and roleplay. If a scene is flowing through conversation and everyone is enjoying it, think twice before breaking that with dice.

Do not roll merely to manufacture drama. A good description of a scene is not improved by a series of rolls.

Do not repeat a test that was just made. It is tempting to ask for several tests to represent a long or arduous task, such as a difficult climb or a track through dense jungle. Do not. Repeated rolls break immersion and turn a challenge into bookkeeping. Ask for one test, modified for the real difficulty, and do not ask again until the circumstances change.

## Group tests {#gm-group-tests}

When everyone must roll and any single failure fails the whole group, roll once for the weakest member instead. If the party is moving unseen through an area, roll Deception for the character with the lowest Deception and apply that result to all of them.

Reverse it when any single success would settle the matter. If eight guards each roll Perception to spot the characters, roll once for the guard with the highest Perception, perhaps with `+1B` because the others are helping him look.

## Sorting tests {#gm-sorting-tests}

When several NPCs attempt the same thing and only their skill separates them, roll once and read the single result against each of their skills. Several NPCs leaping between rooftops need one Athletics roll: those whose skill covers it land, and the rest do not.
