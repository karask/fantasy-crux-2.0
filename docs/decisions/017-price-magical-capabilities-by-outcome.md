# ADR-017: Price magical capabilities by outcome, not method

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-06

## Context

Shaping already required transformations to pay separately for movement modes
and senses, but it did not price those capabilities. Its only flight benchmark
also required active control, making every flying subject consume the Shaper's
Combat Action even when the subject directed its own movement. Breathing and
other common adaptations had no consistent benchmarks.

A generic instruction such as “climb walls” also does not identify a cell. It
could describe altered anatomy, supporting force, reshaped terrain, created
handholds, or another valid method.

## Decision

Publish Intensity benchmarks for movement modes, speed, senses, and breathing.
The benchmark prices the mechanical capability while the declared
Technique·Form must explain its method and determines targeting, defence, and
side effects.

A subject-directed movement mode is a passive ongoing benefit. The subject
spends its own Movement Action and chooses its route without concentration or
the Shaper's Combat Action. Directing another subject's movement remains active
control and costs the Shaper's Combat Action each round.

## Consequences

- Alter·Flesh may grant climbing through a physical adaptation, but wall
  climbing does not inherently belong to that cell.
- A bearer can use a sustained or continuous flight item and still take a
  Combat Action while directing their own movement.
- Telekinetically piloting another subject retains the active-control cost.
- Movement permission does not remove Athletics tests or environmental hazards,
  and breathing does not silently grant a swim speed or broader immunity.
- The preserved `freeform-magic/` alternative remains unchanged because it is
  unpublished and noncanonical.
