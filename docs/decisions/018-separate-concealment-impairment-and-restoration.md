# ADR-018: Separate concealment, impairment, and restoration

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-06

## Context

Sensory concealment priced invisibility but did not distinguish it explicitly
from impairing an observer's sense. Shaping also lacked general condition
benchmarks and a removal procedure. An unrestricted restoration effect could
therefore become a cheaper substitute for Dispel or bypass the specific rules
for wounds, poison, disease, and fatigue.

## Decision

Keep sensory concealment and sensory impairment as parallel but distinct
effects. Concealment hides a selected subject or evidence; impairment affects a
selected perceiver. Both use the same I2 partial, I3 one-sense, and I4
cell-wide ladder, but their targets, Reach, defences, and consequences differ.

Treat every other condition as a named description of paid mechanics. Publish
benchmarks from an I1 brief hindrance through I4 incapacitation, with bounded
retry or break procedures. Duration sustains a state but never repeats a
one-use denial such as a lost Combat Action.

Condition removal follows the source. Dispel alone ends active magic; poison,
disease, wounds, and fatigue use their specific procedures; another nonmagical
condition requires an appropriate cell and at least the Intensity of its
mechanical severity. Generic removal cannot erase mythic prices, oaths, debts,
or quests.

## Consequences

- Invisibility affects perception of the concealed subject only, while
  blindness affects everything the impaired observer attempts to see.
- Alternate senses work according to their evidence; optical invisibility does
  not defeat heat-based Blind Sight.
- Conditions cannot hide free damage, control, transformation, or repeated
  action denial behind a descriptive name.
- Restoration does not undercut Dispel, Surgery, Potency, or recovery rules.
