# The Elder Concord

> **Candidate setting:** This gazetteer is unpublished design material for a
> possible Fantasy Crux 2 default world. Nothing here is canonical until the
> setting is selected.

In Veyra, the land remembers promises.

Across the continent of Veyra, a road may close because a bridge has fallen,
because armed riders hold the ford, or because the hill beside it has withdrawn
its ancient permission. The dead advise their descendants from smoke-dark
hearths. River spirits demand that millers leave the first salmon untrapped.
Courts travel because no throne can speak fairly for every valley. Magic is
familiar, useful, and dangerous; agreement about what magic _is_ remains rare.

For five centuries the Elder Concord held this crowded world together. It was
not a single law or empire, but thousands of agreements among communities,
ancestors, local spirits, and neighboring peoples. Seven years ago, its Great
Renewal ended without a common oath. Most daily covenants still hold. Some are
failing. Others contradict one another. Every faction has a remedy, and every
remedy would make a different kind of world.

## Player's Guide

Everything before the Gamemaster's Gazetteer is safe for players.

### The campaign in one page

The Elder Concord is grounded high fantasy. A market town may have a healer who
can close a sword wound, an ancestor who remembers three generations of
boundaries, and a warded granary door. It still needs farmers, masons, midwives,
carters, guards, and honest witnesses. A Shaper spends Power Points, risks
Backlash, and cannot make temporary matter into lasting wealth. A wounded
traveler may be days from Surgery. A mountain spirit can be reasoned with, but
it does not become safe merely because it can speak.

Adventurers go where ordinary authority becomes uncertain:

- They carry judgments between communities that recognize different courts.
- They inspect failed oathstones, haunted barrows, silent shrines, and disputed
  roads.
- They find missing witnesses before a faction turns uncertainty into war.
- They escort healers, surveyors, negotiators, pilgrims, and High Chorus
  couriers.
- They recover records from dangerous places without assuming every inhabitant
  is an enemy.
- They decide which promises deserve repair, which should be renegotiated, and
  which were unjust from the beginning.

Violence is consequential. An armed group of equal size is a serious problem;
a troll, hag, werewolf, or veteran Shaper can end an unprepared expedition.
Threats usually leave signs. Learn motives, bring the right tools, secure an
escape, and accept that retreat can be the best victory.

The setting supports frontier exploration, civic mysteries, wilderness horror,
maritime expeditions, and faction intrigue. Its central question is not “Who
should rule the world?” but “Who has the right to bind anyone else, including
those not yet born?”

### What everyone knows

The **Elder Concord** was first sworn after a century of feuds called the Years
Without Guests. No surviving copy contains the whole agreement. Hearths, wagon
courts, refuge houses, woodland commons, river assemblies, and harbor feast
courts keep their own articles. Their overlap made peace possible.

Every twenty-seven years, delegates met at **Concord Hearth** to renew the
shared articles. At the last gathering, the central hearthstone split. Several
delegations departed before the final recitation. Since then:

- old guardian spirits sometimes enforce obsolete or contradictory terms;
- ancestors disagree about borders they once described identically;
- roads once safe by covenant have become subject to toll, haunting, or feud;
- harvest, burial, and water rites still work locally, but neighboring rites
  increasingly interfere;
- factions issue rival judgments while insisting they are preserving peace.

The dead are real. Ancestor spirits can counsel or even assist willing
relatives. Nymphs and other spirits inhabit particular groves, waters,
mountains, and darknesses. Their existence does not prove that any greater god
exists. Some people worship distant gods, some honor only present spirits and
the remembered dead, and some consider worship a category error. No side can
settle the argument by pointing to magic: prayer is a valid Shaping Practice,
but so are song, geometry, breath, gesture, and craft.

No people is naturally monstrous. Humans, elves, dwarves, goblins, orcs,
lizardfolk, merfolk, and pixies maintain governments, families, disputes, and
alliances. Old prejudice survives, particularly where a feud has been recast as
an ancestral truth.

### Lands of the Concord

Veyra is roughly two-thirds continental country and one-third coast, strait,
reef, and island. The settled center lies around the River Sable. The west and
north rise through wooded uplands to the Warmstone Crown. The east opens into
grassland. In the south, five rivers spread through hot floodplains before
reaching the Saltward Coast. Beyond the coast, the Broken Choir Isles divide
open water into dangerous channels.

#### The seven regions

**Sable Vale** is the meeting ground. Dark soil, alder-lined rivers, low chalk
ridges, and old barrows surround Merecross, the largest neutral market in the
interior. Almost every faction maintains a house there. Its prosperity makes it
the best starting region and its layered obligations make it the least neutral
place imaginable.

**The Chalkwake Downs** are rolling western pasture cut by chalk ravines,
barrow fields, and slow rivers. Ashwake dwarves keep disputed remains,
inheritance testimony, and adoption records for lineages that would otherwise
end. Barrowmarket, their largest neutral exchange, surrounds nine funeral
landings rather than a palace. Human hearthholds accept Ashwake custody while
arguing over how much authority the custodians should inherit from the dead.

**The Brambleweald** is a worked forest of orchards, coppices, pannage woods,
controlled burns, sacred groves, and abandoned clearings. Bramblekin goblin
communes allocate seasonal use at Greenmoot and negotiate directly with grove
spirits. Dryads speak for their own domains; they are neighbors or litigants,
not subjects. Every useful right carries work, restraint, or restitution.

**The Warmstone Crown** contains snow passes, geothermal caverns, sun terraces,
and old underways. Embercoil lizardfolk refuge houses guarantee winter asylum,
then argue over how many guests their warmth and food can honestly sustain.
Warmstone Gate is the principal surface sanctuary. Oreads claim peaks and
valleys. Rockfall, stale air, and sealed doors make an unknown tunnel a worse
gamble than a long surface road.

**The Windglass Reach** is a broad eastern country of seasonal streams, singing
grass, and wind-polished stone. Long Witness elf caravans complete twenty-seven
year circuits between host communities. Their memories make precedent portable,
but each temporary bench must be elected by the people whose case it hears.
Witness Ford is the circuit's busiest gathering. One disputed crossing can
mobilize grievances remembered by witnesses who were present at their beginning.

**The Five-Reed Delta** spreads five hot rivers through floodplain and marsh.
Five-Reed merfolk cooperatives maintain floating gardens, surface halls, flood
refuges, and short-dive irrigation works. Five-Reed Hall is a raft assembly that
moves between channels; the route table assumes its current Floodturn Mooring.
Humans and dwarves share the levees. Rotating water rights prevent a fixed
throne, but a delayed garden can still starve a district.

**The Saltward and Broken Choir** comprise the southeastern coast, the
Shattered Strait, and hundreds of inhabited islands. Orc-led Open Keel havens
guarantee rescue, repair, and guest-right to the shipwrecked. The mixed
Choirhaven Compact bargains with local spirits over storm refuge and pilgrim
access. Choir's End is its farthest southern haven. Merfolk visitors breathe air
and plan short dives from surface halls rather than living on an abyssal floor.

### Distances and travel

Overland estimates use the standard daily rates: 30 km marching or moving with
wagons, 50 km hiking or riding, and 90 km riding fast. Hiking and fast riding
require the usual end-of-day Fatigue tests. Terrain reduces those rates: rolling
hills and light woods to 70%, and heavy woodland to 50%. Weather, failed
bridges, hostile tolls, and navigation can add days.

| Route                            | Distance and ground                  | Typical time                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Merecross to Barrowmarket        | 170 km, road and rolling downs       | 8 days marching; 5 days hiking or riding         |
| Merecross to Greenmoot           | 145 km, road then heavy woodland     | 8–10 days marching; 5–6 days hiking              |
| Merecross to Warmstone Gate      | 260 km, road and steep rolling hills | 13 days marching; 8 days hiking or riding        |
| Merecross to Witness Ford        | 220 km, road and rolling grassland   | 11 days marching; 7 days riding                  |
| Merecross to Five-Reed Hall      | 285 km, road and river levees        | 10 days marching; 6 days riding                  |
| Merecross to Duskport            | 72 km, maintained river road         | 3 days marching; 2 days hiking or riding         |
| Duskport to the Shattered Strait | 280 km by sea                        | About 2 fair days in a one-masted, 6-knot vessel |
| Duskport to Choir's End          | 480 km by sea                        | About 3 fair days in a two-masted, 5-knot vessel |

Sea estimates use the vessel's normal rate of 20 miles per day per knot. Winds,
storms, shoals, and stops at breathing stations matter more than a line on a
map. A magically arranged favorable wind can help, but it costs PP and active
capacity and does not remove the need for a capable captain.

### Peoples, cultures, and languages

These are cultural homes, not restrictions. Adoption, intermarriage, fosterage,
trade, and exile have mixed Veyra for centuries. Choose the Culture and
Language that fit a character's upbringing. The existing creature profile and
Fantasy Races guidance remain the mechanical source for a nonhuman character;
the setting adds no racial abilities. Merfolk and pixie characters require a
campaign that can accommodate their movement and scale.

#### Hearthfolk humans

The **Hearthfold culture** includes most settled humans of Sable Vale and many
western and coastal communities. A legal hearth is any household that keeps a
public flame, offers emergency shelter, and sends a witness to its local moot.
Households can be large, elective, and unrelated by blood. Hearthfolk prize
reliable testimony more than noble descent. Their common skills include
farming, river trade, craft, mediation, and the quiet art of knowing which
ancestor should _not_ be invited into an argument.

Use **Culture (Hearthfold)** and **Language (Hearthspeech)**. Hearthspeech is the
most common trade language in the central regions.

#### Red Road humans

The **Red Road kindreds** are drovers, performers, ferrymen, tinkers, and
seasonal workers whose painted wagons count as legal hearths. Some travel beside
the Long Witness Circuit, but they neither own its judgments nor exist to serve
it. Settled people romanticize their mobility while distrusting households that
can leave a bad bargain behind.

Use **Culture (Red Road)** and **Language (Road Cant)**.

#### Long Witness elves

The **Long Witnesses** cross the Windglass Reach in wagon-caravans, completing
the same circuit every twenty-seven years. Long life makes them valuable
testimony and dangerous partisans: an elf may remember the original insult
behind an inherited custom. They keep precedent portable, but host communities
elect each temporary bench and can reject a witness for a declared conflict.
Young elves often serve as outriders or recorders for a full circuit before they
may offer remembered testimony.

Use **Culture (Long Witness)** and **Language (Roadsong)**.

#### Ashwake dwarves

The **Ashwake** live along the low rivers and barrow fields of the Chalkwake
Downs. They custody funerals, disputed remains, ancestor testimony, and adoption
into lineages that have no living heir. Their low barges carry ashes, witnesses,
and sealed inheritance chests between otherwise hostile hearths. Authority comes
from trusted custody, not a mine or hereditary king, and younger Ashwake argue
that preserving every old claim can strangle the living.

Use **Culture (Ashwake)** and **Language (Ash Cant)**.

#### Bramblekin goblins

The **Bramblekin** organize orchard, coppice, pannage, and controlled-burn
communes throughout the Brambleweald. Seasonal work-moots allocate use rights;
groves and local spirits bargain in their own names. Goblins are respected for
knowing how much disturbance a living landscape can absorb, not for machines or
ledgers. Their sharpest dispute is whether inherited prohibitions protect the
forest or merely protect old families from newcomers.

Use **Culture (Bramble Commons)** and **Language (Leaf Cant)**.

#### Open Keel orcs

The **Open Keel houses** are harbor crews, ship-share families, rescuers, and
repairers of the Saltward and Broken Choir. Any wreck survivor who reaches their
painted posts receives food, air, and a hearing before debts are argued. Feast
courts allocate salvage shares and elect harbor speakers. An orc who saves a
rival crew earns more standing than one who wins a dockside duel, though
captains feud bitterly over when rescue becomes compulsory.

Use **Culture (Open Keel)** and **Language (Harbor Speech)**.

#### Embercoil lizardfolk

The **Embercoil Refuge Houses** inhabit geothermal caverns and sun terraces in
the Warmstone Crown. Every house promises winter asylum, then must judge how
many guests its warmth, food, and air can sustain. Lizardfolk pauses are acts of
accounting, not indifference: a generous ruling that exhausts a refuge can kill
everyone inside. The houses have no king and cannot command one another to open.

Use the existing Lizardfolk profile with **Culture (Embercoil)** and **Language
(Warmstone)**.

#### Five-Reed merfolk

The **Five-Reed cooperatives** maintain floating gardens, surface halls, flood
refuges, and short-dive irrigation works across the southern delta. Merfolk
breathe air and can hold their breath underwater for 11 minutes, so even their
submerged channels link frequent breathing wells. Gardens and water rights
rotate among five river assemblies; no lineage owns a channel forever. Their
great political question is whether emergency flood refuge creates a permanent
claim on the land that received it.

Use **Culture (Five-Reed)** and **Language (Reed Speech)**.

#### High Chorus flights

Pixies of the **High Chorus** operate a decentralized physical postal relay.
Couriers fly short stages between weatherproof eyries, transferring sealed
personal letters, witnessed rulings, and public warning ribbons to rested wings.
They are adults doing dangerous work in hawk country, not mascots. Local eyries
answer to their host communities, while the Chorus as a whole can refuse a route
that falsifies or attacks its messages.

Most deliveries are mundane flight. A few trained route-choristers know
`Bend·Paths` or `Scry·Paths`; those cells follow normal limits and do not create
instant long-range post.

Use **Culture (High Chorus)** and **Language (Wing Cant)**.

### Faith, ancestors, and local powers

A **hearth remembrance** names the dead who may be addressed and the living
people entitled to address them. Most ancestors never manifest. Those who do
are not omniscient: they know what they knew in life, have loyalties, and can be
wrong. Helpful possession is intimate and always requires a willing living
relative.

Local spirits are treated according to relationship rather than category. A
naiad may be a respected water-neighbor, an extortionist, or both. A guardian
spirit obeys its binding, not a universal morality. Predatory spirits,
possession, and spiritual attack are real dangers. The Lantern Communion trains
people to distinguish grief from a haunting and a family disagreement from an
intruding passion spirit.

Belief in greater gods takes several forms. The **Far-Power temples** address
beings said to stand beyond local memory. The **Near Reverence** holds that
duty begins with present land, spirits, and ancestors. **Quiet Houses** refuse
metaphysical claims and maintain funerals as civic obligations. Their members
can all be sincere Shapers. A successful prayer proves that the practitioner
shaped something; it does not prove who listened.

### Shaping in everyday life

People know effects, teachers, and traditions rather than a universal magical
grid. A farmer might recognize the reed-cutting rite that Bends Water without
knowing it shares a Technique with an orc storm-call or a goblin orchard
working. Scholars who compare all six Techniques and ten Forms are rare,
politically suspect, or both.

In rules terms, every Shaper learns exact Technique–Form cells and has an
observable, interruptible **Practice** and an unavoidable **Tell** around the
Shaper and any ranged target or area. Communities care about both. A healer
whose Practice requires sung names cannot work while gagged; a court diviner
whose Tell blackens every flame cannot plausibly deny that magic occurred.
Veiling can conceal a Tell, but it increases Magnitude and makes the Shaping
harder.

Minor Shapers are visible. A village may support an apprentice with two useful
cells. Veteran Shapers are uncommon, Experts are matters of regional politics,
and a Master is a figure of legend or alarm. The same limits apply to every
tradition:

- `sum of outcome Intensities + Range + Duration + Reach + adjustments = Magnitude`.
- Magnitude determines the PP requirement and base cost, difficulty, active
  capacity, and resistance to Dispel.
- Expertise sets maximum Intensity and Safe Magnitude. Normal casting reaches
  at most Safe Magnitude + 2; greater work requires a ritual.
- Total active Magnitude cannot exceed permanent, unmodified POW.
- Overreach risks Backlash. Work above Safe + 2, or with Intensity 5, Range 4+,
  Duration 3+, or Reach 3+, requires a ritual.
- Any dial at 5 makes a Shaping Mythic and demands a declared lasting price.
- Permanent supernatural enchantments remain active and Dispellable through
  vulnerable physical or spiritual anchors.
- Sense Magic and Dispel are universal actions for Shapers and require no known
  cell. Counter requires its Talent.

These facts keep magic consequential. `Alter·Flesh` can save a wounded person,
but treating a Major Wound requires Intensity 3+ and counts as Surgery.
Paths can shorten a local crossing, but they cannot deliver armies freely,
create unsafe arrivals, or ignore Range and Reach. Ritual contributors may
pledge PP, but they do not lend expertise, and only the leader rolls and suffers
Backlash. Temporary creation cannot supply permanent craft or wealth.

### Starting region: Sable Vale

Sable Vale offers three scales of play without a long journey. Merecross
supplies same-day intrigue. Neighboring settlements are one to three marching
days away; most are within two days for fit hikers or riders. Barrows, forest
roads, mountains, and the coast support longer expeditions.

#### Merecross

Merecross, population about eighteen thousand, stands where the Sable and
Nettle Rivers meet beneath a three-arched stone bridge. Its districts are
organized around public hearths rather than walls.

**Concord Square** contains the cracked local oathstone, faction houses, open
court steps, and the Long Market. Armed retainers are permitted but must bind
their sword knots in blue cord. The custom has no magical force; violating it
simply tells everyone that violence was planned.

**Lantern Row** holds undertakers, surgeons, shrines, and cheap lodgings for the
bereaved. The **House of the Last Lamp** provides Healing and has two resident
surgeons capable of ordinary Surgery. Sister Olan Reed, a human Communion
veteran, is also a Shaper, but her PP, availability, and judgment are finite.

**Bridgeward** houses ferrymen, warehouses, the river watch, and the **Seven
Knots**, an outfitter selling standard travel, climbing, camping, weapon, and
armour supplies at settled-market availability. Owner Dema Moss-Ear is a
Bramblekin former coppice-factor who knows which merchants sell unsafe rope.

**Chorus Eyrie** is a reinforced loft beneath the old tollhouse roof and the
Vale's High Chorus relay. High Chorister Talar Quickrain schedules flights
around hawks and storms. The counter accepts sealed personal letters, witnessed
rulings, and warning ribbons sized for pixie carriage; larger loads go by cart.

**Ash Court** is an older district built around sealed barrow entrances.
Mara Venn, human keeper of the Steam Cup bathhouse, is the most useful
underworld contact in the city. She arranges discreet meetings, stolen-record
recovery, and passage past corrupt toll keepers. She will not broker murder,
possession, or grave desecration.

**The Chalk House** is an Open Hand Circle. Its teachers test potential
initiates through service, supervised Sense Magic, and exposure to a tradition
willing to teach. It is a route to the normal supernatural initiation required
for Shaping access, not a way around the 20 IP cost or the need to learn exact
cells.

#### Neighboring settlements

**Alderwash** lies 24 km west on a maintained road. Its mill, tannery, and eel
fishery depend on a naiad's old water covenant. The millers say the river has
begun returning objects thrown into it, including things lost years ago.

**Bellfound** lies 38 km north beside the quarry road. Ashwake bell-casters and
Bramblekin charcoal burners make mundane bells whose tones test nearby chalk
caverns. One bell has started answering itself from underground.

**Gloamharrow** lies 42 km northwest through heavy woodland. Its sheep graze
among low barrows under a strict sunset curfew. The people are neither ignorant
nor superstitious: three shepherds have vanished, and tracks show one walked
home after losing too much blood to live.

**Reedwake** lies 56 km south along the levee road. A mixed Five-Reed and
Hearthfolk council manages floating gardens, boatyards, and flood gates. Its
water markers put the coming flood three days later than an upstream assembly.

**Duskport** lies 72 km southeast where the Sable becomes tidal. It has average
lodging, ship passage, repair yards, an Open Keel feast court, a Choirhaven
embassy, and a High Chorus weather perch. Open Keel crews deny repair to captains
who abandoned wreck survivors.

#### Three party premises

**Concord troubleshooters:** Each character has been sponsored by a different
community or faction to investigate failing covenants. The party has authority
to gather testimony, not to impose a ruling.

**Long Witness warrant:** A traveling judge has issued the party a limited warrant
to find a missing witness and preserve evidence. Every local authority agrees
the warrant is genuine; they disagree about what it permits.

**Dead-letter company:** The High Chorus hires larger folk to recover relay
bags, repair dangerous eyries, and deliver messages too large or politically
sensitive for a lone courier. The party learns secrets because everyone assumes
the post does.

Characters can instead begin as locals drawn together by one of the opening
adventures in the Gamemaster's Gazetteer.

### What failed at the Renewal

The Elder Concord was never a single landscape-scale enchantment. It is a
network of social agreements and separately built Shapings. A ford might carry
a permanent anchored `Ward·Spirit`, a boundary stone a permanent
`Scry·Paths`, while neighboring villages keep a mundane maintenance schedule.
Each inherited magical article was its own Duration 5 Mythic ritual, with an
exact cell and outcome, leader, possible contributors, Magnitude, Practice,
Tell, lasting price, and vulnerable anchor. The Great Renewal coordinated those
works; it did not combine them or bypass a normal limit.

Three things broke the last Renewal.

First, revision was honestly necessary. Old woodland articles assumed villages
that no longer existed. Coastal law ignored new refuge harbors. Several delta
channels crossed fields built during an unusually dry generation. Delegates
could not truthfully repeat every inherited promise.

Second, six crucial witnesses missed the gathering for different defensible
reasons. Five-Reed boats answered a flood alarm. Open Keel crews diverted to a
wreck. A Bramble moot closed a road after a grove spirit withdrew permission.
An Embercoil house kept its advocate for an early freeze, and storm and hawks
grounded two High Chorus stages. No hidden hand coordinated their absence.

Third, delegates would not let disputed magic renew by silence. During the
closing recitation, several Shapers each attempted a separate Dispel against one
article they believed unjust: a road ward excluding a grove, a harbor ward
blocking rescue, and a funeral scry naming an obsolete heir among them. Some
individual tests won and others failed. Their Tells overlapped with dozens of
Practices as a mundane thermal crack split Concord Hearth. The result was a
patchwork of active, ended, and mutually incompatible local works.

There is no central conspiracy to expose. The Sepulchral Regency arrived ready
to exploit the failure and now destroys evidence that weakens its continuity
claims, but it did not cause the Renewal to fail. Repairing the stone alone will
not recreate ended Shapings or make unjust articles worthy of consent.

#### Running covenant magic

Treat every magical covenant as an ordinary Shaping. Decide its exact cell and
outcome, then build its Magnitude normally. An inherited supernatural article
is normally a permanent, Duration 5 Mythic ritual: record its leader,
contributors, Practice, Tell, Magnitude, lasting price, result, anchor, and
whether the price removed it from the creator's active total. It remains active
and Dispellable through that anchor.

An indefinite article is different. Its leader personally commits full
Magnitude from current and available maximum PP; the reduced maximum governs
recovery, no one contributes that PP, and a lasting price does not remove the
load. A permanent mundane transformation is inactive and cannot be Dispelled.

Never let “ancient covenant” become permission for a free effect. If an old
article would exceed the rules, it was a unique campaign event with exceptional
preparation and a serious lasting price. Its anchor is still vulnerable.

### Three philosophies of Shaping

Comparative Shapers agree that six Techniques and ten Forms describe reliable
practice. They disagree about why. Do not confirm one theory. For every deep
clue, provide at least two credible interpretations.

#### The Living Chronicle

Open Hand archivists describe reality as a chronicle that remembers its current
text and the revisions that made it. Forms are subjects; Techniques are kinds of
revision. A cell is learned permission to amend one subject in one way.
Practice cites the desired revision, and the Tell is the world showing its
“margin.” PP measures how much of the Shaper's own presence must be pressed
into the change. Active Magnitude is limited by POW because a person can sustain
only so many contested revisions.

This theory explains why permanent mundane creation becomes ordinary reality,
why completed transformations cannot be Dispelled, and why permanent
supernatural properties need anchors. Its critics ask who wrote the first text and why
different Practices can make the same change. Politically, Chronicle doctrine
appeals to reformers—and to censors who believe only trained editors should
touch the world.

#### The Six Courtesies

Lantern mediators and many spirit practitioners teach that every Form is a kind
of neighbor. The six Techniques are the six permissible approaches:
**invitation** for Conjure, **guidance** for Bend, **dismissal** for Unmake,
**exchange** for Alter, **refusal** for Ward, and **attention** for Scry. A cell
is not power over a Form but knowledge of the correct courtesy. Practice makes
the approach intelligible. The Tell is the neighbor's acknowledgment. PP is the
gift that makes an approach consequential; Backlash is a failed relationship
returning force upon the discourteous speaker.

This view fits spirits, resistance, ritual offerings, and the way a named being
may answer a summons without becoming controlled. Its critics note that fire,
distance, and dead flesh do not demonstrate intentions. Its worst adherents can
excuse danger as “offense given” and blame victims for a Shaper's failed
overreach.

#### Deep Resonance

Ashwake listeners and Five-Reed cadence counters argue that Forms are stable
families of resonance in one underlying medium. Techniques describe how a
Shaper drives, redirects, damps, retunes, isolates, or reads a resonance. A
known cell is a practiced interval that the mind and body can reproduce.
Magnitude is the total load of intensity, distance, persistence, extent, and
complication. PP supplies the impulse; POW limits the patterns held in active
resonance. Anchors keep long harmonics coherent but also give a dispeller
something to disrupt.

Deep Resonance predicts Magnitudes beautifully and offers no satisfying account
of vows, souls, or why a Practice must be personally performed and remains
interruptible. Its defenders call meaning a resonance not yet measured. Its
political danger is the belief that every covenant can be standardized without
the consent of those who live inside it.

### Faction dossiers

Each dossier gives the faction enough direction to act without the characters.
Leader shorthand is a roleplaying and build prompt, not a replacement stat
block. Build important humans as experienced characters; use the named
nonhuman profile as a base and add suitable skills, equipment, cells, or
abilities rather than increasing crowd size.

#### Hearth Moot

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** It represents fixed hearth communities and
  organizes another Renewal. Speaker Avel Dain privately wants to replace the
  single gathering with regional renewals, preventing any future central
  failure but weakening common law.
- **Fracture:** Market towns want votes by population; villages insist every
  recognized hearth community is equal.
- **Leader and reach:** Avel is a human elder, patient coalition-builder, and
  poor field commander. The Moot has meeting halls, food levies, witnesses, and
  close ties to Lantern Communion; Long Witness judges distrust its fixed borders.
- **Shaping:** Senior hearthkeepers favor `Ward·Spirit` and `Scry·Fate`.
  Practices count coals into a bowl; Tells settle soot in concentric rings.
- **Now:** Its census takers are verifying which settlements still qualify.
  They ask escorts and neutral witnesses. If ignored, rival censuses create two
  incompatible delegations before winter.

#### Long Witness Circuit

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** Elven wagon courts carry testimony among
  jurisdictions that elect each bench. Chief Witness Aren Twenty-Seven has lost
  the mundane wheel-staff authenticating this circuit and conceals the loss to
  keep opportunists from rejecting every pending ruling.
- **Fracture:** Young judges weigh present harm; elders fear that abandoning
  precedent turns local popularity into law.
- **Leader and reach:** Aren is an elf, incisive in public and privately
  exhausted. Use the Elf profile with Influence, Lore, and Riding. The Circuit
  has living witnesses, outriders, guest-right, and tense relations with the
  fixed Hearth Moot.
- **Shaping:** Judges favor `Scry·Fate`, `Scry·Mind`, and `Ward·Mind`.
  Practices recite both claims; Tells sound like a wagon passing out of sight.
- **Now:** Aren is hearing three claims to Witness Ford. The party may recover
  an absent witness. Without one, any ruling triggers retaliation.

#### Lantern Communion

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** The Communion provides funerals, healing, and
  defense against possession. Its inner council knows manifestations decline
  where contact enchantments and hearth rites have broken; the ancestor spirits
  themselves are not Anchored. It maps the silence before panic spreads.
- **Fracture:** Severers expel dangerous spirits quickly; Listeners attempt
  negotiation even after harm.
- **Leader and reach:** Prior Enna Moss is a dwarf physician, practical,
  compassionate, and ruthless about quarantine. Use the Dwarf profile with
  strong Healing (including Surgery), Lore, and Shaping. The Communion has hospices,
  mortuary records, and public trust; the Regency calls it partisan.
- **Shaping:** `Alter·Flesh`, `Ward·Spirit`, and `Unmake·Spirit`; Practices use
  lamps and spoken names, while Tells make every nearby flame lean inward.
- **Now:** Enna asks for samples and testimony from silent hearths. Delay lets a
  disease spirit's work be mistaken for ancestral punishment.

#### Open Hand Circles

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** The Circles initiate and teach Shapers across
  cultural boundaries. A comparative school wants to publish the complete cell
  grid, ending elite monopolies but exposing dangerous knowledge without local
  safeguards.
- **Fracture:** Openers consider secrecy oppression; Keepers believe cells
  cannot be separated from accountable traditions.
- **Leader and reach:** Ilyon-of-Chalk is an elf itinerant teacher, curious,
  candid, and incapable of leaving a mystery alone. Use the Elf profile with
  Shaping and several noncombat cells. Circles possess teachers, copied
  journals, and safehouses; every other faction both uses and watches them.
- **Shaping:** Cells vary. A common Practice keeps one hand empty and one on a
  focus; Tells leave fleeting handprints on nearby surfaces.
- **Now:** Ilyon plans a forbidden comparative convocation. The party can
  secure it. If it fails violently, cross-tradition teaching collapses.

#### Bramble Commons

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** Goblin work-moots allocate orchards, coppices,
  burns, and pannage while recognizing grove spirits as parties. The Briar Bench
  would close one inhabited road to reconnect two failing dryad domains.
- **Fracture:** Old-root communes defend inherited limits; new-clearance
  families demand rights based on present labor.
- **Leader and reach:** Tavi Moss-Mouth is a goblin advocate, funny, exact, and
  merciless toward hidden costs. Use the Goblin profile with Influence and
  Natural Lore. The Commons has seed stores, foresters, fire crews, and
  independent dryad allies.
- **Shaping:** `Alter·Flesh`, `Bend·Earth/Stone`, and `Ward·Paths`; Tells turn
  leaves pale-side outward and scent the air with rain.
- **Now:** Greenmoot marks a proposed road closure. Residents ask the party to
  document homes and test alternate corridors. Without mediation, they burn the
  marks and awaken an older feud.

#### Embercoil Refuge Houses

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** The houses coordinate sanctuary, warm caverns,
  and mountain passage. Elders hide failing vents beneath three refuges, fearing
  that honest warning will bring guests they cannot support.
- **Fracture:** Open-Coil houses treat refuge as absolute duty; Banked-Coil
  houses put current residents first.
- **Leader and reach:** Serrak Warm-Coil is a lizardfolk refuge keeper, blunt,
  methodical, and ashamed of the concealment. Use the Lizardfolk profile with
  Engineering, Lore, and Shaping. The houses control shelter, guides, food
  caches, and mountain routes.
- **Shaping:** `Bend·Earth/Stone`, `Alter·Earth/Stone`, `Ward·Earth/Stone`, and
  `Scry·Earth/Stone`; Practices tap heated stones, while Tells give nearby rock
  a warm sheen without revealing structure.
- **Now:** Serrak hires outsiders to inspect a sealed underway. If no one goes,
  a winter collapse traps a whole refuge.

#### Ashwake Covenant

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** Ashwake dwarves custody funerals, disputed
  inheritances, and adoption into ended lineages. Their senior keepers secretly
  accepted living petitioners under extinct names to keep land from Regency
  seizure.
- **Fracture:** Letter-keepers defend exact descent; Hearth-Adopters say duty,
  care, and consent can make a truer lineage.
- **Leader and reach:** Dora Ashwake is a dwarf custodian, dry-witted and
  attentive. Use the Dwarf profile with Influence, Lore, Healing, and Trade.
  The Covenant has barrow keys, death records, low barges, and public trust.
- **Shaping:** `Scry·Spirit`, `Ward·Spirit`, and `Scry·Mind`; Practices mix ash
  with river water, while Tells leave a grey rim around reflections.
- **Now:** Dora seeks an original adoption witness before the Regency exposes
  the altered register. Exposure without a fair hearing disinherits hundreds.

#### Open Keel League

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** Orc-led harbor houses guarantee rescue, repair,
  and guest-right after wrecks. A hard-line feast court plans to declare all
  Duskport docks perpetual refuge ground, displacing customs law.
- **Fracture:** Rescue houses treat aid as unconditional; ship-share captains
  demand repayment from crews that took foolish risks.
- **Leader and reach:** Rakka Keel-Scar is an orc captain, disciplined,
  hospitable, and impatient with ceremony. Use the Orc profile with Sailing,
  Influence, and Culture (Open Keel). The League has crews, repair yards,
  shares, island havens, and fierce ties of rescued guest-right.
- **Shaping:** `Bend·Air/Storm`, `Ward·Paths`, and `Unmake·Force/Motion`;
  Practices strike a ship's bell, and Tells make rigging hum without wind.
- **Now:** Rakka occupies one condemned pier as refuge after a wreck. Without a
  settlement, rescue becomes annexation and Duskport arms its watch.

#### Five-Reed Assembly

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** Merfolk water-garden cooperatives coordinate
  flood refuge, food, and rotating channels. Their records predict that the next
  eclipse coincides with widespread covenant failure; several cooperatives hold
  back garden stores to compel attendance at an emergency Renewal.
- **Fracture:** Upper reeds favor one delta schedule; lower gardens trust local
  water signs and call the withholding manufactured scarcity.
- **Leader and reach:** Oru-of-Five-Reeds is a merfolk convener, deliberate and
  protective of dissenters. Use the Merfolk profile with Lore, Influence,
  Sailing, and Shaping. The Assembly has rafts, food gardens, breathing wells,
  and flood markers.
- **Shaping:** `Scry·Fate`, `Bend·Water/Ice`, and `Alter·Flesh`; Practices align
  reed floats, while Tells straighten ripples into hard-edged bands.
- **Now:** Conflicting markers change floodgate timing by three days. Oru asks
  travelers to compare all five rivers. Failure floods Reedwake or strands a
  downstream garden.

#### Choirhaven Compact

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** This mixed island-and-shore league maintains
  storm refuges and negotiates pilgrim access with local spirits. It wants five
  independent refuge covenants renewed as one route, improving safety while
  letting its feast court choose which harbors qualify.
- **Fracture:** Haven keepers favor common obligations; spirit-speakers insist
  no local power consented to regional rule.
- **Leader and reach:** Ista Shellbell is a human island advocate, patient in
  negotiation and fierce about closed shelter. Build her as an experienced
  navigator and mediator. The Compact has pilots, guest halls, weather lore,
  small vessels, and spirit interpreters.
- **Shaping:** `Ward·Air/Storm`, `Scry·Spirit`, and `Ward·Paths`; Practices use
  shell taps and breath counts, while Tells hold droplets motionless.
- **Now:** Ista closes a pilgrim channel after its refuge spirit falls silent.
  Ignored, privateers offer unsafe passage and blame the Compact for every loss.

#### High Chorus

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** The Chorus carries sealed letters, witnessed
  rulings, and warnings through a physical flying relay. Senior choristers have
  route logs proving the Renewal's absences had unrelated causes and break
  ordinary custody rules to keep factions from manufacturing a simple culprit.
- **Fracture:** Sealed-Wing couriers will never inspect a packet; Witness-Wing
  choristers argue that public acts must be copied before they can be suppressed.
- **Leader and reach:** High Chorister Talar Quickrain is a pixie logistician,
  formal, brave, and unable to forgive preventable loss. Use the Pixie profile
  with Trade and Culture. The Chorus has eyries, weather logs, seals, witnesses,
  and access earned through local service.
- **Shaping:** Most use none. Trained route-choristers may know `Bend·Paths` or
  `Scry·Paths`; Practices knot route-string, and Tells crease the air.
- **Now:** Talar asks the party to reopen Gloam Eyrie. Without it, exculpatory
  records arrive after factions settle on a convenient enemy.

#### Sepulchral Regency

- **Purpose and concealed aim:** The Regency publicly argues that preserved
  rulers retain offices left vacant by a failed Renewal. Its inner council
  wants government insulated from spirits and mortality, with soulless mummy
  regents applying archived law forever.
- **Fracture:** Living Continuists want strict but humane rule; the oldest
  regents consider the living temporary custodians of property.
- **Leader and reach:** Regent Ossan Caul is a mummy jurist, lucid, courteous,
  and wholly certain consent decays faster than law. Use the Mummy profile and
  add appropriate Lore and Influence rather than followers. The Regency has
  tomb wealth, old seals, living agents, and limited skeleton or zombie labor;
  it courts frightened Hearth delegates and denounces Lantern practice.
- **Shaping:** Living necromancers favor `Conjure·Flesh`, `Ward·Mind`, and
  `Scry·Fate`. Practices rub grave salt into written rulings; Tells repeat the
  last spoken words backward.
- **Now:** Agents seek embers and fragments from Concord Hearth. A living
  Continuist asks the party to authenticate Ossan's chain of office. If
  unopposed, the Regent presents an apparently complete claim at the emergency
  assembly.

### Eight ready-to-run sites

Use the referenced creature profiles as written for an average inhabitant.
Build a named foe from the profile if the party needs a peer opponent. Do not
make a site harder by filling it with disposable bodies.

#### 1. The Bell Below

Under Bellfound, a named **hag** called Mother Hollowtone occupies a cave once
used for solitary mourning. Beyond her profile's cells, she has learned
`Scry·Mind` and `Bend·Air/Storm`: she Scries an isolated worker's secrets, then
voices them through the bell, building and paying for each Shaping normally.
Chalk dust rises around the target and bell with their Tells. She wants the
Communion to restore the darkness covenant broken by quarrying and bargains for
silence and a boundary. Hollowtone claims she ate founder Dela Nymm and trapped
her voice in bronze. In truth, the deafened Bramblekin lives as “Sela Reed” at
Merecross's House of the Last Lamp and fled after knowingly ordering the breach.
Daylight through a new ventilation shaft gives leverage and retreat; tight
ledges punish assault. Bellfound, the Communion, and Bramble Commons disagree.

#### 2. Hushwife's Barrow

The central barrow at Gloamharrow contains a lucid **mummy** called the Hushwife
and a few **skeleton** attendants. Sheep refuse its eastern slope; footprints
enter sealed stone; blood stains stop at the door. The Hushwife preserves
shepherds mauled by Kessa Marr, the same Red Road **werewolf** now sheltering at
Gloam Eyrie. Kessa lost control under the full moon, is horrified, and seeks
escort to Lantern Row before the next; she will testify and accept guarded
travel but attacks if trapped in wolf form. The Hushwife also demands grave
goods stolen by a Regency agent. Her hammer and narrow passages make combat
disastrous. Characters may exchange testimony, recover the goods, or withdraw
before sunset. The Long Witness Circuit needs both women's testimony.

#### 3. The Rootless Orchard

Wind-thrown trees remain green around a **dryad** whose anchor tree has been
undermined but not destroyed. Fruit grows without seeds, axes rust from mundane
damp, and animal paths curve away. The dryad uses her listed Mind cells to
discourage intruders and wants a drainage trench closed before her tree falls.
The trench protects two human cottages from flooding. She can be negotiated
with; Engineering and labor can redirect water without magic. If attacked, her
44-metre grove and vegetation make pursuit costly, but the old cart road offers
clear retreat. Bramble Commons would close the trench; Hearth engineers can
design a compromise if shown the site.

#### 4. Witness Ford Oathstone

A **guardian spirit** at Witness Ford enforces two incompatible articles. A
permanent `Ward·Paths` denies armed eastbound companies passage until they bind
weapons at the west cairn and accept a local host. A later permanent
`Ward·Paths` denies attempts to toll, delay, or disarm a recognized food or
medical company. The Hearth Moot claims the relief right, eastbank villages
claim host and maintenance authority, and the Long Witness Circuit claims
neutral court ground. The spirit's profile Binding, anchored to the old ford
post, makes it attack anyone trying to force either active Ward. Each Ward has a
separate stone anchor on the gravel island; Dispelling one ends only that
Shaping, not the spirit or the other Ward. Fast water is the greater hazard.
Characters can recover both recitations, Dispel one article, close the ford, or
retreat along either bank. Host priority protects locals but delays aid; relief
priority exposes them and shifts maintenance costs; neutral court requires the
Circuit to fund and host the crossing, compromising its claimed detachment.

#### 5. Blackwater Weir

At Alderwash, a distressed **naiad** returns lost objects because silt has
buried the stones marking her water covenant. Dead fish and shared dreams are
fictional warnings of a **disease spirit** embodying **The Shakes**; “feeding on
the quarrel” describes its motive, not greater power. The naiad wants the mill wheel
stopped until the stones are cleared; millers fear losing the harvest. She
fights only if someone poisons or confines the stream. The disease spirit seeks
possession and cannot be solved by repairing the weir alone. Waist-deep current,
slick timbers, and machinery divide the scene. Lantern spirit-workers can expel
the predator; labor and Natural Lore can restore flow. Downstream travel
provides a safe retreat.

#### 6. Old Winter Tollhouse

A **troll** has occupied a ruined mountain tollhouse because hunters killed the
goats it followed. Snapped pines, stones smeared with fat, and wounds that leave
surprisingly little blood warn of regeneration. It wants food and an undisturbed
den, not treasure. The stone causeway is exposed and offers no room to circle;
fire can suppress regeneration but may collapse the timber roof on everyone.
The party can drive replacement goats into a side valley, bargain through signs
and offerings, wait for the troll to forage, or retreat before entering the
causeway. Embercoil refuges want the route open; Bramble delegates oppose
moving the problem into their forest.

#### 7. The Drowned Assembly

In the Shattered Strait, an air-filled ruin is exposed for one hour at the
lowest tide. Merfolk petitioners once met surface delegates there. Gouges on
stone, missing seals, and abruptly empty fish shoals warn that a **sea serpent**
now nests below the entrance. It is an animal defending eggs, not an agent of
the Choirhaven Compact. A direct fight risks the creature's immense strength and the
incoming sea. Five-Reed divers can chart a second air pocket; sailors can time an
escape; characters may distract the serpent with prey or wait until hatching.
The ruin holds testimony about the coastal articles, making it valuable to the
Compact, Open Keel League, and Sepulchral Regency.

#### 8. Ash Crown Ossuary

The Regency's field archive occupies a dry necropolis beneath a ring of ash
trees. Chimney smoke rises on windless days, grave seals have been replaced
with legal wax, and living clerks buy food in nearby villages. Regent Ossan's
deputy is a named **mummy** supported by a few **skeletons** and **zombies**, not
an army. The dead defend records and captured witnesses under narrow written
orders. The living Continuists negotiate safe access if promised a public
hearing. Fire is effective against the mummy but could destroy the evidence;
collapsed side passages permit stealth and withdrawal. The archive connects
the Regency to document thefts after the failed Renewal, but also proves several
current faction claims false.

combat.

### Six opening adventures

#### The packet under Gloam Eyrie

Talar Quickrain hires the characters to reopen an abandoned relay and recover a
sealed dispatch bag. Kessa Marr shelters there; she attacked the Gloamharrow
shepherds under the full moon but did not harm the couriers. A giant hawk and
storm grounded the route during the Renewal, and Regency agents now watch the
road for logs proving the absences were unrelated. Talon and wolf tracks remain
distinct. The party can arrange Kessa's guarded journey to Lantern Row, recover
the bag, expose the watchers, and decide whether the Chorus may copy sealed
evidence before delivery.

#### Three days of flood

Reedwake and the Five-Reed Assembly disagree by three days about opening the
flood gates. The characters carry gauges between raft hall and marsh, protect
workers without turning villagers into enemies, and discover that one marker
was moved for a new floating garden. Neither reading is fraudulent. Opening
early saves Reedwake but strands downstream gardens; opening late reverses the
harm. A new schedule requires public consent and a defensible witness record.

#### The bell that confesses

Bellfound's answering bell repeats secrets spoken nowhere near the foundry.
Panic points toward an Open Hand apprentice. Mother Hollowtone uses her learned
`Scry·Mind` to steal a nearby worker's secret and `Bend·Air/Storm` to voice it
through bronze, paying and rolling for each. The party can exploit daylight,
negotiate a boundary, or fight on terrible ground. Founder Dela Nymm, living
deaf under an alias at the House of the Last Lamp, knows she ordered the breach.

#### Judgment at Witness Ford

The Long Witness Circuit deputizes the party to locate the relief recitation
before armed eastbank keepers close Witness Ford. It rests in Hushwife's Barrow,
where the mummy releases it only if the party recovers stolen grave goods. The
theft leads to a Regency clerk with lawful-looking papers. Returning the text
makes the contradiction undeniable. The party must weigh hosted local control,
immediate relief passage, or a costly neutral court; destroying it makes one
claim simpler and justice less honest.

#### Air for Choirhaven

Ista Shellbell needs allies to restore a refuge air chamber before Five-Reed
petitioners arrive. An Open Keel house holds the repair timber but demands
access to the Drowned Assembly. The sea serpent makes that access lethal until
its eggs hatch. The party can broker delayed recovery, mount a timed search, or
find a second record in the Ash Crown Ossuary. Failure closes Choirhaven's
pilgrim route and sends travelers to unsafe privateers.

#### Warmth behind the sealed door

Serrak Warm-Coil hires the party to enter an underway whose heat vents are
failing. An oread uses its listed `Bend·Earth/Stone` to reshape geothermal vents
because an old refuge cut into its domain; redirected warmth is a natural
consequence. A troll occupies the tollhouse above. Engineering can prove the
sanctuary cannot survive unchanged. The characters must carry bad news, arrange
evacuation or a new agreement, and resist elders who prefer silence until winter
makes choice impossible.

### If no one intervenes

Advance this timeline after major adventures or when the characters ignore the
relevant crisis. Slow or redirect it when their actions warrant; do not preserve
events merely because they are written here.

1. **Competing lists:** Hearth Moot and Ashwake keepers publish different
   delegate rolls. Weather delays High Chorus objections, and each list gains
   local legitimacy.
2. **The closed ford:** A guardian spirit injures travelers at Witness Ford.
   Eastbank keepers close one approach, relief companies occupy the other, and
   Long Witness judges camp between them.
3. **Garden and haven:** Five-Reed stores restrict exports while Open Keel
   rescuers occupy a Duskport pier and Choirhaven closes a pilgrim route. Prices
   rise; factions hire private guards.
4. **The lawful dead:** Regent Ossan produces old seals and missing testimony,
   then claims temporary stewardship of Concord Hearth. Living Continuists make
   the claim respectable.
5. **Broken hospitality:** An Embercoil refuge closes, Bramble boundary marks
   burn, and two local covenant anchors are destroyed in reprisal. Spirits
   enforce fewer shared boundaries.
6. **The eclipse assembly:** Every power arrives at Concord Hearth with armed
   escorts, a different attendance list, and a defensible draft. Several groups
   begin separate renewal rituals while ancestors and guardians press
   incompatible promises. Ossan offers undying clerks as one solution, not a
   hidden master plan. Only negotiated articles—each built and paid for under
   normal Shaping rules—can create another Concord.

### Principles for campaigns in Veyra

- Keep powerful creatures individual and motivated. Add skill, equipment,
  abilities, or cells to a named opponent instead of adding a crowd.
- Telegraph danger through tracks, victims, local expertise, and changes in the
  environment. Let preparation, negotiation, avoidance, and retreat work.
- Make magic public but expertise scarce. A useful local rite should create
  obligations and limits, not erase travel, labor, scarcity, wounds, or
  politics.
- Treat every people as internally divided and capable of alliance. Hostile
  undead, predatory spirits, and dangerous beasts do not make a living culture
  default enemies.
- Let faction victories solve real problems at real costs. The Hearth Moot can
  coordinate food, Ashwake can preserve testimony, Bramble Commons can sustain
  forests, Open Keel can rescue crews, and the Regency can preserve records.
  Intrigue matters because no faction is wrong about everything.
- Never settle the world's metaphysics from behind the screen. The Living
  Chronicle, Six Courtesies, and Deep Resonance should each produce useful
  predictions, moral insight, and dangerous overconfidence.
