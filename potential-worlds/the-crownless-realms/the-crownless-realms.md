# The Crownless Realms

> **Candidate setting:** This unpublished, noncanonical world uses Fantasy Crux
> 2's rules, Shaping, creature profiles, and equipment without new mechanics.

## Player Primer

### A realm held together by old roads and new bargains

Ninety-three years ago, Emperor Caldras IV entered the Hall of Measures to be
crowned and never emerged. The ministers waiting outside heard the coronation
bells ring once, backward. When they forced the bronze doors, the throne was
empty, the imperial crown was ash, and every mirror in the hall reflected a
different claimant.

The empire came apart by writ, siege, unpaid garrison, marriage, and quiet theft.
Its roads, aqueducts, keeps, and sea walls still bind the continent. Seven great
enchanted public works—the Crownworks—perform fragments of their old purposes.
All seven are failing.

The successor realms have laws, grudges, and grandchildren, but none can
dominate. Three noble houses claim the vanished throne. Free cities defend their
charters. Elven,
dwarven, goblin, orc, lizardfolk, merfolk, and pixie powers negotiate as states,
not curiosities. Temples preach civic duty beneath the Seven Lamps, while
philosophers quarrel over whether any god has ever answered them.

This is high magic as a ruined capital is wealthy: visible, inherited, local,
narrow, and expensive. A village may have a boundary stone that turns grave-cold
near undead and no one capable of repair. A Shaper may calm one river bend, then
lack the PP to repeat it before resting. The impossible does not make food, safe
roads, Surgery, or honest officials commonplace.

Adventurers matter because they can go where armies cannot. A chartered company
can cross a disputed border without starting a war, examine a Crownwork without
admitting a claimant owns it, or recover an anchor before a rival learns it was
missing.

### What everyone knows

- **There is no crowned emperor.** Anyone who claims otherwise is making a
  political statement, committing treason somewhere, or both.
- **The old infrastructure is dying.** The Crownroads crack, ward-stones go
  silent, and once-predictable gates open onto the wrong destinations. Failures
  are still rare enough to be alarming and common enough to support a profession.
- **Magic is real and dangerous.** People know prayers, diagrams, songs, bargains,
  and battle gestures that produce effects. Only rare scholars recognize these
  practices as expressions of the same Techniques and Forms.
- **A Tell can save your life.** Soldiers learn to recognize the copper taste,
  doubled shadow, ringing glass, luminous script, or other unavoidable sign of a
  Shaping. They may not know what is coming, but they know to break the Practice,
  seek cover, Counter, or run.
- **A serious wound changes the plan.** A Wounded traveler needs Surgery or
  equivalent treatment before natural recovery. Wise companies identify healers.
- **The roads are political.** A bridge toll, ferry seal, clan banner, or city
  charter may matter more than a sword. People who can read local authority
  survive longer.
- **Nonhumans are neighbors and powers.** Every named people may hold lawful
  authority; species alone does not determine allegiance.
- **The gods are disputed, not forgotten.** The Synod runs hospitals, courts,
  schools, and cemeteries. Its prayers use the same Shaping limits; whether that
  proves divine favor is the oldest argument in the realms.

and the adjoining Pearlwater. Roughly two-thirds of play lies on continental
roads, farms, forests, uplands, and marches; the remaining third lies along three
long coasts and among the Shard Isles.

### Eight broad regions

#### Crown Vale

The former imperial heartland is a quilt of claimant estates, charter cities,
temple holdings, and villages that still call themselves imperial because no one
has told the tax rolls otherwise. Wheat fields and vineyards surround abandoned
signal towers. Crownseat, the old capital, remains the largest city in the west:
three governments occupy different districts, each issuing permits for the
whole. The empty Hall of Measures is sealed behind three competing guard posts.

This is the realm of succession intrigue, forged writs, urban expeditions, and
ruins under inhabited streets. Its danger wears livery. A company may be hired to
escort a witness, then discover that every faction wants the witness alive for a
different trial.

#### The Greenward

West of Crown Vale, forests swallow the old provincial boundary. The Verdant
Court is an elven federation of grove-cities, human woodland cantons, dryad
domains, and road abbeys. Its law distinguishes between land that may be owned,
land that may only be tended, and land old enough to be consulted. Outsiders
dismiss this as mysticism, but change their minds when they see a dangerous
bridge officially closed by the unanimous vote of its engineers, its villagers,
and the naiad beneath it.

The Greenward is neither timeless nor united. Younger elves want roads reopened;
old houses remember imperial logging levies; human charcoal towns fear elven
courts will erase their charters. Heavy woodland halves ordinary travel rates.

#### The Ironspines and Nine Warrens

The northern mountains contain the dwarven cities of the Deep-Road Compact and
the goblin commonwealth called the Nine Warrens. Their tunnels overlap like two
legal arguments. Dwarves hold the monumental roads, ventilation towers, and
deep bridges; goblin leagues hold side galleries, hot springs, mushroom caverns,
and most of the trade that actually keeps miners fed.

Dor-Morak is the Compact's principal southern gate-hold, built into the stepped
cliffs where the road from Crownseat enters the Ironspines and joins the Deep
Causeway. Its daylight terraces hold caravan yards, embassies, lift houses, and
metal markets; its deeper wards contain clan vaults, foundries, and the elected
engineering council. Dwarves govern the hold, but goblin factors and surface
merchants are essential to its trade. Visitors see a fortress cut into a
mountain; Moruni say it is a treaty with the mountain that must be renewed every
day.

Farther east, Kar-Morun is the Compact's great forge-hold and arms market, where
deep iron becomes Moruni steel. Banner clans, claimant agents, and Ninefold
factors all buy there, leaving its council to decide not what it can make, but
whom it can afford to arm.

The Compact and the Warrens have been allies, rivals, and besiegers within living
memory. Both now face subsidence along the old imperial Deep Causeway. Surface
lords covet their iron and engineers. Things sealed below the causeway test every
new crack. Underground travel rewards survey, Mechanisms, light discipline, and
the humility to retreat before a sound becomes a creature.

#### The Emberplain March

Eastward plains belong to the Iron Banner March, an orc-led confederation of
clans, farm towns, veteran colonies, and mobile herding courts. The empire
contracted the Banner to guard its frontier, then branded the same warriors as
raiders whenever it failed to pay them. Banner courts still preserve the old
service contracts alongside the warrants that contradicted them.

Banner law is blunt, literate, and obsessed with witnessed obligation. A guest
may be safer under an orc roof than in a claimant palace, but an oath-breaker can
be hunted across three realms. Wyverns nest in the red mesas. Old battlefield
dead rise when weather exposes imperial mass graves. The March offers cavalry
war, contested water, clan politics, and courts where a scar may be admissible
evidence.

#### The Saffron Reach

Beyond the eastern escarpment lies hot savanna, irrigated lowland, and salt
desert governed by the Saffron Scale Throne. Its lizardfolk calendar-judges
divide authority by season. Flood magistrates govern canals during the wet
months, while dry magistrates govern roads during the dry months; neither office
may command the other during the other's season. Human and orc communities hold
seats in the same courts, while caravan guilds exploit each seasonal transfer of
authority to seek favorable routes, tolls, and permits.

On the Reach's western edge, Synod estates surround the Lantern Aqueduct, which
carries protected water toward southern Crown Vale.

Imperial maps label the Reach a conquered province. No surviving Scale record
agrees. The region's buried reservoirs, crocodile waterways, giant-lizard
caravans, and sunken observatories attract treasure hunters. Heat, thirst, poison,
and the wrong season's warrant kill more reliably than monsters.

#### The Wester Capes

Cliff towns, fog islands, and wind-cut pasture stretch west of the Greenward.
The League of Open Cities was born here when five ports bought their charters
from three different claimants and dared any of them to admit the fraud. The
League's merchant councils protect free passage, private contracts, and their
own monopolies with equal conviction.

This is a place of smuggling coves, signal towers, shipyards, and old sea forts.
Harpy nests and griffin aeries overlook shipping lanes. League captains know a
Pearl Compact navigator is worth more than another mast, but many resent the
Compact's new tolls and its claim of authority over routes their families sailed
for generations as open water.

#### The Salt Coast

South of Crown Vale, broad estuaries and tidal marshes hold mixed cities older
than the empire. The Whitewater March—an established hub for chartered
adventuring companies—occupies the lower river. Vellbridge stands inland, while
the Crownroad runs south across the White Span to Gull's Rest and the open sea.
Farther west, goblin salt factors and elven shipwrights share flood-prone wards.

The Tide Lock Crownwork guides currents along the entire Salt Coast and should
divert the worst storm surges into the marshes before they reach its ports and
lowland settlements. The Lock now sounds its warning at low tide and sometimes
closes when the water is already high. Coastal campaigns combine court business
with muddy boots: missing cargo, compromised levees, customs murders, wreck
salvage, and villages that have one night to prepare for a sea serpent.

#### Pearlwater and the Shard Isles

The Pearlwater is governed as much below the surface as above it. The merfolk
Pearl Compact controls reef passages, tidal anchorages, surface platforms, and
air-filled halls built into sea caves. Merfolk breathe air but can remain
submerged for long stretches, so their settlements are engineered around
breathing wells, sheltered chambers, and easy access to the surface.

The Shard Isles contain independent barons, temple refuges, pirate republics,
and the shattered observatories of the imperial storm fleet. Sea travel can be
fast, but the margin between an intact ship and drowning is small. A strong wind
may double a day's passage; a storm can strip Structure from a vessel before a
sword leaves its scabbard.

Humans are numerous but politically divided. Their advantage is not a single
empire-building culture; it is that no border can plausibly call them foreign as
a whole. Elves of the Araini groves are long-lived forest citizens, not secluded
spectators. Dwarves of the Moruni holds combine clan inheritance with elected
engineering councils. Goblins made the Nine Warrens into a commercial
commonwealth because surviving between larger powers required reliable rules.
Orcs of the Banner prize strength, but define it as the ability to carry an
obligation when conditions turn hostile. Lizardfolk organize public life around
water, heat, and seasonal authority. Merfolk make maritime sovereignty possible
through pilots, reefs, and air-linked coastal architecture. Pixies are physically
small, politically protected, and very difficult to prevent from delivering bad
news.

Use the existing fantasy-race guidance and creature profiles when a player
chooses a nonhuman character. Dwarf and elf player packages are already
specified. Goblin, orc, and lizardfolk require the same profile-to-player
procedure and Gamemaster approval; use the existing Lizardman profile for
lizardfolk. Merfolk and pixies can create pronounced movement, Size, and access
differences; include them only when the campaign can support those differences
rather than quietly ignoring them.

### Religion under the Seven Lamps

The Synod teaches that seven divine Lamps illuminate mortal life:
**Hearth**, which keeps; **Gate**, which judges passage; **Scale**, which weighs;
**Rose**, which binds love and grief; **Hammer**, which gives labor dignity;
**Storm**, which tests; and **Star**, which guides the dead. Most temples contain
seven niches and emphasize two or three. Names and genders change by region.

The gods do not appear in public and settle disputes. No universally accepted
scripture contains a statement that cannot be translated three ways. Yet Synod
hospitals work, oaths sworn at Gate altars carry social force, and some priests
Shape through prayer with astonishing skill. Skeptics say disciplined ritual is
sufficient. Believers answer that discipline is how mortals become audible.

Temple miracles are not effortless gifts. Priests must learn their sacred arts,
draw upon their own strength, and accept that every working leaves signs others
may recognize. Miracles can be opposed, and reaching beyond one's abilities
carries a price. The Synod's achievement is institutional: it trains healers,
maintains supplies, records who can treat a Major Wound, and stations them along
important roads.

Ancestor observances, elven land consultations, dwarven memorial guilds, Banner
oath fires, Saffron solar courts, and Pearlwater tide vigils coexist with Synod
worship. None requires a new magic system. A spirit may be encountered using an
existing profile, while a rite performed by a Shaper uses the normal Technique
and Form cells.

### Shaping in daily life

People usually learn a rite before they learn a theory. A Whitewater ferryman
might know that Surveyor Kelm taps three brass nails before sensing a damaged
route. A Moruni miner knows the blue grit around a dwarf stone-reader's hands
means to stop hammering. Neither needs the terms `Scry·Paths` or
`Scry·Earth/Stone`. Those exact cells are the rules truth and the language of a
small cross-tradition scholarly network.

Most settled districts can name one Novice or Apprentice Shaper. Their useful
magic is minor or specialist in scale and bounded by PP. Veterans are recruited
by name, and Experts draw the attention of courts. Masters belong to legend,
terrifying old beings, or campaigns in which a lasting price can redraw history.

Every tradition teaches recognizable rituals, though individual Shapers develop
their own variations. Common examples include:

| Tradition           | What its magic often looks like                                                              | Favored cells                                              |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crown Survey        | Recite a measured route while aligning brass tools; nearby straight edges seem to double     | `Scry·Paths`, `Ward·Paths`, `Alter·Earth/Stone`            |
| Synod service       | Spoken litany with lamp, cord, or washing; the worker throws seven faint shadows              | `Alter·Flesh`, `Ward·Spirit`, `Scry·Spirit`                |
| Moruni stonecraft   | Hammer rhythm and traced load lines; dust rises in geometric ridges                          | `Scry·Earth/Stone`, `Bend·Earth/Stone`, `Ward·Earth/Stone` |
| Araini husbandry    | Breath, sung names, and living focus; leaves turn their pale sides outward                   | `Alter·Flesh`, `Scry·Flesh`, `Ward·Flesh`                  |
| Banner oathwork     | Declare witness and consequence over iron; every voice nearby carries a second beat          | `Bend·Fate`, `Ward·Mind`, `Unmake·Force/Motion`            |
| Ninefold wayfinding | Knotted thread, rapid notation, and counted corners; nearby corners seem briefly nearer      | `Scry·Paths`, `Scry·Fate`, `Conjure·Mind`                  |

Whatever the tradition, Shaping cannot be performed invisibly: its rituals can
be interrupted, and its effects leave recognizable signs. Institutions guard
their teaching because it is scarce and politically useful.

The Crownworks make the world feel more magical than its living population could
support. Each is a permanent supernatural enchantment with vulnerable physical
or spiritual anchors. They can be sensed and, through their anchors, Dispelled.
Their makers paid prices no current archive records cleanly. Repairing one is not
a Craft roll with glowing tools; it is the subject of expeditions, rituals,
negotiation, and possibly another lasting price.

### The Whitewater March: starting region

The Whitewater March is prosperous by Crownless standards: grain barges run on
schedule, its road watch is usually paid, and three powers disagree over its
ownership without currently besieging it. It gives a new company three useful
scales of play.

- **The city ring:** Vellbridge offers same-day investigations, guild feuds,
  temple politics, burglaries, and court hearings.
- **The frontier ring:** the White Span and four satellite settlements lie within
  one or two days, close enough for urgent travel and far enough for weather,
  Wounds, and pursuit to matter.
- **The expedition ring:** Crownseat, the Greenward, Ironspines, and Shard Isles
  require multi-day planning, supply, border terms, and a safe way home.

#### Vellbridge

Vellbridge stands beside the Whitewater where the imperial Crownroad enters the
March. About 500 metres south of the city, the road crosses the **White Span**,
one of the seven Crownworks, before continuing roughly 12 km to Gull's Rest. The
Span is a monumental bridge-causeway of pale stone, 30 km from gate to gate,
though travelers walking it count fewer steps than its survey length should
require. It once remained dry, lit, and structurally sound in any weather. Now
its lamps dim in sequence, distances lurch by a few paces, and one parapet weeps
salt water at noon.

Thirty thousand residents live among tiled counting houses, crowded river wards,
old barracks, shrines, mills, and the claimant manors on Measure Hill. The elected
March Council governs markets and drains. House Veyran appoints the road warden.
The League of Open Cities holds the river customs contract. The Synod claims the
chapel at the White Span's north gate. None can act without the others accusing
it of preparing an annexation.

Useful places include:

- **The Brazen Mule**, an outfitter near South Gate, sells ordinary core-rule
  weapons, armour, general gear, trail supplies, and access to draft animals at
  prosperous-city availability. Proprietor Hesta Coil refuses to call debt
  charity but extends it to companies who bring wounded hirelings home.
- **The House of the Rose Lamp** maintains Healing Kits, safe beds, trained
  healers, and Surgery. Senior surgeon Doctor Samel Ancor is not a Shaper and
  considers this professionally important. Sister Irra Vale can provide
  `Alter·Flesh` at Intensity 3+ as Surgery when present and able to pay its PP.
- **Third Measure Hall** is a reduced Crown Survey office. It offers a possible
  Shaping initiation after a candidate assists a real survey, demonstrates
  restraint around an unstable anchor, and accepts continuing obligations. It is
  not initiation on demand.
- **The Bent Copper** is a respectable bathhouse above an unrespectable archive.
  Its goblin manager, “Aunt” Neris Skell, is the best underworld contact in the
  March for smuggling, forged travel seals, stolen correspondence, and rumors.
  She sells introductions, not loyalty.
- **Thistledown Station Seven** occupies the old western bell tower. Pixie relays
  fly sealed message tubes between weather shelters. Normal service is physical,
  tiring, and delayed by storms or predators. A few trained couriers know
  `Bend·Paths` for short, rules-legal relocations; there is no instantaneous
  long-range post. Postmaster Pella Underleaf treats neutrality as sacred and
  unpaid postage as a personal insult.
- **The Charter House** records small adventuring companies. A charter grants no
  immunity, but it names legal partners, distributes salvage shares, and lets
  authorities hire a company without recognizing a rival state's soldiers.

#### Four satellite settlements

**Rillmarket**, 28 km northwest on good road, collects grain, horses, and news.
Its timber palisade encloses three inns and a weekly claimant court. A dry culvert
beneath the market leads toward an imperial storehouse no map admits exists.

**Blackglass**, 24 km east through light brush, grew around a dark-stone quarry.
Dwarven engineers inspect its dangerous faces; goblin crews work its best
mechanisms. The quarry exposed an imperial acoustic conduit linked to the White
Span's anchor casing beneath the crossing, and all work is officially paused.

**Hollow Ward**, 30 km north on the Crownroad, is a half-inhabited imperial fort.
The March Watch holds its gate, Banner traders hold its caravan yard, and
something opens the sealed infirmary from inside on moonless nights.

**Gull's Rest**, 42 km south by maintained road or river, is the March's seaport.
The road reaches it across the White Span. Its tidal basin supports fishing
boats, river barges, one-masted couriers, and larger League ships. Pearl Compact
pilots operate an air-filled treaty hall cut into the breakwater. A merfolk
visitor can reach every chamber by flooded ramp and surface frequently; landfolk
enter through the upper door.

#### Ready character premises

1. **The new charter:** Each character has bought, inherited, or worked off one
   share in the same Vellbridge company. The March Council offers its first
   contract: determine why a bridge lamp went dark without damaging the Span or
   letting a claimant seize the investigation.
2. **The missing survey:** Each character is tied to a member of Survey Team
   Hallow, missing after descending beneath the White Span. The Survey calls it
   a routine inspection; a fourth member surfaced alone and sold Aunt Neris a
   copied notebook.
3. **The hard road home:** The characters survived the same disastrous caravan
   journey. They owe Doctor Ancor for Surgery, Hesta Coil for equipment, and one
   another for getting back alive. A lawful salvage warrant offers a way to clear
   the debt—if three other parties have not bought the same warrant.

## Gamemaster Gazetteer

Everything below reveals setting secrets, active conspiracies, site contents, and
an advancing crisis. Players should stop here unless the group prefers open
information.

### Running the Crownless tone

The setting works when splendor and vulnerability appear together. Show an
impossible bridge shortening a road, then reveal its cracked anchor. Let a temple
Shaper stop Bleeding, then ask who has the Healing Kit and safe room for the next
patient. Keep dragons, spirits, portals, and enchantments visible while preserving
the importance of maps, witnesses, armour, food, sailors, and retreat.

Combat should rarely be the only honest solution. Telegraph threats: carcasses
before a wyvern, unnatural silence before undead, scales before a sea serpent,
warped stone before a golem. Negotiation, preparation, avoidance, and escape are
successes. A named political enemy usually needs better skills, equipment,
position, and allies—not an unwieldy crowd.

The principal campaign question is not “Who deserves the crown?” It is “What
should people preserve when the system that built their world cannot be trusted?”
Claimants, republics, old peoples, and new institutions answer differently.

### What Shaping reliably does

The metaphysics remain unresolved, but the observable process is consistent.

1. A Shaper learns an exact relationship between one Technique and one Form—a
   cell—not an unlimited school of effects.
2. The Practice makes the intended relationship coherent and interruptible. Its
   words, diagrams, tools, gestures, prayers, or trance differ by tradition.
3. The Shaper's Power Points pay for imposing that relationship. The formula
   `sum of outcome Intensities + Range + Duration + Reach + adjustments = Magnitude`
   measures the burden of the complete Shaping, not how ornate its description sounds.
4. The Tell is reality's unavoidable local response around Shaper and ranged
   target or area. Concealing it requires Veiled and raises Magnitude.
5. Safe Magnitude reflects expertise. Overreach can fail with PP loss and
   Backlash; a fumble does so even within safety. This is why institutions prize
   conservative practitioners and why interrupting a Practice is effective.
6. Continuing effects occupy active capacity: total active Magnitude cannot
   exceed permanent, unmodified POW. Indefinite effects commit maximum PP. No
   tradition escapes these limits by changing vocabulary.
7. Permanent supernatural enchantments need vulnerable physical or spiritual
   anchors and remain active and Dispellable through them. Permanent mundane
   creation and completed permanent transformations follow their own rule.

Rituals distribute preparation and, where permitted, pledged PP; they do not
increase a Shaper's maximum Intensity. Any dial at 5 is Mythic and exacts a
lasting price. These facts are known to the few scholars able to compare
traditions. They are also why Crownwork repair cannot become a routine civic
expense.

#### Three theories, none confirmed

**The First Accord** says reality is a covenant made of permissions, names, and
reciprocal obligations. A Technique is a kind of legal act; a Form identifies the
party addressed; Practice states the petition; PP is earnest payment; and the
Tell is public witness. Its strongest evidence is Fate, Spirit, thresholds, and
the strange importance of declared outcomes. Banner oath-workers, many elves,
and legal theologians favor it. Its weakness is indifferent matter: fire and
stone respond to a correct cell when no plausible will hears the bargain.

**The Lattice** says existence has an underlying geometry. Cells are valid
operations upon ten families of structure. Practice constructs the proof,
Magnitude describes its length, PP supplies the work, and Backlash is a failed
operation resolving through the nearest available structure. Crown Surveyors,
Moruni engineers, and Ninefold analysts favor it. The Lattice predicts spatial
and material Shapings well, but becomes metaphorical when explaining grief,
souls, vows, and why two equally correct diagrams can have different Tells.

**Divine Writ** says the Seven Lamps authored the permitted operations of the
world. Shaping is possible because creation remains legible; Practice is an act
of reading, PP is mortal breath, and Tell is the reflected light of authorship.
The Synod favors it, though not every priest does. It explains the consistency of
cells across cultures and the high price of boundary-breaking Mythic work. It
cannot explain why blasphemers, undead minds, and practitioners who deny every
god can Shape as effectively as believers.

Do not reveal a winner. Evidence discovered in play should strengthen one theory
while creating a harder question. Even the imperial makers disagreed: Crownwork
plans contain contractual language, geometric proofs, and hymns in the same
hand.

### The seven failing Crownworks

Each Crownwork is a complex of one or more permanent, anchored Shapings. Its
observed functions should be expressed through existing cells when exact
resolution matters. Repairs may stabilize mundane masonry without magic; true
restoration needs access to anchors, capable Shapers, an appropriate ritual, and
possibly a lasting price. Nothing here grants a new spell or item.

| Crownwork                | Public function and present failure                                                                                            | Concealed anchor problem                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The White Span**       | Shortens and protects the lower Whitewater crossing between Vellbridge and Gull's Rest; distances now lurch and salt water appears | One of nine brass route-rods was replaced with a convincing mundane copy                                |
| **The Deep Causeway**    | Stabilizes the most important Ironspine road with Earth/Stone workings; collapses advance from below                           | Its foundation anchor is intact but being deliberately moved a handspan at a time                       |
| **The Tide Lock**        | Directs coastal currents and denies the worst surges across the Salt Coast; linked gates now close at dangerous tides          | Its treaty pearl remains seated, but the Pearl Compact has legally withdrawn the consent it represented |
| **The Lantern Aqueduct** | Carries and protects clean water into southern Crown Vale; sickness now follows particular channels                            | Its fixed Reach ends at the old boundary; three expanded districts draw through unprotected connectors  |
| **The Orchard Bell**     | Protects a belt of orchards through layered works; outer sections fail each winter                                             | Blight kills outer root anchors, producing discrete failures                                            |
| **The Storm Needle**     | Scries approaching weather and once signaled the Wester Capes; it now gives precise false warnings                             | Someone sustains a Veiled indefinite Shaping that biases its mundane selector gears                     |
| **The Gate of Names**    | Watches and denies passage to marked imperial corpses beneath Crownseat; dead officials now appear above                       | Grave registers used as linked anchors are being stolen and sold as succession evidence                 |

The shared crisis is coordination, not a single magical infection. The Crownworks
have distinct, often legitimate weaknesses. The Pale Cabinet, a compartment
inside the Office of Continuance, worsens them so age looks like the only cause
and emergency rule like the only cure.

#### How the Tide Lock fails

The great seaward structure marked on the map is the Lock's primary control
station and houses the treaty-pearl anchor. The Tide Lock is not a single
barrier: mundane bronze gates, spillways, and secondary anchor chambers extend
through the Salt Coast's estuaries and marshes. Permanent `Scry·Water/Ice`,
`Bend·Water/Ice`, and `Ward·Paths` workings link the network. The first reads
tide and pressure across the coast, the second guides water toward safe channels,
and the third denies a dangerous surge passage into protected harbors and river
mouths. Crews can operate local spillways during a failure, but they cannot
coordinate the network or reproduce the Crownwork's speed and force.

The treaty pearl is neither a power source nor a simple key. It anchors the
Pearl Compact's continuing consent to the Lock's use of merfolk waters. The
imperial ritual treats that permission as one of its operating conditions, just
as real as water level or gate position. The Pearl Compact has lawfully withdrawn
its consent, but the pearl remains physically seated. The anchor therefore presents
the Crownwork with two incompatible facts: the installed token says the treaty
still authorizes the Lock, while the living parties to that treaty say it does
not.

The contradiction makes the Lock choose the wrong thresholds. It may sound its
warning when there is no danger, close the seaward gates after a surge has
entered, or keep landward spillways shut and trap water against the districts it
was built to protect. Simply reseating the pearl changes nothing, while removing
it without the rejected legal procedure could unravel the protections attached
to it. A lasting repair requires a renewed treaty, a consensual removal and new
anchor, or a costly redesign by capable Shapers. This is why diplomacy is part
of the engineering problem rather than merely an obstacle to reaching it.

### Faction dossiers

Each entry gives enough pressure to make the faction useful immediately. Favored
cells describe trained members, not every member, and the Tell is an example for
that tradition rather than a free effect.

#### The Claimant Houses

- **Public purpose:** Houses Veyran, Ordel, and Castaine each claim to preserve
  lawful succession until a true coronation can occur.
- **Concealed objective:** The houses jointly fund the Office of Continuance,
  whose jurists and engineers prepare lawful plans for an interregnum. Only its
  compartmentalized Pale Cabinet engineers Crownwork failures; house leaders
  believe that wing is testing repair and emergency procedures.
- **Internal fracture:** Veyran would expose sabotage and repair the works even
  at cost to its claim. Ordel wants evidence published as part of an elective
  settlement. Castaine wants quiet control of the works before any disclosure.
- **Resources and relations:** Estates, courts, soldiers, archives, and marriage
  ties. They compete openly, court the Synod, resent the League, and hire the
  Survey they undermine.
- **Leader shorthand:** Lady Maud Veyran, austere veteran and true believer;
  Prince Oren Ordel, charming constitutional schemer; Duchess Sal Castaine,
  patient creditor with impeccable manners.
- **Shaping:** Court adepts favor `Scry·Mind` and `Bend·Fate`; their Tell makes
  spoken titles echo from the wrong direction.
- **Current operation:** Rival house agents seek the White Span rod: Veyran to
  return it, Ordel to publish it, Castaine to impound it, and the Cabinet to
  destroy it.
- **What they ask:** Prove a rival tampered with the bridge, escort a genealogist,
  or retrieve a “private family relic.”
- **If ignored:** The Cabinet manufactures a bridge panic, and a claimant
  garrison enters Vellbridge in the sincere belief that it is emergency
  protection.

#### League of Open Cities

- **Public purpose:** Defend municipal charters, open trade, navigable water, and
  courts that enforce contracts across borders.
- **Concealed objective:** Convert failing Crownworks into a toll-funded League
  trust, making private creditors the practical heirs of empire.
- **Internal fracture:** Smaller cities need collective protection; the five
  founding ports intend to retain controlling votes.
- **Resources and relations:** Ships, money, warehouses, advocates, and spies
  disguised as factors. The League works with goblin and merfolk traders, opposes
  claimant control, and treats the Survey as an acquisition target.
- **Leader shorthand:** First Factor Ysabet Dorn, plainspoken, funny, and never
  surprised by a debt.
- **Shaping:** Navigators favor `Scry·Water/Ice` and `Ward·Paths`; their Tell beads
  moisture along every nearby written line.
- **Current operation:** Buy Blackglass quarry debts and the rights beneath them.
- **What they ask:** Protect an auditor, break a smuggling monopoly, or obtain a
  defensible copy of an imperial easement.
- **If ignored:** The League forecloses on Blackglass during the crisis and gains
  private access to the White Span's acoustic conduit.

#### Crown Survey

- **Public purpose:** Inspect roads, bridges, archives, boundary stones, and
  Crownworks without recognizing any successor crown.
- **Concealed objective:** Senior Surveyors believe the seven works form one
  distributed ritual. They suppress data that might prompt a claimant to attempt
  it before the pattern is understood.
- **Internal fracture:** Field teams favor public warnings; the Central Measure
  values neutrality and fears panic more than secrecy.
- **Resources and relations:** Maps, route seals, engineers, modest Shapers, and
  unrivaled institutional memory. Everyone needs the Survey and suspects it.
- **Leader shorthand:** Chief Measurer Tal Orsic, exhausted dwarf, exacting

- **Shaping:** `Scry·Paths`, `Ward·Paths`, and `Scry·Earth/Stone`; the Tell doubles
  brass edges, while a successful Scry supplies any useful direction.
- **Current operation:** Quietly recover Survey Team Hallow and its White Span
  readings.
- **What they ask:** Guard a survey, enter an unsafe Crownwork, or carry sealed
  measurements outside official channels.
- **If ignored:** Central Measure burns the compromising field archive, leaving
  the next repair attempt dangerously incomplete.

#### Synod of Seven Lamps

- **Public purpose:** Operate temples, hospitals, burial grounds, arbitration,

- **Concealed objective:** Preserve institutional unity by proving all legitimate
  Shaping traditions descend from Divine Writ, even if inconvenient records must

- **Internal fracture:** Hospital clergy value useful cooperation; doctrinal

- **Resources and relations:** Public trust, healers, safe houses, records of

- **Leader shorthand:** Canon Prelate Jeren Sorn, gentle bedside presence,

- **Shaping:** `Alter·Flesh`, `Ward·Spirit`, and `Scry·Spirit`; seven soft shadows

- **Current operation:** Move a recovered imperial hymn-proof from Vellbridge to
  the sealed library in Crownseat.
- **What they ask:** Escort patients, investigate disturbed graves, or recover a
  stolen reliquary-anchor without scandal.
- **If ignored:** Examiners blame heterodox Shapers for Crownwork failures and
  restrict hospital cooperation with them.

#### Deep-Road Compact

- **Public purpose:** Maintain dwarven holds, safe mining law, and the deep roads

- **Concealed objective:** Abandon one failing Causeway district and redirect its

- **Internal fracture:** Engineers say the sacrifice is necessary; families in

- **Shaping:** `Bend·Earth/Stone`, `Ward·Earth/Stone`, and `Scry·Earth/Stone`;

- **What they ask:** Enter an unstable gallery, escort evacuees discreetly, or

#### Verdant Court

- **Public purpose:** Defend the Greenward's peoples and living domains through
  negotiated stewardship.
- **Concealed objective:** Allow the Orchard Bell to fail in order to end an

- **Resources and relations:** Elven archers, foresters, long memories, living

### Eight ready-to-run sites

Use existing creature profiles and ordinary NPC guidance. The sites provide
positions, motives, and decisions rather than bespoke statistics.

#### 1. The ninth rod chamber

A maintenance stair beneath the White Span descends through three eras of
masonry. The lowest door opens only when approached from the “short” direction
of the bridge; careful measurement or `Scry·Paths` reveals the discrepancy.
Inside, eight brass route-rods hum around one dull replacement. Two gargoyles
roost in ventilation hollows, attracted by vibration rather than loyal to a
faction. Team Hallow's injured leader and two survivors hide beyond them with the
real rod. A Castaine clerk supplied the replacement on Cale Rusk's restoration
order, but it also bears a Ninefold archive mark. Removing or restoring anything
without a full survey risks a spatial lurch. The immediate reward is the team and
its evidence; the real choice is who may inspect the rod.

#### 2. Hollow Ward infirmary

The sealed infirmary opens because its old service golem still follows an order
to admit the wounded after sunset. It drags sleepers from the fort yard, treats
them as imperial casualties, and prevents them leaving until discharged by an
officer dead for a century. The rooms contain useful mundane medical stores,
some spoiled, and the officer's seal in a cabinet behind the surgical theatre.
The golem can be fought, avoided through shift records, or satisfied by presenting
a valid chain of command. In the crypt lies an intelligent mummy—the former
surgeon—who wants the golem stopped but refuses to surrender patient registers
that implicate House Veyran in wartime experiments.

#### 3. Blackglass undercut

The quarry's new face reveals a dark-glass acoustic conduit carrying vibration
to the White Span's anchor casing. Quarrying altered that physical resonance
path, and workers stopped before a dangerous oscillation. League,
Compact, and Ninefold crews occupy rival camps above. A giant spider in a warm
side shaft is announced by drained carcasses and webbed tools. On the deepest
brace, a stabilizing screw-jack has been mounted backward to widen the conduit
with every vibration. Its serial matches an Office of Continuance loan signed by
Pale Cabinet surveyor Cale Rusk. It proves deliberate interference, but not that
any claimant leader authorized it. Reaching it needs Engineering, quiet movement,
or agreement among three groups trying to “secure” the site.

#### 4. The drowned tollhouse

At low tide outside Gull's Rest, the roof of an imperial customs fort breaks the
surface. Its upper rooms are air-filled for only two hours. Lower corridors remain
flooded, and neither landfolk nor air-breathing merfolk can explore them without
planning around suffocation. A giant octopus shelters in the collapsed warehouse
and defends eggs rather than treasure. A ledger records the treaty pearl's
original delivery to the still-seated Tide Lock and a rejected legal procedure
for removing it. Pearl Compact agents want the ledger; League wreckers claim the
structure as salvage. A retreat timed before the tide is a clean victory. Being
trapped turns an archive search into a survival problem.

#### 5. Station Black Measure

This Deep Causeway station hangs over a vertical fault on iron chains. Its anchor
platform has moved exactly a finger's width north during each of the last seven
new moons. A solitary troll lives in the warm drainage tunnel. It has learned
that striking the platform makes food-bearing inspectors arrive, so it repeats
the action when hungry; the “sabotage” is opportunistic rather than political.
Killing it is hazardous in tight dark space and its Regeneration is telegraphed
by recovered footprints after bloody encounters. Diverting drainage, feeding and
relocating it, or sealing its access can save the road. Compact officials prefer
a sabotage narrative that justifies their planned evacuation.

#### 6. The root crypt of the Orchard Bell

The Bell hangs from a vast living root beneath a roofless abbey. Blight has made
the root brittle, while an anchored dryad bound to an older tree insists the
imperial orchard must die. Synod pilgrims have tied hundreds of petitions to the
Bell, obscuring its physical anchor and adding fire risk. The blight is mundane
and treatable if discovered early; the political disease is harder. Orchard
families will accept smaller local protections only if the Court recognizes
their land claims. The dryad can identify the original graft and negotiate, but
will fight anyone who cuts the elder root. A hag in nearby limestone caves
secretly encourages all sides and can only range beyond its domain at night.

#### 7. The Storm Needle

The Needle rises from a Wester Cape accessible by a causeway at low tide. Its
weather chamber still observes accurately. League Keeper Jessa Kelm sustains a
Veiled indefinite Shaping that biases the mundane selector gears; the committed
PP and active Magnitude remain hers, and the effect can be sensed and Dispelled
directly. She meant to wreck a rival's ships, then learned the Pale Cabinet was
choosing targets. Harpies on the outer balconies bargain for food and safe
nesting rights. As a real storm approaches, the party must pursue Jessa, Dispel
the sabotage, or operate the signal lanterns before the causeway floods.

#### 8. The Observatory of Two Noons

Floodwater in the Saffron Reach reveals a brass dome only at midday. A mirrored
shaft records old storm cycles. A basilisk nests where the twin sunbeams warm its
clutch and canal vermin provide prey. Carved warning animals flank the opening,
and unwounded vermin lie dead nearby. Bronze
sight-baffles protect a cool north gallery, and bait can draw it into the outer
sun court while an iris closes behind it. It will not pursue past the cold flood
stair unless cornered, providing a safe withdrawal. Saffron astronomers want
observations, a claimant expedition wants a portable mirror, and Banner jurists
want a massacre order in the same cabinet. A bad salvage attempt could collapse
the structural anchor.

### Six opening adventures

#### The lamp that went dark

During evening traffic, one White Span lamp extinguishes and every person on the
bridge hears the coronation bell. The March Council charters the party to keep
the crossing open, find the cause, and avoid claimant occupation. Witnesses saw
Survey Team Hallow descend below; a Castaine agent offers money to recover a
“stolen rod”; Thistledown carries a forged evacuation order. The ninth rod
chamber provides danger and proof. Success means choosing who receives the rod,
the original field notes, and the public explanation.

#### Five hours to Gull's Rest

A storm warning reaches Vellbridge while riders report the Tide Lock closed at
high water. A fast ride can reach the port before the surge but risks Fatigue for
rider and mount; a slower force arrives too late. At Gull's Rest, Pearl envoys and
League officials are seconds from violence. The party can open mundane spillways,
carry terms into the treaty hall, or reach one of the Lock's accessible secondary
anchors. The crisis tests movement, negotiation, and whether anyone spends scarce
PP before the true storm arrives.

#### The patients of Hollow Ward

Three people vanish from a caravan yard; one crawls back with neat bandages and
an imperial discharge token. The fort commander wants a quiet recovery before
rumor empties the route. Inside, the service golem is dangerous but consistent,
and the mummy surgeon knows more than the commander expects. Fighting may rescue
the captives; solving the order can rescue them without destroying a surviving
piece of public infrastructure. The registers create a longer Veyran plot.

#### A share in Blackglass

Hesta Coil accepts the party's debt payment in the form of a simple escort job:
take League auditor Pell Dorr to Blackglass. Dwarven workers accuse Dorr of
engineering foreclosure; goblin salvagers possess a better map; and a worker is
missing below. The giant spider is a telegraphed physical danger, while sound
transmission to the anchor makes careless combat risky. Recovering the worker,
the sabotage evidence, and a lawful claim are three different objectives.

#### The letter with seven seals

Postmaster Pella has seven copies of one Survey warning, each authenticated and
worded differently. She hires the party to carry them outside the relay network
to Keeper Brindle. Claimant agents want one version destroyed. A goblin printer
wants all seven. At a grounded relay, giant hawks threaten the pixies but can be
driven off without a pitched fight. Comparing delivery times reveals that the
forgeries originated inside Central Measure, not Thistledown.

#### Grave credit

A merchant pays the company to recover an ancestor's debt tablet from a flooded
family crypt. The dead inside include a ghost seeking testimony and corporeal
undead still denied passage by a damaged fragment of the Gate of Names. The
tablet proves the merchant owns valuable land—and that the land was seized after
the Red Field massacre. Claimant, Banner, and Synod interests emerge as soon as
the party asks for translation. The adventure begins as salvage and ends as a
choice about evidence.

### The advancing crisis

Advance one step after a major failure, after two adventures in which the party
ignores the Crownworks, or whenever the campaign needs a harder public problem.
Do not erase victories: a repaired work remains stable, and its listed event
becomes an attempted incident the characters can expose.

| Step | Development                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---: | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|    1 | Forged Survey warnings spread. Thistledown slows priority post while it authenticates messages; every faction begins using private couriers.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|    2 | The White Span lurches during port and caravan traffic. No one must die, but trade halts and a claimant offers troops “temporarily.”                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|    3 | The Tide Lock mishandles a surge while the Storm Needle sends a false all-clear. Coastal factions mobilize and blame one another.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|    4 | Station Black Measure collapses unless previously secured. Compact secrecy and Ninefold publication turn engineering loss into civil unrest.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|    5 | Gate of Names registers disappear in bulk. Imperial dead emerge in Crownseat carrying forged orders that recognize the Office of Continuance as temporary custodian.                                                                                                                                                               |
|    6 | The claimant houses announce a joint emergency council. Their leaders discover that the Pale Cabinet controls its procedures, witnesses, and undead guards; each now needs outsiders it can trust.                                                                                                                                 |
|    7 | The Pale Cabinet attempts to make all seven Crownworks sound in one distributed ritual. Its leaders do not know whether this crowns a regent, restores an imperial command network, opens the Hall of Measures, or exacts a Mythic price from the realm. The party's alliances and preserved anchors determine what options exist. |

The final step is a convergence, not a predetermined apocalypse. Earlier
relationships supply the options: Survey knowledge, Synod records, Ninefold
maps, Compact engineering, living anchors, Banner witnesses, Saffron timing,
Pearl consent, League logistics, claimant access, and Thistledown communication.
No faction provides all of them. A dependable company can decide the future.
