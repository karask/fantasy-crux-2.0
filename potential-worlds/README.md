# Potential Worlds

> **Status: candidate material.** Nothing in this folder is part of the canonical Fantasy
> Crux 2.0 setting. These gazetteers are unpublished alternatives for playtesting and
> comparison; the core rules take precedence wherever a setting draft is unclear.

## Current candidates

- [The Crownless Realms](the-crownless-realms.md) — rival crowns, chartered companies,
  failing imperial enchantments, free cities, and organized temples.
- [The Elder Concord](the-elder-concord.md) — traveling courts, clan obligations,
  ancestor claims, local spirits, and territorial covenants whose renewal has failed.

## Shared assumptions

- Both settings aim for **high magic but grounded consequences**. Shaping is visible in
  institutions, crafts, law, and old infrastructure, but it does not erase work, distance,
  scarcity, wounds, or political uncertainty.
- Shaping always uses the canonical Techniques, Forms, cells, Magnitude, PP, Practice, Tell,
  Counter, Dispel, Backlash, ritual, anchor, and mythic-price rules. A setting's philosophies
  explain the art in-world; they do not replace or extend its mechanics.
- No single philosophy confirms the final nature of magic, souls, gods, or reality. The
  strongest answers remain competing Gamemaster-facing possibilities.
- Humans, elves, dwarves, goblins, orcs, lizardfolk, merfolk, and pixies all have cultures,
  interests, and political agency. A people is not a moral alignment or a single nation.
- Danger is legible before it is lethal. Investigation, preparation, negotiation, avoidance,
  and retreat are valid answers to threats that cannot be won by a fair fight.
- The gazetteers reuse the published creature profiles and rules. They do not add species
  packages, stat blocks, spells, items, or faction subsystems.
- Each document begins with spoiler-safe player material. Hidden motives, true explanations,
  faction secrets, and advancing consequences belong only after a clearly marked
  Gamemaster section.

## Evaluation questions

1. Which world gives a new group the clearest reason to stay together and take risks?
2. Which starting region produces the best first three sessions without requiring setting
   study?
3. Which factions create conflicts with several defensible sides rather than an obvious
   quest giver and villain?
4. Which interpretation of Shaping makes the rules feel native to the setting while leaving
   room for doubt?
5. Which dangers best support warning, hard choices, retreat, recovery, and return?
6. Do elves, dwarves, and the other peoples feel like distinct societies rather than familiar
   species stereotypes?
7. If all proper names were removed, would the worlds still be unmistakably different in
   government, religion, imagery, and adventure structure?
8. Which material would remain useful if the other world were selected?

## Candidate acceptance checklist

Apply every item to both gazetteers before selection.

### Rules fit

- [ ] All Shaping examples use one of the six Techniques and one of the ten Forms as an exact
      cell; Technique–Form analysis is rare scholarship rather than universal vocabulary.
- [ ] Practice and Tell remain observable, PP and active Magnitude remain limited, overreach
      can cause Backlash, Counter and Dispel remain possible, and enduring supernatural works
      retain vulnerable anchors.
- [ ] The world offers several incompatible Shaping philosophies without confirming an
      objective ontology in either player-facing or Gamemaster text.
- [ ] Pixie posts are primarily physical flying relays. Only trained individual Shapers use
      `Bend·Paths`, with its normal range, destination, Magnitude, PP, Practice, and Tell
      limits; there is no effortless instantaneous global service.
- [ ] Merfolk breathe air and can hold their breath underwater for 11 minutes. Their domains
      provide reefs, tidal or surface settlements, reliable air access, or air-filled halls
      rather than assuming unlimited underwater breathing.
- [ ] “Lizardfolk” is the setting's cultural term for characters and communities represented
      by the canonical Lizardman profile. Elves and dwarves remain supported player options,
      and no new racial rules are implied.

### Danger, recovery, and movement

- [ ] Major threats have warning signs, motives, exploitable circumstances, and credible
      negotiation, avoidance, or retreat options; named foes become harder through skills,
      equipment, and existing abilities rather than arbitrary crowds.
- [ ] Settlements and routes respect severe wounds: battlefield Healing stabilizes but does
      not restore HP, a Major Wound needs Surgery or equivalent treatment before natural
      recovery, and travel after injury has a real cost.
- [ ] Land distances agree with the canonical daily paces and terrain multipliers: 30 km for
      marching or slow riding, 50 km for hiking or ordinary riding, and 90 km for fast riding
      before terrain and conditions.
- [ ] Maritime routes identify the assumed vessel and derive fair-weather duration from its
      Speed at 20 miles per day per knot. Sailing tests cover hazards; any listed passenger
      fare uses the canonical 1 SP per kilometre unless a local exception is explicit.
- [ ] The detailed starting region provides practical access to outfitting, Healing and
      Surgery, underworld information, Shaping initiation, and a pixie relay without making
      those services universally safe or free.

### Content quality

- [ ] Every named people has more than one loyalty, social role, or internal disagreement,
      and intelligent nonhumans are not treated as default monsters.
- [ ] Player material reveals only common knowledge. Hidden objectives, metaphysical answers,
      site truths, and timeline outcomes appear only in the Gamemaster section.
- [ ] Factions have actionable relationships, internal fractures, current operations, and
      consequences if ignored; intrigue changes the situation rather than pausing it.
- [ ] Existing creature profiles support every prepared threat; the draft adds no unsupported
      mechanics disguised as setting lore.
- [ ] Maps, route tables, rumors, sites, opening adventures, and faction timelines agree with
      one another on names, distances, and current events.
- [ ] The name-removal test passes: institutions, religion, political assumptions, imagery,
      and expected adventures still distinguish one candidate from the other.

## Adding another candidate

1. Give the world one kebab-case Markdown file in this folder and label it **Candidate —
   noncanonical** at the top.
2. Use the same broad comparison points as the existing gazetteers: premise, player entry,
   regions and routes, peoples, factions, religion, Shaping philosophies, dangers, starting
   region, adventures, and a separate Gamemaster section.
3. Add its link and one comparison column or a second compact comparison table here. Keep
   world-specific facts in its own file; do not silently merge candidate histories.
4. Mark an unchosen draft **Archived — noncanonical** rather than deleting it. When a world is
   selected, record that decision separately before moving any of its material into canonical
   rules or publishing it on the website.
